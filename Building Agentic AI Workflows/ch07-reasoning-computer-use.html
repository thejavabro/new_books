Reasoning LLMs and Computer Use

Reasoning models think out loud before they answer, and that lets agents drive a real screen. Both are powerful and expensive, and neither is guaranteed to win: treat reasoning as a lever to test, not a default.

1

Seven pillars of intelligence

We tend to want AI that is smarter, not just cheaper, and will wait or pay for it. Across centuries of philosophy, roughly seven themes keep coming up when people describe intelligence. The book has already touched every one. Tap a pillar.

2

Context engineering: a name for what we've been doing

A term popular from mid-2025: context engineering, giving the model everything it needs for the task to be plausibly solvable. Nothing new, but a useful shared vocabulary. It has four parts, and every earlier chapter used them. Switch them on and watch the context the model actually sees.

3

What a reasoning model actually is

Aristotle called humans the rational animal and split reasoning into discursive (step by step) and intuitive (grasping something at once). Reasoning models (R1, o3, Claude 4 with thinking) are ordinary autoregressive LLMs trained to write a discursive chain of thought before the answer. No new architecture: the difference is training, usually reinforcement learning. Reasoning tokens are billed like output tokens.

Where those tokens go afterwards is a system choice. Frontier APIs return them as a separate block and drop them from later turns, which saves input tokens but also breaks the prompt cache (Chapter 1's o4-mini result). Many open models write them inline between <think> tags, so they persist and you have to parse them out. More persisted reasoning means more context, and more chances to snowball a mistake.

4

When reasoning helps: spell it out

Asked how many r's are in "Constantinople", a non-reasoning model blurted 1 and only fixed it when challenged. The reasoning model spelled the word out first and got it right in one go. Models see tokens, not letters, and spelling out is known to help with letter and syllable counting. Try any word.

A cherry-picked example, and the book says so. Some models let you switch reasoning on and off or choose an effort (low/medium/high), and some non-reasoning models (Kimi K2-Instruct) break problems down in markdown when it seems useful, which blurs the definition.

5

Does more thinking score more? Three tests

"Effort" isn't one thingOpenAI's o-series takes low/medium/high and can't switch reasoning off. Anthropic's effort sets a thinking budget as a share of max output: about 20%, 50% and 80%, and can be turned off. Gemini Flash Lite offers "minimal" or "high".
6

Where reasoning earns its keep: agents

ReAct literally means Reasoning + Action, yet the non-reasoning agents in earlier chapters mostly called tools without saying why. A reasoning model interleaves thought before each call. The same question to the same open model (Qwen3 32B), thinking off then on, reached the same answer, but with thinking on you can see why each tool was chosen. At best that improves consistency; at worst it's a transparency window.

7

Computer use: seeing the screen vs. reading it

Anthropic popularised "computer use" with Claude 3.5 Sonnet (October 2024): a model that takes screenshots, points to x, y and calls "click". Two approaches: truly multimodal (look at pixels, act on coordinates) and grounded textual (parse the page into a numbered list of elements and say "click element 31"). Some systems combine both.

8

Benchmarking clicks: IoU and centroid score

The test set: 228 screenshots of app interfaces, each with one task: 28 free-text, 32 coordinate, 63 yes/no and 105 number questions. Coordinates are the hard part: the answer depends on where things are, not just what's on screen, so perception and reasoning both matter. The prompt gives the screen size and asks for a box [(x1, y1), (x2, y2)]. Each coordinate answer scores the average of two numbers. Move the predicted box.

Opus 4.1: reasoning off vs. high

Opus 4.1 improved in every task category with reasoning, but coordinate tasks gained just 2.4% while latency rose 96.5%. Sonnet 4 actually dropped (82.7% → 81.4% average). Bar lengths are schematic around those reported deltas.
