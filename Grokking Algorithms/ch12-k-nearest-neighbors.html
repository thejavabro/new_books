k-Nearest Neighbors

To guess what something is, look at the things most like it. That simple idea classifies fruit, recommends films, predicts bread sales and opens the door to machine learning.

Is it an orange or a grapefruit?

Grapefruit tend to be bigger and redder than oranges. Plot known fruit by size and redness, then for a mystery fruit look at its k nearest neighbors: whichever kind is in the majority wins. That's the whole k-nearest neighbors (KNN) algorithm for classification. Tap or drag anywhere to move the mystery fruit.

2

Feature extraction and distance

To compare things you first turn each one into a list of numbers: its features. A fruit might be (size, redness). Similarity is then just distance, using the Pythagorean formula: √((x₁−x₂)² + (y₁−y₂)²). The same formula works for five numbers, or a million. Such a list of numbers is called a vector.

For film recommendations, ask each user to rate a few genres from 1 to 5. Now every user is a point in 5-dimensional space, and users with similar taste are close together. Rate the genres yourself and find your nearest neighbor:

This is why streaming services keep asking you to rate things: every rating makes your vector more accurate, so they can find people genuinely like you.

Regression: predicting a number

KNN can also predict a number instead of a label: take the k nearest neighbors and average their values. That's regression. Picture a bakery deciding how many loaves to bake from three features: weather (1 to 5), weekend or holiday (0/1), and whether there's a big game on (0/1).

When distance misleads: cosine similarity

Two people can have the same taste but use the scale differently: one gives 5 stars to everything they like, the other saves 5s for masterpieces. Distance says they're far apart. Cosine similarity compares the angle between the two vectors instead of the gap, so a stingier rater pointing in the same direction still counts as similar. (The book leaves the details for you to look up; here is the intuition.)

5

Picking good features

KNN is only as good as its features. Good features relate directly to what you predict and are unbiased (asking people to rate only comedies tells you nothing about whether they like action films). There's no single right answer: you have to think about what actually drives the outcome. For a film recommender, is each feature good or bad?

A first look at machine learning

KNN is your doorway into machine learning. Optical character recognition (OCR) is the same idea as the fruit: extract features from many images of digits (lines, points, curves), then classify a new image by its nearest neighbors.
