1

Two recipes, one architecture

A diffusion model can fail you in two ways. It will not draw your thing (your dog, product or mascot), or it will not draw in your look (your brand photography, your illustrator's hand). Both are fixed the same way: a small LoRA on the UNet's to_q, to_k, to_v and to_out.0, trained on a focused dataset with the same loop. The thing that differs is the shape of the dataset and how you caption it.

Production work often wants both: your product (subject) in your brand look (style). It is usually cleaner to train two adapters and combine them at inference than to force both into one.

2

DreamBooth: teaching a subject

DreamBooth (Google, 2022) rests on two ideas. First, a rare token: "my dog" already points at a strong general idea of a dog, so you bind your subject to something the model has barely seen, such as sks or ohwx. Second, prior preservation: you also train on generic images captioned "a photo of a dog", so every dog does not turn into yours. (Newer encoders on SDXL and Flux can often rely on descriptive captions instead of a rare token. The classic recipe teaches the fundamentals.)

3

Diffusion loss is noisy by nature

Each step draws a random timestep. Low t means a whisper of noise and an easy correction; high t means a hard one. In the book's run the subject loss went 0.0345 → 0.2096 → 0.5768 in three consecutive logs, and nothing was wrong. A 50-step moving average still wanders. The fix is a fixed validation set: 32 frozen (image, timestep, noise) tuples, scored every 100 steps for about 1% overhead. Since the inputs never change, only the model can move that number.

1,200 steps, 5 photos, 200 priors

A healthy val_loss drifts down slowly and flattens: here from 0.083 to 0.074, about 10%. If it bottoms out and climbs, use the checkpoint from the lowest point (they are saved every 300 steps). The final adapter is not always the best. Even val_loss measures noise prediction, not "does it look like my dog in a new pose", so check by eye as well.

5

Style LoRAs: a visual vocabulary

The counterintuitive part: to teach one style you need many different subjects in it. Train on 100 teacups in your brand style and cross-attention learns that the trigger means "move toward teacup". Ask for "a bicycle in mybrand style" and you get teacup-ish bicycles. A style trigger works like an adjective (sks dog is a noun). Aim for 50 to 100 images (under 30 overfits, over 200 buys nothing), resized for the model, with no watermarks or duplicates, and a held-out eval set.

Caption review

The book generates its style set with SDXL and trains SD 1.5 on it, which is real cross-model style transfer. Training on SD 1.5's own output would be circular. Copying a living artist's style without permission is not fair game. Training is the DreamBooth loop without the prior stream: encode, noise, predict, MSE, with gradient accumulation.

6

The trigger is not a switch, and the scale is a dial

Every LoRA layer computes y = W₀x + scale · (BA)x. Scale 0 is the base model, 1 is what you trained, and above 1 amplifies the update, with artifacts. Most good LoRAs look best between 0.6 and 0.9. Find the spot with a strength sweep: same prompt, same pinned seed, several scales tiled into one grid. Pick the highest scale that adds no artifacts. Sweep before you retrain.

The book's sweep used scales 0 to 1.7; 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9 looked best. Fusing with pipe.fuse_lora(lora_scale=0.8) bakes the chosen scale into the UNet. You then ship one artifact with no PEFT dependency, but the dial is gone.
