Knowing what to say vs. how to say it

After SFT the medical model knows the terminology and beats the base on perplexity and judged accuracy. Then a worried parent writes: my six-year-old has had a high fever for two days, what should I do? The SFT model, trained on clinician-style answers, may produce a correct differential diagnosis and a lab-test plan: right facts, wrong person. SFT teaches what to say; alignment teaches how to say it. Next-token loss cannot capture preferences about audience, tone, caution or brevity.

2

RLHF, and the shortcut called DPO

RLHF turned GPT-3 into ChatGPT in three stages: SFT on demonstrations; train a reward model on human rankings of response pairs; then optimize the policy against that reward with PPO, a trial-and-reward algorithm from robotics and games. It works, but means three models, two datasets, finicky RL, and reward hacking. DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) shows you can skip the reward model: the language model already assigns probabilities, so just push up the chosen answer relative to the rejected one. Same lesson, fewer steps: instead of hiring a food critic to score the chef, show the chef which of two dishes customers preferred.

3

Build a preference dataset

DPO data is a set of prompt, chosen, rejected triples. The gold standard is domain experts judging (and ideally writing) the answers. For learning, the book generates two answers per question with different system prompts (an empathetic plain-language physician vs. a thorough clinical expert), shuffles them, and asks a judge model which suits this asker best, ranked by safety, accuracy, tone, actionability, completeness. The rejected answers are not wrong, just pitched at the wrong level. You be the judge:

4
The DPO loss, step by step

  
Keep two copies: the frozen reference (your SFT model) and the model being trained. For each pair compute log-probabilities under both, then: chosen ratio = model − reference on the chosen answer; rejected ratio = the same on the rejected answer; margin = chosen ratio − rejected ratio; loss = −log σ(β × margin). It is ordinary classifier log loss for "did the model rank chosen above rejected?".

5
Why the reference model matters

  
Without the reference, the loss would just crank chosen probabilities up and rejected ones down until the model collapses onto a few memorized strings. Measuring everything relative to the reference limits drift: once the margin is large the sigmoid saturates and pushing further earns nothing. That is an implicit KL-divergence constraint (how far the trained distribution has moved), which RLHF must add and tune separately. β sets its strength: high keeps the model close and safe, low lets it move. 0.1 is the common default; 0.01 to 0.05 is increasingly popular to avoid over-optimization.

Running DPO with TRL

  
TRL's DPOTrainer looks a lot like SFT. The dataset needs prompt, chosen and rejected columns (answers without the prompt), converted to messages so Gemma's template applies. Start from the SFT model, not the base: the book merges the SFT adapter first, then trains a separate DPO LoRA on top, keeping the stages cleanly apart.
