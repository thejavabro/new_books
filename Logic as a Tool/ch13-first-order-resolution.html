Normal Forms, Unification and Resolution

To run resolution on first-order formulas, pull the quantifiers to the front, replace each ∃ with a witness function, drop the ∀, and let unification match literals.

1

Prenex normal form: quantifiers to the front

Resolution works on clauses, so first-order formulas need a normal form. Step one is prenex form: all quantifiers in a prefix, followed by a quantifier-free matrix. Rename bound variables apart, eliminate → and ↔, push negations in (switching quantifiers), then pull quantifiers out using laws such as ∀x A ∧ B ≡ ∀x(A ∧ B) when x is not free in B.

2

Skolemization: replace ∃ by witness functions

An existential that follows some universals says the witness depends on them. Skolemization names that dependence with a new function symbol: in ∀x∃y R(x, y), replace y by f(x). An existential with no universal before it becomes a new constant. The result is not equivalent, but it is equisatisfiable, which is all a refutation method needs. Tap the quantifiers to switch ∀/∃.

3

Clausal form

After Skolemization only universal quantifiers remain. Drop them (every variable in a clause is read as universally quantified), put the matrix in CNF, and split into clauses. Because each clause is implicitly quantified on its own, variables in different clauses are independent and can be renamed apart.

4

Unification: making two literals match

Propositional resolution needs complementary literals to be identical. In first-order logic, P(x, f(a)) and ¬P(b, y) become complementary after substituting b for x and f(a) for y. Such a substitution is a unifier; the most general unifier (mgu) commits to as little as possible. The algorithm scans for the first mismatch, binds a variable to the other term unless it occurs inside it (occurs check), and repeats. Variables are u … z; everything else is a constant or function symbol.

5

Resolution with unification

The first-order resolution rule: from clauses C ∪ {P(s̄)} and D ∪ {¬P(t̄)}, where σ is an mgu of P(s̄) and P(t̄), derive (C ∪ D)σ. Rename the clauses' variables apart first. A companion rule, factoring, merges literals in one clause that unify. Together they are refutation complete.

6

Strategies: keeping the search finite in practice

Unrestricted first-order resolution can generate clauses forever. Tautology and subsumption deletion still apply; on top of them, strategies limit which pairs may be resolved.
