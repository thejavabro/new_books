Two routes to a visual

Claude can build diagrams, charts, timelines and small interactive tools itself, as artifacts made of code (HTML, SVG, a chart library) in a panel beside the chat. What it does not do, unlike some assistants, is turn a description into a photo or illustration: Anthropic focused on reasoning, writing and coding. For a picture, Claude writes the brief and you use a separate image generator. Sort these jobs.

Write a strong brief for an image generator

Image generators respond to subject, setting, style, lighting and framing, and each tool prefers a different phrasing (plain sentences vs keyword lists and camera terms). Tell Claude which tool you will use, then refine in short turns ("evening light, drop the garden").

Licences before publishingCheck your employer's policy and the generator's licence before using an image commercially: many free plans restrict it, and training-data disputes were still in the courts at writing. Try the same prompt on two or three free trials before subscribing. Then bring the image back to Claude to check it against the brief and write alt text.
4

A deck leads to one decision

Chapter 2.7 treats a presentation as an argument: evidence, then reasoning, then one decision. State the audience, what they know, how long you have, and the decision ("approve the scheduling priorities", not "discuss next quarter"). Ask for a presentation brief, then a slide outline, before any slides. Put this seven-slide outline in a sensible order.

5

Titles that say the point

A useful slide title states its message. Choose the better title for each slide.

Topic or message?

Design and notes requirements worth asking for16:9, large text, one message per slide, at most five short bullets, consistent style, nothing that relies on colour alone, charts built from the data and labelled, no generated image standing in for numbers, unsupported points marked [REVIEW]. Speaker notes should add context (a missing value, how a measure was calculated, a likely question), not a script.
6

Inspect every slide before delivery

A deck can look finished and still be wrong. Read every slide in order against the source, not against Claude's summary. Four of these invented slides have a problem. Tap them.

The colleague testShow the deck to someone unfamiliar with it and ask what decision you are asking for. If they cannot say, fix the narrative. For people who will not attend, ask Claude to redesign the same content as a one-page report that works without speaker notes.
