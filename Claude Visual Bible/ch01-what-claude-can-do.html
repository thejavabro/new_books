What Claude Can Do, and Which Plan You Need

Claude is a strong drafting and thinking partner that can still be confidently wrong. Know where it shines, where it slips, and which plan unlocks what.

1

From chatbot to work platform

The chat box is still the centre of Claude, but on most plans you can also speak, show a photo, upload a spreadsheet, search the web or pull in information from another service. Longer-lived features (Research, Projects, Cowork, scheduled tasks) let one good conversation grow into something bigger. You set the goal, context and limits; Claude supplies speed and broad knowledge; you stay responsible for the decision. Step through how a conversation grows.

2

Tasks Claude handles well, and what you check

A good task for Claude is one where you can describe the result and check the reply: enough information, a clear audience, a useful format, and consequences you can live with if the answer is incomplete. Each kind of task has its own typical failure. Tap a task type to flip between what Claude does and what you check.

Pair AI help with a check you can performIf you lack the experience to judge an answer, ask a qualified person to review it. The value of the output is capped by the quality of the check.
3

When a person must stay in control

Claude can produce a polished answer that is wrong: it may misread a file, miss an option, or sound more certain than it should. Six kinds of situation call for tighter human control. Sort each scenario.

Light check, or a person decides?

Neat is not the same as rightTables, citations and a professional tone make a weak answer look dependable. Open the sources and check the facts underneath.
4

Three questions before you use any answer

Before you rely on a reply, ask: Is it accurate? Am I allowed to use it? What happens if it is wrong? The more it would cost to be wrong, the more review you add. Try the dial.

5

From a long answer to professional value

A long response creates no value on its own. Value shows up when work helps someone understand a problem, choose well, or finish a task with less waste, and you need evidence of that. The book follows a community arts centre trying to lift weekend workshop turnout. Each added feature moves the work one step closer to a measurable result.

Clients pay for results, not promptsTrust comes from understanding the problem, protecting information, checking the work and owning the deliverable. Start with a small, low-risk task you already understand and compare time and quality with your usual method.
6

Create an account and watch your usage

A free account gives you saved chats, settings and access across devices. Pick a sign-in method you will keep (email, or an existing account such as Google), check the address is really claude.ai, and keep personal and managed work accounts separate. Usage limits cap how much you can use in a session and in a week; heavy features like Research burn through allowance faster. Simulate a busy day.

Upgrade on evidence, not impulseStay on Free while you learn. When a limit or missing feature interrupts the same useful task more than once, that record is your reason to upgrade. Monthly billing lets you stop any time; annual is a little cheaper.
7

Which plan fits?

For individuals there are Free, Pro and Max; organisations use Team or Enterprise, which add managed accounts, admin controls and business data protections. A paid plan never makes answers correct, and never means unlimited use. Match each person to a plan.

8

Privacy is several separate switches

History, model improvement, memory, administration and retention are separate controls. Tick the ones you have reviewed; each explains what it does and does not do.

Tick a control to read what it changes.

No setting makes oversharing safePasswords, private keys, health records, confidential files and other people's data stay out of chats unless you have permission and an approved account. A managed work account is not private from the organisation that runs it. Also note: the interface language setting changes menus only; Claude replies in the language you write in.

10

Try it now: where can Claude help with one routine task?

Pick a recurring task that needs no private data and will not send, buy or delete anything (planning meals, prepping meeting notes, comparing routine purchases). Ask Claude to interview you with a few short questions, then lay out which parts it can help with and which stay yours. A prompt in the same spirit:

I want to get better at [my recurring task]. Ask me up to five short questions first. Then give me a table: steps Claude could help with, steps I should keep, what I should check, and the assumptions you made.
