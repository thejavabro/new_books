Search, Verify and Research with Sources

When the answer depends on today, Claude needs to look it up, and you need to check what it found. Quick facts get a search; big questions get a cited report.

1

When does Claude need to look it up?

Claude's built-in knowledge stops at a training cutoff. Anything that changes (prices, opening hours, news, availability, someone's current job) needs a live web search. With Web search switched on in the chat's + menu, Claude decides when to search; words like today, latest, current or price are strong cues. Not sure it searched? Ask it to, and to show sources.

Memory or search?

2

Three links can still be one source

Ask for several sources, including at least one official or primary one. Watch for sources that all repeat the same original article: three links, one voice. Toggle to see the difference.

3

Open the citation and check three things

A linked source is a starting point, not proof: Claude can summarise a page wrongly or attach the wrong page to a claim. For each citation check the date, whether it supports the specific claim, and whether any quote matches exactly. Inspect these invented citations for the claim "The Riverside Library is open until 8pm on Thursdays."

4

One search, or a research task?

A single search answers a single question well. Research (a paid-plan feature) plans its own steps, reads many sources over several minutes and returns a structured, cited report. It also eats usage allowance faster. Pick the right tool.

Search or Research?

5

How a research task runs, and where you steer

Research moves through its stages on its own. You hold the wheel at two points: the scope at the start (including reviewing the plan it proposes, which is far cheaper than fixing a finished report) and the review at the end.

6

Sharpen a research question

A vague question produces a vague plan. Name the topic, the period, the region or focus, source and date requirements, and the shape of the report: key findings, where sources disagree, what is still unclear.

Long is not the same as accurateA thorough-looking report can still misread a source or overstate a weak finding. Treat it as a strong first draft: open a few citations, check dates and claims. And note that publishing a report makes it public on the internet, possibly in search results, even though your chat stays private.

8

Try it now: check a current fact, then run one research task

Ask a current question and request three sources; click through and see whether one really supports the claim. Then pick a question that needs several sources, scope it, review the plan before it runs, and ask for findings, disagreements and gaps.
