Web, Phone, Desktop and Your First Useful Conversation

The same account follows you from browser to phone to desktop. Pick the surface that fits the moment, then have a conversation that ends in something you can use.

1

Pick the surface that fits the moment

One account, three places to use it. The web version (claude.ai) is the best start for most people: nothing to install, works on locked-down laptops and Chromebooks, plenty of room to read. The phone app shines away from a keyboard: voice conversations and photos of signs, whiteboards or objects. The desktop app (Mac, Windows) is for long sessions beside your other apps, and it is where the Chat/Cowork switch lives. Choose for each situation.

Where would you start?

Check the account, then the addressBefore you type or attach anything, confirm which account or workspace is active. Install apps only from the official store page (publisher: Anthropic) or claude.ai/download, never from an ad or file-sharing site. Grant microphone, camera or photo access only when you know why a feature needs it.
2

Start on one device, finish on another

Signed-in conversations follow you across devices, so you can ask in a browser, read on your phone and return to the computer for more space. Two exceptions: incognito chats are not kept, and a task using local files or installed apps may need the original computer. If a chat does not appear, check you used the same sign-in method: a different one can create a second account with a different history and plan.

One conversation, one subjectSwitching from holiday planning to a work memo? Start a new chat. Focused chats give Claude less unrelated text to misread and are easier to find later.
3

Summon the desktop app with a shortcut

The desktop app can pop up a small Quick Entry box from inside any other program. It is off by default; you turn it on in the app's general settings and record a key combination. If it does not respond, another program probably owns that combination. Build one and see if it collides.

4

Typing, dictating, talking or showing

How you send information changes; the conversation habits do not. Claude usually replies in the language you write or speak in, across many languages, though quality is best in widely spoken ones. Tap an input method.

Dictate is not VoiceThe microphone button turns speech into text you then send: still typing, just with your voice. The waveform button starts a spoken back-and-forth. Either way, read the transcript: background noise and cross-talk cause mistakes.
5

Choose a model and an effort level

A control in the chat box sets the model and how much it reasons before replying. Higher effort means slower answers and more of your allowance, and it still does not guarantee correctness; even over-thinking is possible. Match effort to the job.

6

The five-step improvement loop

A useful conversation has a goal you can describe and an answer you can improve. The book's loop: name the result, answer questions, correct assumptions, compare alternatives, review and keep. Notice the first message asks Claude to ask questions before suggesting anything, so it does not guess. Play the conversation.

Correct only what is wrongA correction does not need to restate the whole task. Change the part that was off, and if Claude keeps guessing, ask it to label each assumption and list what it needs from you.
7

Keep useful work without oversharing

Rename the chat so you can find it ("Balcony gift ideas, July"). Copy the finished result, with its sources and checks, into your own document: the chat is the working record, your document is the version you edit and approve. A shared link lets anyone with the link view the whole conversation and pass it on, so read everything before you share.
