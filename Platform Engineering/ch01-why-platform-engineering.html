Why Platform Engineering Is Becoming Essential

Every company eventually asks what to do with the code, tools and infrastructure many teams share. Central teams often disappoint; scrapping them exposes everyone to the cloud's complexity. Platforms are the third way.

Four words the whole book leans on

The authors set out a small vocabulary first. A platform here is not a wiki page and not "the cloud" on its own: it is something a team engineers, builds on top of cloud and open source pieces, and offers to other engineers as a product. Tap each term.

2

The over-general swamp

Most of what software costs comes after it ships: patching, retesting, upgrading dependencies and migrating. Cloud services and open source software give teams a huge menu of general-purpose building blocks (primitives) that are not integrated with one another. Each team writes its own glue (integration code, scripts, config) to wire them together. Glue holds things together, and it also makes them sticky: every change underneath now ripples through every team's glue.

Count the glue

More boxes, fewer linesPlatforms cut glue with two old ideas, abstraction and encapsulation: an opinionated layer over a limited set of choices, with an interface that hides the parts that will change. The ideas are old; what is new is how badly cloud and OSS growth made the problem.
3

How we got stuck: two changes

Change one: an explosion of choice. Frustrated with data-centre hardware, developers flocked to the public cloud. The hope was PaaS (the vendor runs everything, you push code). What actually won was IaaS (you get virtual machines and primitives, and you run them), because PaaS couldn't fit enough real applications. Kubernetes tried to simplify IaaS, but it is a leaky abstraction: less Terraform glue, more YAML glue. Meanwhile OSS offers a "free, like a puppy" option for every niche.

Who runs what?

Change two: higher operational needs. As software went 24/7 and releases sped up, the old split between developers and sysadmins broke down. Step through how operations work moved around.

Why existing teams struggle to build platforms

Four platform-adjacent kinds of team bring valuable skills, but their missions point them somewhere slightly different. It's not about the people; it's what their organizations ask of them. Platform engineering asks each to widen its balance. Tap to see the gap and the balance.

6

Do platforms support innovation?

Yes, within the known. A good platform lets teams ship experiments safely and fast with existing technology. But real technical innovation often needs tools the company doesn't have yet, and a platform will resist it. The skill is knowing when to let a team build its own shadow platform to prove an idea, then pull the winners in later. Decide each case.
