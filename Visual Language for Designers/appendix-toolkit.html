1

Glossary flashcards

The book's glossary is short and focused on the cognitive science of section one. Each card gives the term; tap for a plain-language definition in our own words, plus where it shows up in the principles.

2

Match the term to the idea

Tap a term, then the description it belongs to. Six pairs, drawn at random from the glossary.

3

Which principles should I reach for?

The six principles are meant as a space for exploration, not hard rules. Still, the job a graphic has to do points to some more than others. Tick what your graphic needs to do.

4

About the back matter and the source file

The book closes with reference lists that don't need a visual treatment, so they are summarized here rather than reproduced.

Note on the source PDF. No chapter content is missing: the introduction, Section One and all six principles (pages 13 to 344 of the PDF) have readable text, and the many full-page artwork images are intact. Three spots in the file carry no content: page 1 is a web-viewer "loading" placeholder in front of the title page; pages 6 to 12 are blank where the front matter (likely copyright, dedication and acknowledgments) would sit; and page 64 is blank just before the Section Two divider. None of these hold chapter text.
Companion notes for Visual Language for Designers: Principles for Creating Graphics That People Understand by Connie Malamed (Rockport, 2009). Explanations, examples and drawings are original summaries; none of the book's artwork is reproduced.
