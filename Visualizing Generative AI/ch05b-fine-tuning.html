Fine-Tuning a Model

When prompts grow long, latency matters or data must stay private, you teach the model instead of telling it. Three ways to do that, and what each one costs you.

Why fine-tuneAdaptersBuild the datasetSix stepsDistillationHuman feedbackQuick check
1

When prompts get too long, train instead

As an application matures, prompts grow: more instructions, more few-shot examples, more error handling. Every token is paid for on every call, and picking the right examples per question is its own engineering problem. A model fine-tuned on the task carries those lessons in its weights, so the prompt shrinks. Slide to see where the line crosses.

Illustrative: 300 tokens of instructions, 120 tokens per example, $0.15 per million input tokens, and a 1.5× price for calling a tuned model. Real prices vary; the shape of the trade-off is the point.

Adapter tuningAdd a small set of trainable weights beside a frozen model. Supervised (labeled pairs), parameter-efficient, usually LoRA, so you will see it called SFT, PEFT or LoRA.
DistillationTrain a smaller student on a bigger teacher's answers for a narrow task: faster, cheaper, and deployable behind a firewall.
Human feedbackLearn from preferences between two outputs when there is no single right answer. Called RLHF; usually done with DPO.
2

An adapter is a small side path

In LoRA, the big weight matrix stays frozen and the update is learned as the product of two thin matrices. At inference you need the base model plus the adapter, which providers host for you; the slight extra compute is usually outweighed by shorter prompts. (The matrix arithmetic below is general LoRA background, not from the book.)

Lock-in, unavoidableFor prompting, the book favors provider-agnostic frameworks. For fine-tuning it uses each provider's own API, because you deploy wherever you trained. Even Hugging Face ties you to the base model and its framework (though not to a cloud); open-weight models like Llama keep on-prem and edge options open.

Step 1: turn sonnets into training pairs

The book's adapter example teaches a model to write a Shakespeare-style line containing a word you give it, a stand-in for "write in our brand voice about anything". Each source line becomes a pair: input = one of its three rarest non-stopword words (so the model is not trained on "but" or "they"), output = the line. Pick a line.

5
Distilling step by step

  
A common path: launch on a big model, log real inputs, then swap in a small model to cut cost. The book's task reads a restaurant review and returns the cuisine, a likely star rating and a one-line summary. The trick from the 2023 "distilling step-by-step" work: have the teacher give its rationale as well as its answer, and train the student to produce both. That multi-task signal needs far less data.

6
Preferences, then DPO

  
Generation has many right answers, so instead of labels you collect preferences between two outputs. Three sources, ideally all of them: a dedicated rating phase before launch (raters may not resemble users), explicit thumbs up/down from real users, and implicit signals such as time on page or conversion, which tie the model to outcomes you care about.

The book's DPO run uses Hugging Face on Llama 3.1 8B: load the preference JSONL, split 80/20 so evaluation can stop training when it starts to overfit, load the model to tune and a frozen reference copy, then train and save:


pairs = load_dataset("json", data_files="prefs.jsonl")["train"].train_test_split(train_size=0.8)
policy = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(BASE)      # gets updated
anchor = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(BASE)      # stays frozen
DPOTrainer(policy, anchor, train_dataset=pairs["train"],
           eval_dataset=pairs["test"], tokenizer=tok).train()

  
Check with legalAdapter tuning leaves base weights untouched; distillation and DPO create new weights. Whether a provider's terms allow that, or allow its outputs to train a student, varies and changes. In August 2024 the book read OpenAI's terms as ruling out distillation, Google did not offer Gemini as a teacher, and Meta allowed distilling Llama 3.1.
