1

Transparency is the express lane

In most companies goals are still secret. Box's CEO Aaron Levie put the problem plainly: at any moment a big share of people are working on the wrong things, and the hard part is knowing which. Research says goals made public are more likely to be reached. In a survey of a thousand US workers, 92 percent said they would be more motivated if colleagues could see their progress.

In an OKR system even the newest hire can read the CEO's goals and challenge them. Tap each effect of turning the lights on.

92%more motivated if colleagues can see their progress
2×as likely to be top performers: companies with highly aligned employees
7%of employees fully understand their company's strategy and what's expected of them

Cascading goals: the Sand Hill Unicorns

Doerr's favorite teaching example is an imaginary football team. The general manager's key results become the objectives of the level below, and so on. Expand the tree, then hunt for the problems.

3

Four costs of cascading everything

Some cascading brings coherence. When every goal is cascaded, it turns into colour-by-numbers. Slide the number of management layers to watch the cycle slow down.

4

Bottoms up: let goals rise

Because OKRs are visible, they don't need to pass down every rung. An objective can jump from the CEO straight to a manager, or leaders can publish theirs and trust people to adapt. Google's former people chief Laszlo Bock described a market: everyone's OKRs are visible, so teams converge, and the badly misaligned ones stand out.

Innovation lives at the edges. Gmail began as a "20 percent time" side project called Caribou. The Unicorns' physical therapist might bring back an injury-prevention regimen from a conference: not aligned with her boss's OKRs, but very aligned with winning the Super Bowl. Find the right mix.

Doerr's experience: in urgent times leaders get more directive; when a company has grown cautious, a lighter touch. Over time the mix tends to settle around half and half.

Micromanagement is mismanagementDrucker: professionals need demanding standards, but how they do the work should be their call. Grove warned that meddling teaches people to bring every problem upstairs. The ideal frees people to set some of their own objectives and most of their key results. If you choose to run the marathon, you're far likelier to finish than if your doctor orders it.

Align: the MyFitnessPal story

Mike and Albert Lee built MyFitnessPal after a trainer handed Mike a paper list of three thousand foods to count calories for his wedding. At two people, alignment was easy. At thirty, entropy set in: give two great people big projects and they drift apart. Step through the lessons.

6

Connect: the Intuit story

Intuit's CIO, Atticus Tysen, piloted OKRs inside IT while the company moved to the cloud: first his direct reports, then directors, then all six hundred staff, one level per quarter. He wanted "enthusiastic compliance", not bureaucratic compliance.

~2,500active objectives in IT each quarter
~½aligned to higher-ups' or department goals
4,000+views of managers' OKRs per quarter, about seven per person

One surprise: Tysen found hundreds of key results linked to one of his own objectives. His message: your manager is still your manager, but disconnect from us and connect to each other. Toggle between the two network shapes.

Modernize, rationalize, and secure the technology used to run Intuit.Migrate Oracle eBusiness Suite to R12 this quarter.Deliver wholesale billing as a platform by end FY16.Onboard small-business agents to Salesforce.Create a retirement plan for all legacy tech.Agree new workforce technology strategy.
Same three verbs everywhereFrom Texas to Bangalore, people describe projects as rationalizing, modernizing or securing. If a project fits none, someone asks why they're doing it.
Adjust the altitudeWhen remote collaboration became urgent, a workforce-technology KR was promoted to a top-level objective (Slack, Google Docs, Box, BlueJeans in six months), with a candid note that it would drop back to a KR later.
