1

Goals need a medium

Culture eats strategy for breakfast. OKRs carry leaders' priorities; CFRs make sure they're heard. But like sound, goals need a medium to travel through, and that medium is culture: the living expression of what an organization values. Grove, ever the engineer, saw culture as efficiency. People who share values behave consistently without needing rules, so management spells values out and, more importantly, models them.

Google's Project Aristotle studied 180 teams. Standout teams said yes to five questions. Rate a team you know.

An accountable culture is self-governedYou push toward a goal not because the boss ordered it, but because it is visibly important to the company and to colleagues who count on you. Nobody wants to be the one holding the team back.
2

OKRs catalyze, CFRs nourish

In The Progress Principle, Teresa Amabile and Steven Kramer studied 12,000 diary entries from 238 people. Motivated cultures combine catalysts (actions that support the work, much like OKRs) and nourishers (interpersonal support, much like CFRs). Sort these.

Pulsing: a culture's heartbeat

Instead of once-a-year surveys, short "pulse" questions go out weekly or monthly. Feedback is a listening system; leaders shouldn't wait for a bad review online or a resignation.

3

Wiring OKRs to values: Coursera

A year after its founding, Coursera (guided by president Lila Ibrahim, an Intel alumna) did something rare: every team objective rolled up to strategic objectives, which rolled up to five core values and the mission, a world where anyone, anywhere can transform their life through the best learning. Tap a value.

4

Out-behave to outperform

Business philosopher Dov Seidman argues that behavior, how you do things, is the one thing competitors can't copy. "Self-governing" organizations replace rules with shared principles and carrots and sticks with purpose. A rulebook tells you what you can do; culture tells you what you should do. To measure trust he looks at behavior, not perceptions: does information flow freely, or get hoarded?

LRN's HOW report, 2016

17 countries, 16,000+ employees

Self-governing organizations rose from 3 to 8 percent of the total between 2012 and 2016. Doerr's takeaway: active transparency is the most powerful cultural force, and an OKR/CFR culture is above all a transparent one.

5

Culture first: the Lumeris story

Health-care technology firm Lumeris had "used" OKRs for three quarters with great participation, on paper. HR chief Andrew Cole found people editing metrics at quarter end to tick boxes, a lone HR person chasing numbers before board meetings, and objectives unconnected to real work. Leave the culture untouched, he warned, and the body rejects the transplanted organ. Step through the turnaround.

An exercise of our own, not the book's: several of these read more like tasks than measures. How would you add numbers and dates to them?

Selling your reds

try it

President Art Glasgow banned yellow: every top OKR is green or red. At the monthly review, executives skip their greens and "sell" their reds. The group votes on the most important at-risk OKRs, then colleagues volunteer to "buy" them. ("We're all in the same bathwater.") Vote, then buy. (Invented examples.)

6

Culture change: Bono's ONE Campaign

Bono's advocacy groups (DATA, then the ONE Campaign) ran like a punk band: pick one fight, attack it on a vicious schedule. Debt relief first, then universal access to AIDS drugs, which people called impossible. His method even then: picture Everest, describe how hard the climb is, then describe how you'll reach the summit.

21Mpeople on antiretroviral therapy by 2017
−45%AIDS-related deaths over ten years
~$50Bin health funding ONE helped deliver

Growth brought trouble: eleven merged groups, too many goals (agriculture, girls' education, energy, climate), unclear sign-offs and a cultural schism. OKRs, championed by board chair Tom Freston, forced agreement on what was achievable with the resources at hand, and the traffic-light colors transformed board meetings. Then came the pivot. Step through it.

Objective: Integrate a broad range of African perspectives into ONE's work, align with African priorities, and use ONE's political capital for specific policy changes in and toward Africa.1. Three Africa-based hires onboard by April; two African board members approved by July.2. African Advisory Board in place by July, convened twice by December.3. Relationships with 10–15 leading African thinkers who regularly challenge ONE's positions.4. Four participatory trips to Africa in 2010.

Measuring passion

shape only, not ONE's data

ONE had 8.9 million members by December 2017, over 3 million in Africa. Bill Gates' challenge: signers aren't members. So ONE built a metric for moving people up the ladder, rewarding anyone who took more than one action.

"If everything's at green, you failed."Bono's fear is that ONE goes corporate and chases every quarterly goal. Doerr kept urging "more red": bigger ambitions. The structure is what lets the passion work: a safe place where failing isn't a firing offense.
