Baking Better Every Day: The Zume Pizza Story

A tiny robotics start-up uses OKRs to align a mixed crew of chefs, engineers and drivers, and its CEO learns to lead through the process.

1

Robots, pizza and three giants

US pizza delivery, a $10 billion market, was ruled by three national chains. In spring 2016 Zume Pizza opened in a concrete bunker in Silicon Valley with a plan the skeptics mocked: let robots stretch dough, spread sauce and load an 800-degree oven, spend the labor savings on better ingredients, and finish baking in trucks on the way to your door. Within three months it had 10 percent of its local market. Cofounders Julia Collins and Alex Garden (who met OKRs at Zynga) tell this story. Step through the line.

2

Seven people, eight answers

With two founders, Zume lived in two heads. By seven people, "what's the most important thing today?" got eight different answers. Project tools helped (a waterfall planner for building the kitchen, sprint tracking for engineers), but neither could say what mattered most. Zume adopted OKRs three weeks after its first pizza shipped. Add people and see.

3

OKRs as a school for executives

Garden's point is the hidden value: OKRs teach managers to run a business within real limits. Push the envelope, but the envelope is real. At Xbox Live he had lived the opposite: visionary leaders, an overscoped mandate, and the "hows" left to the foot soldiers. Climb the ladder.

Two sad endings, one better pathStart-ups skip goal setting because they move fast. Then either the company outgrows its leaders and dies, or it succeeds, outgrows them, and replaces them. Better to teach people to think like executives while their department is a staff of one.

5

Calling the fly ball

When a fly ball drops between two outfielders, someone has to call it, or it falls in or they collide. Early on, both the marketing lead and the product lead (each a month into the job) were chasing Zume's revenue objective. Who should own what?

Pick an option.

6

"My KR is at risk"

Zume launched a food company, a logistics company, a robotics company and a manufacturing company in eight months, with a team from New York kitchens, film production and software. OKRs became their Esperanto. A new seasonal pie, for example, ripples everywhere: marketing needs a week's notice, then photography, the website and the app. Compare two ways the fleet manager could raise a delayed vehicle.

Without shared OKRs"What the hell, can you hurry up? I've been waiting forever!"Blame; defensive reply; nobody escalates for resources.
With shared OKRs"My key result for expanding our delivery radius is at risk."Everyone already agreed to the KR and the dependency. No judgment, just a problem to solve, and the two leads advocate together for more resources.

OKRs also give quiet people equal voice: every department's objectives get their turn on the screen. And the most enthusiastic users were the early skeptics; marketing director Joseph Suzuki expected "another diet program" and found his biweekly check-ins a moment to reset his compass.

7

Sacred one-on-ones (not about work)

Every two weeks each person at Zume has an hour with their manager. You can't be late and you can't cancel. The one rule: don't talk about work. The topic is the person, their two-to-three-year goals, and their two-week plan. Garden opens with three questions:

His side of the deal: always tell the truth, always do the right thing, and we'll back you every time. Paradoxically, these conversations reveal what moves people forward or holds them back, which is performance feedback by another route. After an all-out sprint, leaders can dial personal-development time up for a quarter.

8

Mission, culture and calm leaders

Zume's founding principles hang on a giant kitchen poster: serving food to people is a sacred trust, and every American has a right to delicious, affordable, healthy food. They block the small compromises (a bit more salt, a bit of sugar in the sauce instead of finding the freshest tomatoes). Everyone can call out anyone, especially the CEO, and leaders make a big deal of thanking people who do.

Objective: Delight customers.Feeding people is a sacred trust; customers should be so happy they order again and rave to friends.1. Net Promoter Score of 42 or better.2. Order rating of 4.6/5.0 or better.3. 75% of customers prefer Zume to a competitor in a blind taste test.
Know your objectives like your last nameCollins noticed great leaders share a cold, sober focus. Amid fund-raising, robots and kitchen build-outs, knowing the company's OKRs that well is calming; you can always default back to what matters.
