Resource 1: Google's OKR playbook

Google's internal guidelines, as excerpted in the book, boil down to a few rules. Objectives are the whats: aggressive yet realistic, tangible and unambiguous, obviously achieved or not, and clearly valuable. Key results are the hows: measurable milestones that describe outcomes, not activities, with evidence of completion (links, reports, published metrics). Committed OKRs are expected to score 1.0; aspirational ones average 0.7 with high variance.

Try the checker below. It applies the playbook's litmus tests to whatever you type (a heuristic, so use judgment).

Six classic OKR traps

Tap each trap, then test yourself.

Spot the trap

Reading a committed OKRRearrange other priorities to deliver 1.0 on time. If you can't promise it, escalate early: that is required, not shameful. A miss gets a blameless postmortem to improve planning.
Reading an aspirational OKRBy design they exceed capacity; work them in priority order after commitments. Carry them forward until done; dropping them for lack of progress hides real problems. Hand one to a better-placed team if it has the skills and bandwidth.
