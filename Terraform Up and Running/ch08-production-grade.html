Production-Grade Terraform Code

Production-grade infrastructure takes months, not days, because "it runs" is only the first of many requirements. This chapter gives the checklist and the module habits that make the work repeatable.

1

Why production-grade takes so long

"Production-grade" means infrastructure you'd bet the company on: it won't fall over under load, lose data in an outage or leak it in a breach. The book's estimates, drawn from years of building it at many companies, surprise most engineers.

Upper end of each range. Even these, the author says, are best-case numbers.

The chapter gives three reasons. DevOps is still in its Stone Age: the tools are young and few people know them deeply. The work involves a lot of accidental complexity, the dull tangle of compatibility problems, and plenty of essential complexity, because the list of real requirements is long. And it is especially prone to yak shaving: all the little side tasks you have to do before you can do the one you set out to do. Watch a one-character fix grow.

3

Small modules

Newcomers often put a whole environment, sometimes every environment, in one module. The book's claim: modules longer than a few hundred lines are a code smell, just like a 1,000-line function. Drag the slider to grow a module and watch what happens.

The fix is the Unix philosophy: do one thing and do it well. The book splits the web server cluster into three:

asg-rolling-deployan ASG with zero-downtime rolling deploys
alban Application Load Balancer
hello-world-appan app built from the two above
4

Composable modules

Small modules are only useful if they fit together. The trick is the one functional programmers use: minimize side effects and pass everything in through inputs and out through outputs. Instead of looking up the Default VPC inside the module, take subnet_ids as an input. Instead of hiding the target group, accept target_group_arns. Step through how hello-world-app wires them up.

5

Testable modules and built-in checks

Every module gets an examples/ folder: small root modules that show how to use it. They double as documentation, as manual test harnesses and, in Chapter 9, as the subject of automated tests. Pin versions in them too. Terraform also has three built-in ways to fail fast. Try a few inputs.
