Cracking the Application Architect Interview

Application architects turn requirements into the shape of the software: tiers, services, APIs, data flow and the trade-offs between them. The interview leans on design scenarios like a sudden 10x traffic spike.

1

What an application architect does

Application architects bridge business requirements and the structure of the software. They pick technology stacks and patterns, keep new apps compatible with existing systems, and stay with the team through delivery. The interview checks five areas of responsibility.

2

Anatomy of an application

Application architecture is the blueprint for an app's components and how they interact. The book uses a shopping website with five building blocks. Tap one to see what it covers and the questions an interviewer may hang on it.

3

Design principles versus methodologies

Principles tell you how to structure code: SOLID, DRY, KISS, YAGNI, clean code, design patterns. Methodologies tell you how the team works and delivers: TDD, BDD, Agile, DevOps, microservices as a delivery approach. Sort them: tap a chip, then tap a bucket.

SOLID, briefly

  • Single responsibility: one reason to change
  • Open/closed: extend without editing
  • Liskov: subtypes stand in for their base
  • Interface segregation: small focused interfaces
  • Dependency inversion: depend on abstractions

9

Scenario: from monolith to microservices

A big scenario in the chapter: a monolithic e-commerce app is slow to change and scale. The book's answer identifies bounded contexts, starts with components that change often or need independent scale, and uses the strangler fig pattern to reroute traffic piece by piece. Its illustrative results: release cycle from 8 to 5 weeks, page load 5 s to 3 s, critical outages from 12 to 3 a year.

Also in the chapterA video streaming platform growing from 10,000 to 1 million users (microservices, object storage, CDN, managed DB, event streaming, adaptive bitrate), holiday-peak e-commerce redesign, cross-device retail and productivity apps, cross-OS enterprise apps, choosing a stack for a social platform, and redesigning a learning platform's UI after early drop-off.
