1

The ML and generative AI architect

This architect finds business problems where ML or generative AI adds value, designs the models and data pipelines, takes them to production, and keeps them fair and reliable. The book lists seven capability areas.

2

Pick the learning approach

Supervised learning uses labeled examples (predict churn, price a house). Unsupervised finds structure without labels (customer segments, anomalies). Reinforcement learns by trial and reward (pricing policies, robotics). Choose a problem to see a reasonable algorithm family.

Neural networksCNNs for images, RNNs and LSTMs for sequences, transformers for language and more.
Transfer learningStart from a pre-trained model and fine-tune on your smaller dataset: less data, less compute, faster.
PreprocessingClean, impute missing values, handle outliers, scale, encode categories, split train/validation/test.
Feature engineeringCreate, select and transform features; reduce dimensions (e.g. PCA) when there are too many.
3

Evaluation metrics you must be able to explain

A fraud model scores 1,000 transactions. Move the decision threshold and the class balance and watch accuracy, precision, recall and F1 move. This is the intuition behind "why not just use accuracy?"

PrecisionOf those flagged, how many were right? Matters when false alarms are costly (spam filters).
RecallOf the real positives, how many did we catch? Matters when misses are costly (diagnosis, fraud).
F1Harmonic mean of the two, for a balance.
AUC-ROCRanking quality across all thresholds; 1.0 perfect, 0.5 random.
MSE / MAERegression error; MSE punishes big misses more.
4

From problem to production, and around again

The book's eight steps: define the problem, collect and prepare data, choose a model, train, evaluate, tune, deploy and monitor, iterate. MLOps makes that loop repeatable: versioned data and models, automated pipelines, validation for accuracy and bias, deployment, monitoring, and collaboration between data scientists, ML engineers and operations. Watch a deployed model drift.

5

Generative AI and foundation models

Generative models create new content rather than labels. GANs pit a generator against a discriminator; VAEs learn a compressed latent space to sample from; transformers power today's large language models. Foundation models are pre-trained on huge, broad datasets and adapted by prompting, retrieval or fine-tuning. The book lists many providers' models; what matters in an interview is knowing how to choose and adapt one, and the challenges: cost, hallucination, bias, privacy and evaluation.

6

Whiteboard: a RAG assistant

The book's generative AI whiteboard is an employee assistant that answers from company documents. Foundation models don't know your travel policy, so retrieval-augmented generation finds the relevant passages and adds them to the prompt. Type a question and watch retrieval pick documents by vector similarity (a toy 2-D embedding).
