Solutions Architect Role and Responsibilities

Ask ten people what a solutions architect does and you get five answers. This chapter pins the role down, maps the generalist, specialist and industry variants, and shows how to turn a job description into a resume that gets read.

1

Who is a solutions architect?

A solutions architect (SA) designs technology-based answers to business problems and stays with them after launch. They gather requirements from business leaders, project managers and developers, choose technologies, write the technical plan, guide the build and then watch the result in production for scalability, availability and maintainability. "Solutions" (plural) and "Solution" architect are used interchangeably; the plural hints at integrated, end-to-end designs rather than one point fix.

The book's running example: a company's website is slow. Step through how an SA attacks it.

The pattern behind the exampleAnalyze before proposing. Tie each recommendation to a measured bottleneck. Implement with the teams who will own it. Then measure again. Interviewers listen for that loop in every design answer.

Read the job description like a spec

Treat the job description as the requirements document for your application. Read all of it, highlight technologies, certifications and duties, note questions to ask, and line up an example from your own work for each requirement. Minimum requirements sit under basic qualifications; the nice-to-haves sit under preferred. Tap the phrases in this sample posting (written for this page) that you would highlight.

We are hiring a Solutions Architect to design cloud-based solutions for enterprise customers and to migrate and scale existing workloads. You will lead technology discussions with directors, VPs and CXOs, articulating the business value. We look for technical breadth with depth in one or two areas.
Basic: 5+ years in software development or architecture; experience with AWS, Azure or Google Cloud; API development and integration; strong communication.
Preferred: Hadoop, Spark or Kafka; DevOps practices; pre-sales experience; a scripting language.
Blue = must prove · orange = bonus points

Each highlight becomes a line in your prep sheet: "they want X, my evidence is Y".

5

Resume bullets: action plus impact

Tailor the resume to the posting, keep it scannable with bullets, and make every bullet show what you did and what changed, with a number. The book's sample resume for "John Doe" does exactly this: cost cut by half, deployment time down 40%, adoption up 30%. Build one of your own below and compare it with a duty-style bullet.

Responsible for cloud architecture and working with development teams.

Numbers here are made up for practice. Use your own, and be ready to explain how you measured them.

Cover letterPersonalize it to the company: who you are, why this role, one or two achievements with numbers that match their needs, and how you would help their customers. One page, enthusiastic, specific.
6

Polish your LinkedIn profile

Recruiters search LinkedIn first. The book cites survey figures that most recruiters vet candidates there and that complete profiles get far more opportunities. Tick what your profile already has.
