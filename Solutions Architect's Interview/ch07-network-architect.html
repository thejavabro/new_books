Cracking the Network Solutions Architect Interview

Networks are the nervous system of a distributed platform. Network SAs design for scale, reliability, security, performance and cost, and are often asked to walk through what happens when a link or region fails.

1

The network SA and what keeps them up at night

Network solutions architects design, implement and manage networks that are reliable, secure, adaptable and scalable, from branch LANs to global platforms and hybrid cloud links. The book lists the challenges they face; interviewers often ask which one you've wrestled with.

2

What happens when someone opens a website

A classic opener: walk through a request to a globally distributed social site. The book's nine steps, animated. Architecture is the layout of routers, switches and firewalls; protocols (TCP/IP, DNS, HTTP) are the rules they speak.

3

Anycast: one address, many places

Global platforms combine data centers in several regions, segmentation and load balancing inside each, CDNs at the edge, and anycast: the same IP prefix announced from many sites, so routing delivers each user to the nearest healthy one. Take a site down and see where users go.

4

Eight network design principles

Scalable, adaptable, reliable, secure, high-performance, operable, cost-optimized, and quality-of-service aware. Plus documentation, which the book calls the most critical habit of a network architect.

5

Resilient hybrid connectivity

The book's whiteboard: two customer data centers, each with a dedicated private link to the cloud (Direct Connect on AWS, ExpressRoute on Azure, Dedicated Interconnect on GCP) running BGP, plus a site-to-site VPN over the internet as backup, and a link between the data centers. 6

BGP: steer traffic, stay safe

BGP is how the hybrid design picks paths. Architects use attributes to engineer traffic: Local Preference picks the exit on your side (higher wins), AS-path prepending makes a path look longer so the other side avoids it. Set them and see which link carries traffic.

BGP securityRoute leaks and hijacks have caused real outages (the book cites incidents involving a Turkish ISP in 2004, a Pakistani ISP in 2008 and a 2018 DNS hijack). Defend with peer authentication (MD5), RPKI with signed Route Origin Authorizations, and prefix filters in both directions.
