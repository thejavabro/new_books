1

Why industry solutions architects exist

Cloud services take care of much of the hard technology work, so technology choices are no longer made only by the CIO. Business-line heads now buy technology too: a finance head picks a fraud-detection provider, and a retail head funds product recommendations. An industry SA speaks both languages. They know the domain's workflows and regulations, and they turn them into architecture.

2

The four verticals in the chapter

Pick a vertical to see the workflows, use cases, regulations and core systems an interviewer will expect you to know. Each vertical has sub-roles: healthcare differs from life science, and supply chain differs from retail.

The book notes that telco, energy and utilities, media and entertainment, and travel and hospitality also hire industry SAs, but it doesn't cover them.

4

Whiteboard: payment fraud prevention for a bank

The book's BFSI whiteboard is about a bank (the authors' fictional BitMaple) that wants fraud protection across online banking, merchant payments and cards. The steps are: analyse current systems and fraud patterns, bring in stakeholders, add security controls (real-time monitoring, encryption, MFA), use ML for anomaly detection, and keep the system patched and compliant with PCI DSS. The AWS reference architecture combines a data lake, streaming, ETL, ML scoring and a graph database for fraud rings. Below is my simplified version of that architecture.

Batch path (gray): systems of record → migration and file transfer → S3 data lake → Glue ETL and catalog → model training. Real-time path (blue): transaction → Kinesis stream → scoring Lambda (ML model + graph lookup in Neptune) → decision → customer alert.

5

A retail STAR answer, taken apart

The book's worked example question is: "Tell me about a time you built a solution that solved the end customer's problem. How did you find the challenges? How did you make it scale?" Its sample answer is about cart abandonment on an e-commerce site. Step through it to see how a STAR answer is built.

Follow-up 1"More detail on the technical changes?" Guest checkout, one-click buy, code and DB tuning, CDN, ML recommendations, microservices with auto scaling.
Follow-up 2"What did you learn?" Put the user first: do more user research and usability testing earlier, and keep monitoring after launch.
