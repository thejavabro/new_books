Starting in a Solutions Architect Role in a New Organization

Getting the offer is the start. The first months decide how much influence you will have: learn the business, map the current architecture, draft a blueprint and earn trust before you push big changes.

Why the first steps matter

You got the job. Now the organization expects you to connect technical detail to business outcomes. What you do in the first weeks shapes your reputation, your relationships across teams, and which projects you get. The chapter gives four steps. This page turns them into tools you can use in your first 90 days.

2

Step 1: understand your new organization

Before you design anything, learn four things: the goals, vision and culture; the key stakeholders and what they expect; the current technology; and how digitally mature the organization is. Maturity tells you whether to change things gradually or boldly. The book's example is an online-learning company. Click each stakeholder to hear what they care about.

Where is this organization on digital maturity?

3

Step 2: evaluate the current architecture

Review the technology stack, data flow and integration, performance and scalability, security, and operations. The book's example is a retailer whose e-commerce site slows down at peak times. Its customer data sits in silos, and deployments are manual. Score each area for your own organization to get a first roadmap.
