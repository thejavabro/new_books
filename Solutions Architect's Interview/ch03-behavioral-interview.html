Cracking the Behavioral Interview and Soft Skills

Most SA interviews spend as much time on how you work as on what you know. Behavioral questions probe past behavior as a predictor of future behavior, and a structured story beats a clever one.

1

The soft skills that make an SA

Technical skill gets you considered; soft skills decide whether you are trusted with big work. The chapter names eight: leading through ambiguity, communication, executing strategic projects, driving organizational efficiency, being a trusted advisor, professionalism and leadership, thought leadership, and pre-sales (including RFPs, RFIs and RFQs). Open each to see what an interviewer wants to hear.

2

Say it for the person in front of you

The book's favorite example: the head of loan processing at a lender cares how much each application saves, not which queue technology you used. Being concise and outcome-first with senior people is itself a skill interviewers test. Pick an audience and see which message lands.

Message A

We replaced the nightly batch with event-driven processing on a managed queue and serverless functions, and added an OCR step for document intake.

Message B

Loan decisions now take 2 days instead of 9. That cuts processing cost by about $40 per application and lets us approve more loans with the same team.

3

Technical, behavioral and situational

Behavioral questions ask what you did ("Tell me about a time…"), because past behavior is the best evidence of future behavior and of culture fit. Situational questions ask what you would do in a hypothetical. Technical questions test what you know. They are prepared differently.

4

Build a personal interview guide

Being surprised by "tell me about a time…" is how good candidates ramble. The fix is a personal interview guide: a living document with a role-specific introduction and six or seven strong stories. One story can answer many questions. Map your stories to the ten behaviors the book suggests covering. Your ticks stay in this browser only.

Preparation checklist from the chapterDecode the job description · reflect on relevant projects · frame each with STAR plus how you scaled it · know the SBI variant · predict likely questions · research the company's tech stack · prepare questions for them · rehearse out loud without memorizing.
5

STAR or SBI: two ways to tell a story

Both keep an answer tight. STAR separates the task from the action; SBI (Situation, Behavior, Impact) folds them together and puts the spotlight on your behavior. The book answers "a complex architectural decision without clear precedents" with SBI. Switch frameworks to see the same story re-cut.

Then they digExpect four families of follow-up on every story: What was the outcome? What were the key challenges and constraints? How did you define and measure success? What would you do differently? Write those answers into your guide too.
6

Practice the drill-down

Interviewers keep digging until they are satisfied. Pick a question, then press "Interviewer digs" to walk the conversation. Each turn shows the kind of detail that keeps the story credible.

8

Situational questions: ask before you design

"Design a retail website." Jumping straight to a three-tier microservice diagram signals that you didn't think about the customer. Ask about three key things, state assumptions for the rest, then design. Pick the three questions you would ask.
