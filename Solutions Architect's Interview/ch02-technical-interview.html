Cracking the Technical Interview

The technical loop tests breadth first and depth where it counts. You need a handful of design principles, a working model of the cloud, a whiteboarding routine, and crisp answers across application, network, storage, security and database topics.

The technical skills map

The book lists eleven technical areas an SA should be comfortable in: systems design, application development, programming, infrastructure, networking, storage, DevOps, security, databases, middleware and cloud. You are not expected to master all of them. You are expected to know where your depth is and to speak sensibly about the rest. Rate yourself and see your shape.

Enterprise architecture and its frameworks

Enterprise architecture (EA) is how an organization plans its systems as a whole. Interviewers check whether you can connect a single solution to that bigger picture through four domains: business (strategy, processes), data (models, flows, governance), application (systems and their interfaces) and technology (infrastructure and platforms). Two frameworks come up most.

Zachman is a classification grid: rows are perspectives, columns are the six interrogatives. Tap a row to see what that audience cares about.

How much EA do you need?For hands-on, technology-heavy roles, EA frameworks are a supplement. For roles close to business strategy and long-term planning, they become central. Read the job description and weight your preparation accordingly.
4

Cloud concepts you will be asked about

Know the big three providers by their strengths (AWS breadth and global footprint, Azure integration with Microsoft software and hybrid, GCP analytics, Kubernetes and open source), and know one of them deeply: depth in one cloud convinces interviewers you will learn the others fast. Then be fluent in service models.

Who manages what?

pick a model

PublicShared provider resources; agility, scale, pay as you go.
PrivateDedicated environment for strict control or compliance (healthcare, finance).
HybridPlace each workload where security, performance and cost are best; connect them with high-speed links.

Emerging technology matters too. Show you track things like data mesh, data fabric, generative AI, vector databases, Matter (smart-home standard), robotics and blockchain through analyst reports, conferences, meetups, communities and your own writing. You don't need to be an expert, but informed curiosity sets you apart.

5

Whiteboarding a data lake

Interviewers watch five things at the whiteboard: problem solving (do you ask clarifying questions?), technical knowledge, communication, design quality against functional and non-functional requirements, and industry awareness. The book's routine: reserve the top-left corner for goals and the customer questions you will answer, keep a "parking lot" for out-of-scope topics, and tick questions off as you cover them. Step through a data lake board.

Presenting itKnow your audience and objective, tell it as a story (setup, design, takeaways), use visuals, know the material without slides, invite questions, admit what you don't know and follow up, keep to time, and practice until it is boring.
6

Structure answers with STAR

Even technical questions like "describe the most complex architecture you designed" want a story. Prepare six or seven of them in a personal interview guide, each with a number at the end. Tap the letters to see how a global e-commerce redesign story is built.

Expect follow-upsAfter a big story, interviewers drill in: exactly how did you handle the scale? How did you integrate third-party systems with different formats? What security protected payments and customer data? Have a second layer of detail ready for each.
