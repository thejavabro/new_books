Gelling Your Engineering Leadership Team

Your direct reports should be a team that works on Engineering as a whole, not a set of individuals defending their patches. That takes explicit expectations, shared work and trust.

1

Your peers are your first team

Patrick Lencioni's The Five Dysfunctions of a Team argues your "first team" is your peers, not your reports. It's harder than it sounds: your incentives usually align with your own team but often clash with your peers'. Marketing wants work your team thinks won't matter; People wants fewer top ratings than you think your team earned. For your own leadership team, the goal is the same: a group that puts Engineering as a whole first, not a set of leaders defending their patches.

2

Debugging the team you inherit

By the time you arrive, the last executive is often long gone and the leadership team has run without guidance for a while, so you'll likely see people at their worst. Within a couple of months you need answers to three questions. Form a first opinion, then try to disprove it: you were hired to find the truth, not adopt the most popular story. Then act: move out those who won't be part of the team first. Try it on this inherited team.

Consider senior engineers as direct reportsAn Engineering executive blends the technical and managerial views of Engineering. That's easiest when both are on your leadership team. If you already have too many reports, meet frequently with a group of senior engineers instead, such as an architecture group.
3

Operating the leadership team

The initial edits are quick; gelling the team is ongoing, every week, until your last day. Four themes. Turn each on and watch how the team handles a conflict: one leader needs product features shipped, another needs a migration to retire an unreliable component.

4

Set explicit expectations

First-time members of a leadership team often think the team exists to help them do their job, rather than that they lead their org on behalf of the business. Tell them what you expect, or they'll default to their natural style. Tap each area.

5

When collaboration decays into competition

A team that worked well can, a year later, have slid into internal competition: members believe they'll be rewarded more for capturing capacity from each other than for creating it. Diagnose the cause.
