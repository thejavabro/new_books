Using Cultural Survey Data

Cultural surveys are one of the best views into how your organization experiences you. Read them carefully, commit to two or three real changes, and report progress until the next survey.

1

A neglected lens on your organization

Most scaled companies run a periodic cultural survey (bought from vendors like CultureAmp or Lattice, occasionally built in-house) and generate reports per manager and for the company. Senior leaders often give them little time, yet they're one of the best views into how the organization actually experiences you. Quick version: spend a couple of hours, weigh absolute scores over relative ones (a high score that dipped is still high), and find all the issues instead of fixating on the first two.

2

Practice reading a report

An invented Engineering report. Tag each row as something to celebrate, address, or acknowledge, and notice which changes are even statistically meaningful given the group size. Survey tools are incentivized to produce narratives, even weak ones.

3

Larson's ten-step review

As an interviewAt Stripe, Larson built a Director-plus interview around reviewing survey results. It revealed data literacy (not leaning on insignificant differences without saying so), building a mental model on the fly, asking good diagnostic questions, and above all whether a leader felt wholly responsible for their organization or saw themselves as implementing company policy.
4

Take action: the middle path

Two failure modes: analyze and do nothing (people learn to ignore surveys), or promise to fix everything (they love you now, and not in six months). Pick one to three areas you can genuinely move, and do the work.

Executives are rarely held accountable unless they expose themselves to it. This process does exactly that, and makes it easier to hold your own team accountable later.

5

Should you change the questions?

Executive teams love to debate new questions before each run. Usually it's bikeshedding. Ratings get their value from comparisons over time and against industry benchmarks, so changing them wipes history; free-form questions can change freely but rarely matter, because people with something to say will say it anywhere.

6

When to start, how often to run

Sit with uncomfortable scoresLarson has been called out for ignoring the team's priorities and seen remote experience regress despite his efforts. Take a moment to be uncomfortable, then use it. Few data sources do more to make you a better leader.
