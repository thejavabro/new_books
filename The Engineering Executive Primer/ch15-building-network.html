Building Your Network

Executives have few peers inside the company, so they need a network outside it: to learn, to hire, to get advice, and to keep perspective.

1

Why executives need an outside network

Larson has usually learned more from peers than from managers, because peers see problems from nearby. As an executive you still have peers, but the head of Product answers from a Product point of view. Valuable, but different. For Engineering-executive questions, you need a network outside the company. His routine: try to solve a problem himself, then with his team and peers, and only then reach out. Uses he's found:

Benchmarking the size of platform teams elsewhere
Pricing data before renewing expensive vendor contracts
Advice on exec-team situations you can't discuss internally
Calibrating offers when candidates and comp data disagree
Deciding whether to leave, and reading the exec job market
Fast backchannel on a senior hire with mixed feedback

In his experience, no topic is off limits; people are glad to share almost anything.

2

There's no cheat code

Building the network is hard, especially for a newer executive who is ahead of most of their peers: you may know hundreds of people but only a few who've held the role. Larson started broad and grew more intentional, focusing for a period on infrastructure leaders facing similar scaling problems, then on people at vendors he depended on. 3

Write a cold outreach note

The quickest builder: short notes to people near your network, say a friend of a friend. People like to help when it's easy. Toggle the rules and watch the note change.

What doesn't work

Any single request can get dropped; people are busy. But if you're consistently ignored, something's wrong, and it's worth getting someone to review how you engage. Spot the problem.

Other networks worth building

6

A small, steady habit

Your network is a set of relationships, and relationships need intentional care. Set a small goal, such as one new person a month, focused on whatever you're working on now. Not your top priority; never forgotten.
