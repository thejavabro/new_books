Creating Useful Organizational Values

Values don't create culture; they lock in culture you have already changed. Useful ones are reversible, applicable and honest, and they only stay alive if they show up in hiring, reviews and daily decisions.

1

What values are for

Uber's "Let Builders Build" was well meant, yet a few engineers used it to call any objection to their proposals a cultural failure. Values are hard to use well. Stated values make clear how you want people to make decisions. If senior people live them, they become a living force; if not, a dusty relic.

The key claim of the chapter: values don't start a culture change, they lock in one that's already happening. Saying you value punctuality doesn't make you punctual; turning up on time does. Which of these situations could values help with?

Good job for values?

2

Should Engineering have its own values?

Function-specific values are controversial, and executive teams may take them badly. First try extending a company value with an Engineering interpretation. Larson's example takes Amazon's Frugality and adds: compare the full cost of building and maintaining a service against a vendor; the vendor is usually more frugal. That reinforces the company value and is an easy sell. If nothing fits, choose a path.

3

Reversible, applicable, honest

Larson's test for a useful value has three parts. Reversible: its opposite is also a sensible way to run a company, so it actually guides a choice (irreversible values get lip-service). Applicable: it shows up in planning, reviews and hiring. Honest: it describes how effective people really behave today; too aspirational and it traps your best-intentioned people while cynics follow the real role models. Judge these well-known values, then compare with Larson's view.

4

Values and strategy overlap

A useful value can usually serve as a guiding policy in a strategy, but not every guiding policy is a value. "Reuse common technologies unless you see a 10x improvement" works as both. How you respond to a specific market opportunity is strategy only: it is tied to the moment. Values are presented as timeless; strategies are tied to circumstances.

5

When and how to roll values out

New executives feel a pull to publish values fast, like announcing a new architecture in week three. Don't. Wait at least six months, or at least until you can tell honest values from aspirational ones. If you feel you must rush, ask whether you're retreating into comfortable work. Then treat it like any process rollout: identify the opportunity, document options, involve stakeholders early, test before finalizing so people don't feel trapped, and iterate.

Values are a garden, not a building: without daily tending they're forgotten. Plant them in four places and watch the garden grow.

Company values pull in peer executives and the CEO, who are hard to hold to any process. That chaos is one reason Larson likes Engineering leadership values instead.

6

Values Larson has found useful

Successful companies export their values, so some look universal. Almost every company now claims Amazon's customer obsession; few practice it. Reversibility travels, but applicability and honesty don't. So don't copy these; ask whether each would honestly fit your org.
