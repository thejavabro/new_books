Measuring Engineering Organizations

There is no single number for "engineering productivity". Pick measures by purpose: to plan, to run the org, to improve, and to show the company what Engineering delivers.

1

Measurement without the dogma

In Larson's learning circle for Engineering executives, the second most common topic (after friction with the CEO) is what to report when the CEO asks for Engineering metrics. Opinions are strong: story points are essential or criminal; DORA-style metrics are all that matters; nobody reads the charts anyway. Each has a grain of truth, but the frustration is overstated. You measure to build software better, hold teams accountable, collaborate, and stay aligned with your CEO.

The secret of good measurement is measuring somethingThe number one risk is measuring nothing because you're trying to measure everything. Measure in many small iterations, and don't be afraid of something quick and easy today.

Measuring for yourself: four buckets

Measuring for stakeholders

Others want numbers too, often to confirm their own intuition about your trendline. Each audience needs something different. Pick one.

Measuring for stakeholders can feel like waste, but it turns messy recurring arguments into simple, predictable contracts about specific outcomes.

4

Sequencing what to measure

Your list will always be longer than your capacity. A rote "measure these first" list doesn't work; too much depends on context. Instead, three rules. Run a candidate metric through them.

Work through one measure from each category (CEO, yourself, Finance, peers) to get a baseline everywhere, then come back for second and third passes where the first attempt isn't working.

Building confidence in the data

Freshly rolled-out measures often disappoint: people read them differently, or they clash with what teams feel. Data has value only when you push on it: review it weekly against a goal; hold a hypothesis for why it moves and dig in when it's violated; push on it with others, not alone; segment it; and compare it with what people experience.
