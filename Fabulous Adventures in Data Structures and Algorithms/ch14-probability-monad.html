Interlude: The Probability Monad

The third interlude: discrete distributions fit the monad pattern: Unit is a singleton, Bind builds a joint and marginalizes, and the empty distribution makes it additive. Then an honest look at what the design cannot yet do.

1

Distributions are a monad too

Chapter 11 said a monad is a generic type that adds a capability, has a Unit that wraps a value, and has a Bind that attaches a new function to an existing workflow. Check IDiscreteDistribution<T> against that list.

✓A new capability. It produces as many random T values as you like, following a PMF.
✓Unit. The Singleton distribution: always this value. That is the double-headed coin from chapter 12.
✓Bind, with type IDiscreteDistribution<A> × (A → IDiscreteDistribution<B>) → IDiscreteDistribution<B>. It did not exist yet, but it is just joint, then project to B: treat the distribution as a prior, treat the function as a likelihood, build the joint, and keep only the B. That last step is called marginalizing.

So any workflow on ordinary values can be turned into the same workflow on random values.

2

Bind: from a prior on colds to a distribution of sneezes

Bind the sneeze likelihood to a cold prior and you get the distribution of sneezes, read off the right-hand margin of the familiar table. With a 10% prior that is 113 : 887.

3

Join: a distribution of distributions

Flip a fair coin. On tails roll one die, on heads another. Sampling gives you a die, and you then sample the die: an IDiscreteDistribution<IDiscreteDistribution<int>>. Join, which is Bind with the identity function, flattens it into a single unfair die. With a d6 and a d10, faces 1–6 get weight 8 and faces 7–10 get weight 3.

4

An additive monad: pour urns together

Adding two distributions over the same type means pouring both urns into a bigger one: combine the supports and add the weights. MZero is the empty distribution, where everything is impossible. Pouring in an empty urn changes nothing, and binding anything to it gives it back. To support it, the categorical constructor returns Empty when every weight is zero, and the joint construction leaves zero totals out of its LCM. Then Where can be written in the standard additive-monad way. It is only a slower, less readable Filter, but it proves the point.

5

What we built, and what it can't do

GoodDistributions as values, with statistical vocabulary. Likelihoods, joints and posteriors make Bayesian reasoning explicit. Any finite distribution builds in O(n) and samples in O(1) via alias. And it is a monad, so workflows lift.
Not so goodInteger weights made joints awkward (the next chapter uses unnormalized doubles). A joint of an n-support prior with m-support likelihoods costs O(nm) and can grow huge. No infinite discrete distributions, such as Poisson. So far it covers only a small corner of statistics.

Next comes sampling from continuous distributions and from random sequences. These turn out to be closely linked, and together they let you approximate continuous posteriors automatically.
