Case study: a site that sells videos

Something like the author's own tutorial-video site. Individuals pay one price to stream and a higher one to download and own. Businesses buy streaming licenses in batches with quantity discounts, and often the buyer isn't the viewer. Authors upload videos, descriptions and extras (exams, solutions, source code). Admins create series, add and remove videos, and set prices.

Step one is use-case analysis. By the SRP, each actor is a primary source of change; partition the system so that a change for one actor can't affect the others. Tap an actor.

Dashed: the abstract use case "View Catalog", which "View Catalog as Viewer" and "View Catalog as Purchaser" both specialize. Not strictly needed, but the two were similar enough to unify early. Login/logout and many more are left out to keep the example small.

2

A component architecture

Each actor-facing use case gets its own slice: view, presenter, interactor, controller, with a shared data gateway layer. Each component is a potential jar or DLL, and the abstract View Catalog use case becomes abstract classes inherited by its two concrete variants. Control flows right to left (controller → interactor → presenter → view), but most source arrows point left to right, toward the higher-level policy. Two dimensions of separation: by actor (SRP) and by level (Dependency Rule). Now decide how to deploy it.

Keep the options openBecause the components are separated, grouping them into deployables is a late, reversible decision. Start with two or five jars; split further as the system or team grows.

4

The newcomer's shortcut

In a layered design, a new team member needs another orders page. They find OrdersController and the OrdersRepository interface, and inject the repository straight into the controller, skipping the business logic. Arrows still point downward, the graph is still acyclic: it's a "relaxed layered architecture". But business rules (authorization, auditing) may now be bypassed. Can the codebase stop them?

Pick an enforcement approach, then commit.

Other decoupling modes. Module systems (OSGi, Java 9 modules) separate public from published types. Or split source trees: domain, web and persistence, with the latter two depending on the first. A simpler two-tree split (domain "inside", infrastructure "outside") risks the Périphérique anti-pattern, named after the Paris ring road: infrastructure code calling other infrastructure code directly, never passing through the domain.

The devil is in the implementation detailsMap your design onto code structures deliberately. Decide which decoupling modes to use at compile time and runtime. Be pragmatic about team size, skill and budget. Use the compiler to enforce your style where you can, and watch for coupling in other places, such as shared data models.
