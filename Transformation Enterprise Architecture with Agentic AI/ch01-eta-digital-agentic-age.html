Enterprise Technology Architecture in the Digital and Agentic AI Age

Enterprise architecture used to be a blueprint you reviewed at project gates. With agentic AI it has to become a living capability that guides change while it happens.

1

One bank, three eras of architecture

The chapter's running example is a bank. The same customer problem, a disputed card transaction, travels through the organization very differently depending on how mature its architecture is. Pick an era and watch the case move.

The real shiftIn the first two eras architecture is mostly a review function: it checks designs at project gates. In the agentic era it becomes an operating capability that helps the bank design, guide and control intelligent work while it is happening.
2

Where conventional ETA strains

Conventional enterprise technology architecture (ETA) is top-down: a central team or board sets standards and reviews designs. That gave discipline and alignment, but the operating model was built for slow change. Tap each pressure point.

3

Principles: from drivers to a short list

Principles are the small set of statements that steer every later decision. Following TOGAF's thinking, they should be derived from the enterprise's drivers, not invented in a workshop. Click a driver for a banking-flavoured example, then step through how a principle set is built.

A good principle has four partsName (short and memorable), statement (what we do), rationale (why it serves the strategy) and implications (what it costs and changes). If you cannot write the implications, you do not have a principle yet, just a slogan.
4

The four core domains, before and after agents

Application, data, infrastructure and security architecture are the backbone of ETA. Each one stretches when agents arrive. Flip the switch to compare.

5

Frameworks: TOGAF, Zachman, Gartner and others

Frameworks answer three plain questions: where are we today, where must we go, and how do we get there in a controlled, measurable way? They map to current-state architecture, future-state architecture, and roadmap plus governance. The big three take very different shapes.

6

Why the leadership team should care

Frameworks are not just for review boards. For executives they control spend, align ambition with readiness, absorb disruption, speed up decisions and reduce slow-burning risk. The first lever is easiest to see: uncoordinated AI pilots quietly multiply cost.

Illustrative numbers only. The point is the shape: without a managed portfolio each unit buys its own model access, vector store and guardrails.

7

Into the recursive digital era

The digital era made architecture modular, API-driven and cloud-based, but the blueprint was still static and human-defined. In what the author calls the recursive era, AI feeds back into the architecture itself: systems sense, learn and adjust within governed limits. Compare five shifts.

The architect's new roleLess a builder of static blueprints, more a "digital ecologist": cultivating a living ecosystem that is resilient, resource-efficient and people-centred. The job moves from gatekeeper of standards to strategic influencer.
