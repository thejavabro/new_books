Bridging Enterprise Technology Architecture and Agentic AI

Generative AI does not just add another tool to the landscape. It changes what each architecture layer must do, and it asks whether the organization is ready.

1

The incumbent's dilemma

Established enterprises are caught between the urgency to adopt generative and agentic AI and decades of legacy: fragmented applications, entrenched processes and technical and procedural debt. The architecture office that should lead is often itself built for the old pace. The author's point: readiness starts with the architects. Slide the debt and see where an AI initiative ends up.

2

Principles of AI-enabled architecture

AI-enabled ETA treats the architecture as dynamic, intelligent and adaptive. The chapter's principles, one per card.

3

Enterprise technology architecture for an intelligent agent

The chapter introduces a layered view of what an agentic enterprise needs, the seed of the full reference architecture in Chapter 5. Instead of static integrations it treats the enterprise as a living system where autonomous and generative components collaborate. Click a layer.

4

AI across the architecture lifecycle

Generative and agentic AI are now part of the core, so each phase of the architecture lifecycle picks up new questions. This does not mean dropping discipline: it means architecture must govern systems that behave probabilistically.

5

Automation, augmentation, autonomy

The progression is deeper than "more automation". Each stage changes who decides. Drag through the stages with the same task: handling a supplier invoice.

6

How generative AI learns the enterprise topology

Feed an LLM-based system the scattered evidence (architecture documents, configuration databases, code repositories, API specs, logs and observability data) and it can weave a semantic model of how the enterprise actually fits together: a living digital twin of the technology estate. Add sources and watch the map fill in.

7

Energy efficiency is an architecture decision

The goal is not less intelligence but intelligence delivered responsibly. Agent loops multiply the cost of every choice, because one task can mean many model calls. Try the levers the chapter singles out.

Relative units, not measurements. Edge vs. central inference is a further lever: central pools resources and is easier to govern; edge cuts latency and data movement.

8

AI readiness: the gap behind failed pilots

Many AI investments fail to deliver because of a readiness gap: hype, vague use cases, poor data, missing skills and weak governance. Readiness is multidimensional and the pillars reinforce one another; the weakest one sets the pace. Rate yours.

9

A five-phase AI risk assessment

Responsible AI by design means every agent works inside organizational, legal and security boundaries. The chapter's framework is iterative: context definition, risk identification, risk analysis, mitigation, continuous monitoring. Score a few risks for a claims-triage agent on likelihood, impact and detectability (hard to detect makes it worse).
