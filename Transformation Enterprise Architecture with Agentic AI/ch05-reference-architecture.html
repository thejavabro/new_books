1

Where conventional architecture starts becoming legacy

Today's stacks know their system of record, but the system of knowledge, the truth needed to finish real work, is scattered across documents, inboxes, wikis and people's heads. And integration assumes predictable work. Every new exception adds another branch to the workflow until automation becomes, in the chapter's image, a fragile sculpture. Add exceptions and watch.

2

Logical architecture and viewpoints

A logical architecture lets you reason about the system before choosing products. TOGAF calls it a technology-agnostic view; other frameworks use other names. Agentic systems are multidimensional (cognition, semantic pipelines, model lifecycles, runtime, governance), so one diagram cannot serve everyone. Map each stakeholder to a viewpoint.

3

Modern digital architecture, without and with agents

For a decade digital systems followed a comfortable template: stable APIs, deterministic microservices, CRUD, caches keyed by IDs. Agents change how the stack behaves at runtime. Compare one capability at a time.

4

From proofs of concept to a platform

A handful of impressive PoCs does not transform a business. The chapter argues for a platform approach: accept that many PoCs are demonstrations, then accelerate delivery through reusable components, automated MLOps and secure data integration, and build on four pillars: agents, knowledge, models and governance. Slide the number of use cases.
