Team Interaction Modes

Teams should not just "talk more". There are three well-defined ways for two teams to work together, each with its own cost and benefit, and picking one on purpose removes a lot of friction.

1

Interactions need designing too

Arranging teams into four types is not enough; you also have to decide how they interact, and when that should change. Without that, a team told it's "autonomous" finds it has to negotiate with many others, and a team told to provide an API lacks the experience to do it well. The basic choice between any two teams: collaborate, or treat one as providing a service to the other. Avoid everyone needing to talk to everyone; like a jazz band, curate the communication.

Intermittent beats constant

Bernstein, Shore and Lazer found that groups which interacted only intermittently reached solutions about as good on average as groups that interacted constantly, but kept enough variety to find some of the best solutions too. Here, eight searchers hunt for the highest peak on a rugged landscape. When they "talk", everyone jumps near the current best.

2

The three interaction modes

Every relationship between two teams should use one of three modes. One team may use different modes with different teams, and most medium and large organizations need all three. Pick a mode.

5

A behaviour style for each mode

Like a concert band switching between jazz, swing and film score, the same team adopts a different style depending on who it is working with. Predictable behaviour builds trust and avoids "invisible electric fences". James Urquhart calls well-defined interactions a "communication backchannel" that avoids much of the politics and inefficiency of human-to-human communication.

Promise theory: a version number is a promise

Mark Burgess's promise theory frames team relationships as promises rather than commands or contracts. Semantic versioning is a concrete one: the team promises not to break your code without saying so. What did the provider change?

6

Making a reverse Conway maneuver stick

Reorganize teams to match the architecture you want and, by Conway's law, the existing software will push back. So pair the new structure with temporary, explicit collaboration across the new boundaries and enabling teams in facilitating mode. Problems with the new boundaries then show up early, before too much is built. Ownership may even start back to front.

Architects as designers of team APIsA dedicated architecture team is usually an anti-pattern, but a small group of architects is very effective when its job is to discover, adjust and reshape interactions between teams. They ask: which mode suits these two teams, and what communication do these two parts of the system need? That spares individual developers the stressful job of constant boundary spanning.
IBM around 2014To spread DevOps and Agile across 40,000 developers on six continents, IBM used a small "advocates" team (a few full-timers plus part-timers) that collected successful patterns, trained teams and executives, and ran webinars for thousands. It saw itself as temporary: the goal of a "DevOps team" should be to put itself out of business.
7

Use modes to reduce uncertainty

Awkwardness is a sensor. When observed communication doesn't match the chosen mode, something in the design is off. Set up a situation and read the signal.

Don Reinertsen: be alert for the white space between roles, the gaps nobody feels responsible for. Event storming and context mapping from domain-driven design help find the right boundaries faster.
