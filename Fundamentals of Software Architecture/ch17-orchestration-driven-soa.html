Orchestration-Driven Service-Oriented Architecture

The enterprise-reuse style of the early 2000s: a strict service taxonomy wired together by an orchestration engine. Mostly a cautionary tale about how reuse turns into coupling.

A strict taxonomy of services

Orchestration-driven SOA appeared in the late 1990s when companies were merging and software was expensive. The driving philosophy was enterprise-level reuse. Click each layer.

Business services

Coarse, abstract entry points with no code, just names: ExecuteTrade, PlaceOrder. Defined by business users.

Enterprise services

Fine-grained shared implementations: CreateCustomer, CalculateQuote. The building blocks meant to be reused across the company.

Application services

One-off, single-application services not worth reusing.

Infrastructure services

Operational concerns: monitoring, logging, authentication.

Orchestration engine

The enterprise service bus: stitches business services to enterprise services, handles transactions and message transformation. The heart, and the bottleneck.

Reuse... and coupling

One shared Customer service for every division sounds efficient. Watch what happens when one division needs a change.
