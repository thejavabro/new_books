Architecture touches everything

New in the second edition: architecture doesn't live in isolation. The book walks through each intersection. Click one.

Implementation

Architecture only matters if the code follows it. Watch operational concerns (does the code actually deliver the characteristics?), structural integrity (does it keep the intended structure?) and architectural constraints (are the decisions followed?). Fitness functions bridge the gap.

Infrastructure

Architecture and operations used to be separate. Cloud and DevOps fused them: elastic scale (born after the dot-com era, the book uses Pets.com as the cautionary tale) is now an architectural decision, not just an ops one.

Data topologies

The database topology (monolithic, domain, per-service), the data's own characteristics, its structure (relational, document, graph, key-value) and its read/write priority all must align with the architecture.

Engineering practices

Continuous delivery, testing practices and automation determine whether an architecture can deliver deployability and agility. Process is not separate from structure.

Team topologies

Conway's Law in action: stream-aligned, platform, enabling and complicated-subsystem teams fit some styles better than others.

Systems integration

Systems talk to other systems. Integration styles (sync APIs, messaging, shared data) carry their own coupling and must match each side's characteristics.

The enterprise

Enterprise standards, shared platforms and governance limit choices. A team-level decision may conflict with enterprise strategy.

Business environment

Mergers, regulations, budgets and market timing change what's possible. Architecture must align with business strategy.

Generative AI

Generative AI can help with code, documentation and analysis, and is itself a component with non-deterministic behaviour to architect around. It can't make context-rich trade-off decisions for you.
