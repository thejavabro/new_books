The Scope of Architectural Characteristics

Not every part of a system needs the same characteristics. The architecture quantum gives you a way to scope them, and that scope decides monolith versus distributed.

1

Architecture quantum

An architecture quantum is an independently deployable artifact with high functional cohesion, high static coupling and synchronous dynamic coupling. In practice: the smallest piece that can have its own set of architectural characteristics. Compare three systems.

One deployable unit and one database. Everything shares one set of characteristics: if Payments needs high security, so does Catalog.

Each service has its own deployment and data. Each can scale, deploy and be secured differently. Three quanta.

Deployed separately, but they all depend on one database. If the database goes down, all go down. Static coupling to the shared DB makes this one quantum.

2

Static and dynamic coupling

Static coupling is how things are wired: shared libraries, databases, frameworks, operational dependencies. Dynamic coupling is how quanta call each other at runtime. Synchronous calls temporarily glue quanta together. Watch.

While Bid Capture waits for Bid Tracker, the two share operational characteristics: availability, performance and scale of the slower one. For the duration of the call, they behave as one quantum.

A queue decouples them: Bid Capture fires and forgets. Each keeps its own characteristics. This is why communication style affects quantum boundaries.

3

Kata: Going Green

Going Green buys used phones through kiosks and a website, assesses them and resells or recycles them. Analysing its characteristics reveals three clusters with different needs, which strongly suggests three quanta. Click a cluster.

Customers hit kiosks and the website at unpredictable times: needs to scale and stay up.

New phone models come out constantly. The faster the assessment rules update, the more valuable phones Going Green can buy. This business driver is why agility is its own cluster.

Money and audit trails: security and data integrity dominate. It can be slower; it must be correct.

One set of characteristics for the whole system would over-engineer some parts and under-serve others. Distinct clusters point to a distributed architecture.
