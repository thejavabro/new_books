A year on: what does the future hold?

A year since the big move. The team is talented, you've made friends, and most days you do a good job. But your career has decades left. Five years from now, coffee in hand, where will you be? Will you be happy? The future is like the view from a moving train: luck, opportunity, willpower and acceptance. This final chapter is an exercise. Grab a pen (or use the tools below) and travel through time.

2

Life, the universe and everything in between

Research on places where people live longest finds, besides activity, plant-based diets and close community, a common thread: purpose. In Okinawa it's called ikigai, roughly "what you live for": a compelling goal that pulls you forward. Tech workers have lots of choice, which can paralyse and breed fear of missing out. A structured vision narrows it down.

Careers are fluid. Average tenure at US tech companies was around four years (2018). Lifetime employment is gone, so think in tours of duty: help people (and yourself) reach their next step while they're here. Keep two things in mind:

How did you get here? Your last ten years

Plot the significant events of the last ten years: career and life (education, moves, people). Most people underestimate how much happens in a decade. Then reflect on what part training, luck, people, forethought and hindsight played. Your timeline stays in this browser only.

4

The next ten years: write your vision

Two common problems: people don't dream big enough, and their vision isn't clear enough to pull them forward. Some prompts: What do you enjoy most at work, and what would you change? On which mornings do you leap out of bed, and why? Do you want to manage people, and which people? Do you want more qualifications? Use "I will" statements, then let it sit.

Get input: send it to a partner, friends, or your manager. Is it realistic, overambitious, under-ambitious? Find five people who already live that future and study how they got there. Dream big, but enjoy the journey rather than obsessing over the destination.

5

Your plan for this year

Reread your vision. What must be true in five years? Three? Two? One? Think skills, opportunities, training, networking. Then write a one-year plan statement with a clear success test, e.g. "a positive review and supportive peer feedback."

Rate your competencies

Use your career framework (Chapter 15) and ask peers to rate you too. Big gaps between self and peer views are worth a candid conversation.

Your skills backlog

Turn plan actions into tangible, measurable items mapped to competencies. Keep them somewhere you'll revisit (a Trello board works). Review with your manager quarterly. Tap a card to move it along.
