What we left out, and what to say about it

Let's design a car rental system where customers can rent vehicles.

A car rental system is software that handles renting vehicles for a short time. A rental usually lasts from a few hours to a few weeks.

A rental company often has many local branches. That lets a customer return a vehicle to a different location. Most branches sit near airports or busy city areas.

System Requirements

We will focus on the following requirements while designing our Car Rental System:

  1. The system will support renting different vehicles like cars, trucks, SUVs, vans, and motorcycles.

  2. Each vehicle is added with a unique barcode and other details, including a parking stall number that helps locate the vehicle.

  3. The system should be able to answer which member took a particular vehicle, and which vehicles a specific member has rented.

  4. The system should collect a late fee for vehicles returned after the due date.

  5. Members should be able to search the vehicle inventory and reserve any available vehicle.

  6. The system should send a notification when a reservation is near its pick-up date. It should also send one when a vehicle is near its due date, or has not been returned by then.

  7. The system will be able to read barcodes from vehicles.

  8. Members should be able to cancel their reservations.

  9. The system should keep a vehicle log to track all events related to a vehicle.

  10. Members can add rental insurance to their reservation.

  11. Members can rent additional equipment, like navigation, a child seat, or a ski rack.

  12. Members can add additional services to their reservation, like roadside assistance, an additional driver, or wifi.

Use case diagram

We have four main Actors in our system:

  • Receptionist: Adds and modifies vehicles and workers. Receptionists can also reserve vehicles.
  • Member: Searches the catalog, and reserves, picks up, and returns a vehicle.
  • System: Sends notifications about overdue vehicles, canceled reservations, and so on.
  • Worker: Takes care of a returned vehicle and updates the vehicle log.

Here are the top use cases of the Car Rental System:

  • Add/Remove/Edit vehicle: To add, remove, or modify a vehicle.
  • Search catalog: To search for vehicles by type and availability.
  • Register new account/Cancel membership: To add a new member or cancel an existing membership.
  • Reserve vehicle: To reserve a vehicle.
  • Check-out vehicle: To rent a vehicle.
  • Return a vehicle: To return a vehicle that was checked out to a member.
  • Add equipment: To add equipment to a reservation, like navigation or a child seat.
  • Update car log: To add or update a car log entry, like refueling, cleaning, or damage.

Stage 1: money and messages

A rental is charged for more than the car. There is the daily rate, then insurance, then a child seat, then a late fee. If each of those knew how to charge itself, the total would be in four places at once.

So one class holds the lines and adds them up. Everything that costs money produces a BillItem. Bill is the only thing that knows what a rental costs.

Bill, BillItem, Payment and Notification: the money and the messages:

The diagram shows that rule. Bill owns its items, which is composition, drawn as the filled diamond: an item cannot exist without its bill. Payment and Notification are abstract, meaning a base class that is never created on its own. Each has one subclass per way of paying or delivering. Stage 2: the things added to a rental

Insurance, equipment and a prepaid service are three different things to a customer. To the bill they are the same thing: something with a description and a price that has to end up on a line.

That is why they share a base class. The billing is written once. Adding a fourth kind of extra later means one new subclass and no change to the reservation.

The shared base class is ReservationAddOn. Insurance, equipment and a service each extend it. AdditionalDriver sits outside that tree, and the diagram shows why: it names a person and costs nothing.

Stage 3: the vehicles

This stage contains the most important decision in the design.

A status field says what a vehicle is doing right now: available, reserved, loaned. That cannot answer the question a member actually asks: "is this car free for three days starting the tenth". A car that is loaned today may be free then. A car that is available today may already be promised.

So each vehicle keeps the set of days it is booked for. The status stays, because a receptionist still wants to know where the car is. Availability is answered from the days.

Requirement 9 needs a log of every event on a vehicle. Searching that log belongs to whoever holds the list, which is the vehicle. A single log entry cannot search anything.

Vehicle, VehicleLog and the vehicle types: Here are the classes related to vehicles:

The most important decision in this stage is not an arrow. It is the set of booked days on each vehicle. The five subclasses exist because the class diagram names them. The only thing each adds is what kind of vehicle it is. Vehicle owns its log entries, the filled diamond, so the history goes when the car does.

Requirement 5 is a search by type and by model. Requirement 7 is a lookup by barcode when a receptionist scans one. Three questions, so three indexes. All three are filled by the same method, so they cannot drift apart.

The search a member runs is not "all cars". It is "cars free for these days", which is the last method here.

Search and VehicleInventory: the indexes and the searches over them:

VehicleInventory implements Search, which is the dashed line with the hollow head. Its arrow to Vehicle is a plain association: the inventory refers to the cars but does not own them. An index that is thrown away does not take the cars with it.

Stage 5: the reservation

The reservation is where the rules live. It creates its own bill with the base charge already on it. It refuses add ons once it is no longer confirmed. It works out the late fee from the due date rather than from the whole rental. And it gives the days back to the vehicle when it ends.

That last part matters twice: on return and on cancellation. A canceled rental has to free the car it was holding.

Requirement 3 asks two questions of the same data: what a member rented, and who took a vehicle. Both are answered from the one list of reservations.

VehicleReservation and ReservationService: Here are the classes related to reservations:

Every arrow out of the reservation is a plain association except one. It owns its bill, because a bill has no meaning without the rental it is for. The vehicle, the add ons, the additional drivers and the notifications are all things it refers to and does not own. ReservationService sits beside it with no arrow at all. Creating a reservation is not the same as keeping one.

The finished class diagram

Now that every class exists, here is the whole model in one picture. It is the same picture the seven stages have been filling in, with every class in full color at last. Nothing on it appears here for the first time.

Here are the main classes of our Car Rental System:

  • CarRentalSystem: The main part of the organization this software is designed for.

  • CarRentalLocation: The system has multiple locations. Each location has a 'Name' to tell it apart from other locations, and an 'Address'.

  • Vehicle: The basic building block of the system. Every vehicle has a barcode, license plate number, passenger capacity, model, make, mileage, and so on. Vehicles come in multiple types, like car, truck, and SUV.

  • Account: Mainly two types of account: a general member and a receptionist. A worker who takes care of returned vehicles can be another account.

  • VehicleReservation: Manages reservations for a vehicle.

  • Notification: Sends notifications to members.

  • VehicleLog: Keeps track of all events related to a vehicle.

  • RentalInsurance: Stores details about the rental insurances that members can add to their reservation.

  • Equipment: Stores details about the types of equipment that members can add to their reservation.

  • Service: Stores details about the types of service that members can add to their reservation, like additional drivers and roadside assistance.

  • Bill: Contains a bill item for every charge on the reservation.

The patterns in this design

Template Method. A template method is a base class method that fixes the steps and leaves one step for each subclass to fill in. ReservationAddOn writes the billing once, in add_bill_item, and leaves one hole for the subclass: get_description. Insurance, equipment and a service are three different things to a customer and the same thing to the bill. This is how a design says that in code.

Singleton. A singleton is a class that allows only one instance. The rental system object is one, with the usual trade-off. One global object is easy to reach from anywhere and hard to replace in a test.

Nothing else here is a named pattern. Vehicle with five subclasses and Payment with two are abstract base classes.

What we left out, and what to say about it

Some things are absent, and that was a decision. One way rentals, where the car ends up at a different branch, are out. So is pricing that changes by season or by length of rental. Deposits and holds on a card, mileage limits, and damage claims are out too. So is fuel level on pick up against fuel level on return.

Two places where a reviewer will question the design, and the honest answer to each:

  • Two members can reserve the same car at the same moment. Checking the days and then booking them is two steps. Nothing stops a second request between them. A real system does that check and that write as one operation, with nothing allowed to run between the two steps.
  • A rental can be dropped at a branch that never gets the car back. The reservation records a return location. But returning the vehicle puts it back in the inventory it came from. Moving a vehicle between locations is a transfer the design does not model.

💡 In the interview: the sentence worth saying early is that availability is a question about dates, not a field on the vehicle. Most candidates choose a status enum, and the interviewer asks "so can I book it for next month". Getting to the booked days first turns requirements 5, 8 and the whole cancellation path into one line each.
