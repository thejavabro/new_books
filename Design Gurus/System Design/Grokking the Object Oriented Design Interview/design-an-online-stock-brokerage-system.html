Let's design an Online Stock Brokerage System.

An Online Stock Brokerage System lets its users trade stocks online, that is, buy and sell them. Clients can keep track of their transactions and execute them. The system shows performance charts for the stocks in their portfolios. It also secures their transactions and alerts them when a stock changes by a pre-defined amount. No middleman is needed.

The online stock brokerage system automates traditional stock trading using computers and the internet. That makes each transaction faster and cheaper. It also gives quicker access to stock reports, current market trends, and real-time stock prices.

System Requirements

We will focus on the following requirements while designing the online stock brokerage system:

  1. Any user of our system should be able to buy and sell stocks.

  2. Any user can have multiple watchlists, each containing multiple stock quotes.

  3. Users should be able to place stock trade orders of the following types: 1) market, 2) limit, 3) stop loss and, 4) stop limit.

  4. Users can have multiple 'lots' of a stock. If a user has bought a stock several times, the system should tell the different lots of that stock apart.

  5. The system should be able to generate reports for quarterly updates and yearly tax statements.

  6. Users should be able to deposit and withdraw money by check, wire, or electronic bank transfer.

  7. The system should be able to send a notification whenever a trade order is executed.

Usecase diagram

We have three main actors in our system:

  • Admin: Handles administrative tasks like blocking or unblocking members.
  • Member: Can search the stock inventory and buy and sell stocks. Members can have multiple watchlists, each containing multiple stock quotes.
  • System: Sends notifications for stock orders and periodically fetches stock quotes from the stock exchange.

Here are the top use cases of the Stock Brokerage System:

  • Register new account/Cancel membership: Add a new member or cancel the membership of an existing member.
  • Add/Remove/Edit watchlist: Add, remove or modify a watchlist.
  • Search stock inventory: Search for stocks by their symbols.
  • Place order: Place a buy or sell order on the stock exchange.
  • Cancel order: Cancel an order that was already placed.
  • Deposit/Withdraw money: Members can deposit or withdraw money by check, wire or electronic bank transfer.

Stage 1: money in, money out, and messages

Requirement 6 gives three ways to move money: check, wire and electronic bank transfer. They differ in what the movement is called and how long it takes to clear. The rule that no single transfer may exceed the limit is the same for all three. So it is written once in the base class.

Requirement 7 needs a notification when an order is executed. That is the same shape: one base class, two ways to deliver.

Notification and MoneyTransfer: the messages and the money:

Requirements 6 and 7 produce every class here, and none of them points at anything yet. An abstract class cannot be created on its own; only its subclasses can. MoneyTransfer is abstract with one subclass per way of moving money. The rule that no single transfer may exceed the limit is written once in the base class rather than three times. Notification has the same shape for messages. Both are inheritance, where a subclass gets everything its parent has. Both pass the substitution test, which asks whether a subclass can stand in for its parent. The legend after the diagram shows how to read every arrow used in this lesson.

Stage 2: the four order types

This is the stage worth the most in the interview.

Requirement 3 lists four kinds of order: market, limit, stop loss and stop limit. A candidate who writes four unrelated classes has missed what they have in common. All four hold a member, a symbol, a quantity and a direction. All four are filled in parts. All four have a status.

They differ in exactly two answers:

  • May this order trade at the current price? A market order and a limit order always say yes. The two stop types say no until the price reaches the stop, and yes for ever after.
  • What is the worst price the member will accept? A market order has no limit. A limit order and a stop limit order do.

So the base class holds everything shared and declares those two questions. Each subclass answers one or both, and none of them is longer than a few lines. Notice that StopLimitOrder extends StopLossOrder. A stop limit order is a stop order that becomes a limit order rather than a market order.

OrderPart exists because one order can be filled several times. A request for 100 shares may come back as 70 at one price and 30 at another. So the fill needs its own object with its own price, quantity and day.

OrderPart, Order and the four order types: Here are the classes related to orders:

This is the stage worth the most in the interview, and the diagram is the argument. Requirement 3 lists four kinds of order. A candidate who writes four unrelated classes has missed what they share. A member, a symbol, a quantity, a direction, a status and a list of parts all sit on one abstract Order. The four differ in two answers only: may this trade now, and is there a price limit. That is why the subclasses are so small. The filled diamond is composition: the whole owns its parts, and the parts die with it. Order owns its parts, because a fill means nothing without the order it belongs to.

Stage 3: the market

Stock holds the symbol and the latest price. StockInventory holds every stock and is the one place prices are read from. That stops two parts of the system disagreeing about what a share is worth.

StockExchange is the singleton. A singleton is a class that allows only one instance. It keeps the open orders, the order book, and a list of listeners. Note what it does not know: it does not know what a member is. It calls back through the OrderListener interface. An interface is a list of methods a class promises to provide. So the exchange and the account code can be changed apart from each other.

tick is where a stop order is finally triggered. A new price arrives, and every open order on that symbol gets another chance to trade.

Stock, StockInventory, OrderListener and StockExchange: These classes represent the market:

StockInventory owns the stocks and is the one place a price is read from. That stops two parts of the system disagreeing about what a share is worth. StockExchange holds the open orders and the order book with plain arrows. A plain arrow is an association: a link where neither class owns the other. The exchange does not own an order that a member placed. Notice the interface. The exchange calls back through OrderListener and never learns what a member is. So the exchange and the account code can be changed apart from each other.

Stage 4: lots and positions

Requirement 4 says a member can hold several lots of the same stock. It exists for one reason: tax. Selling 80 shares of a stock bought at three different prices produces a different gain depending on which shares you say you sold.

So a StockLot is one purchase at one price. A StockPosition is the list of lots for one symbol. Selling takes shares out of the oldest lots first unless the member names one. It returns what came out of each lot, because that is exactly what the tax report in the next stage needs.

One consequence to notice: an order that fills at two prices makes two lots, not one. A lot is defined by its price, not by the order it arrived on.

StockLot, LotSale and StockPosition: Here are the classes that track what a member owns:

Requirement 4 exists for one reason, which is tax. A StockLot is one purchase at one price. A StockPosition is the list of lots for one symbol, and the filled diamond says it owns them. Selling takes shares out of the oldest lots first unless the member names one. It returns a LotSale for each lot it touched, because that is exactly what a tax statement needs to know.

Stage 6: the actors

A member funds the account, keeps watchlists, places and cancels orders, and holds positions. An admin blocks and unblocks members.

The important field here is the reserved funds. When a member places a buy order, the money is not gone yet, but it is not spendable either. Without that, a member with 1,000 orders, and every one of them passes the check. So the member tracks what is promised and subtracts it from what is available. It releases the unspent part when the order fills or is cancelled.

callbackStockExchange is where money and shares actually move. It is called by the exchange, not by the member. That is why placing an order and paying for it are two different moments in this design.

Account, Member and Admin: These classes represent the actors:

The actors arrive last. Member extends Account and implements OrderListener, which is the dashed arrow. That is how the exchange calls back without knowing what a member is. Every filled diamond on Member says the same thing: positions, watchlists and trades belong to one member and to nobody else. The plain arrow to Order is different, because the exchange holds the same orders. The field worth naming out loud is the reserved funds. Without it a member with a thousand dollars can place ten separate thousand dollar orders, and every one of them passes the check.

The finished class diagram

Now that every class exists, here is the whole model in one picture. It is the same picture the six stages have been filling in, with every class in full color at last. Nothing on it appears here for the first time.

Here are the main classes of our Online Stock Brokerage System:

  • Account: Holds the member's name, address, e-mail, phone, total funds, and the funds available for trading. We have two types of accounts in the system: a general member and an Admin. The Account class also holds all the stocks the member owns.

  • StockExchange: The stockbroker system fetches all stocks and their current prices from the stock exchange. StockExchange is a singleton class that wraps every interaction with the stock exchange. This class is also used to place stock trading orders on the stock exchange.

  • Stock: The basic building block of the system. Every stock has a symbol and a current trading price.

  • StockInventory: Fetches and keeps the latest stock prices from the StockExchange. All parts of the system read the most recent stock prices from this class.

  • Watchlist: A list of stocks that the member wants to follow.

  • Order: Members place stock trading orders whenever they want to buy or sell stock positions. The system supports multiple types of orders:

    • Market Order: Buy or sell stocks immediately at the current market price.
    • Limit Order: Lets a user set a price at which they want to buy or sell a stock.
    • Stop Loss Order: An order to buy or sell once the stock reaches a certain price.
    • Stop Limit Order: Executes at a specified price or better after a given stop price has been reached. Once the stop price is reached, the stop-limit order becomes a limit order to buy or sell at the limit price or better.

  • OrderPart: An order can be filled in multiple parts. For example, take a market order to buy 100 stocks. It could have one part of 70 stocks at $10 and another part of 30 stocks at $10.05.

  • StockLot: Any member can buy multiple lots of the same stock at different times. This class represents these individual lots. For example, a user could buy 100 shares of AAPL yesterday and 50 more shares of AAPL today. While selling, users can select which lot they want to sell first.

  • StockPosition: Holds all the stocks that the user owns.

  • Statement: All members get reports for quarterly updates and yearly tax statements.

  • MoneyTransfer: Members can move money in and out by check, wire or electronic bank transfer. The three differ only in what the movement is called and how long it takes to clear. So they are three subclasses of one MoneyTransfer class rather than a separate class per direction.

  • Notification: Sends notifications to members.

The patterns in this design

Observer. The observer pattern lets one object announce a change to listeners it does not know. The exchange keeps one listener per member. It tells that listener when the member's order fills or is rejected. The order does not know who is watching, and the member does not poll. That is the pattern the cache and notification service lessons use for the same reason.

Singleton. The brokerage object allows one instance, with the usual trade-off.

Nothing else here is a named pattern. Order with market, limit and stop loss subclasses is a type hierarchy. Each kind decides for itself whether it can execute at the current price. No order ever changes into another kind.

What we left out, and what to say about it

Margin and short selling, dividends and splits, options, and real time streaming quotes are all absent. So are the settlement delay between the trade and the money moving, and the wash sale rule that changes what a tax statement may say. That was a decision.

Two places where a reviewer will question the design, and the honest answer to each:

  • Nothing here enforces the time in force. The enum has five values and no code reads them. A fill or kill order should be cancelled if it cannot be filled whole. A good till cancelled order should survive to the next day. Both belong in the exchange, next to the fill.
  • A market order can spend more than was reserved. The reservation uses the last known price. A market order takes whatever the book offers, which may be worse. The demo shows it: 100 shares reserved at 1,001.50. A real system reserves extra money as a buffer. Or it refuses prices more than a set distance above the last known price.

💡 In the interview: the four order types are the real test in this question. Say early that they differ in only two things: whether the order may trade yet, and what price it will accept. Then write one base class with those two methods. That single sentence is what separates a design from four copied classes. The second thing to say out loud is why lots exist. The tax owed depends on which shares you sold.
