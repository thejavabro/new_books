This chapter hands you the pencil. You play architect for a travel startup and run the whole process from requirements to diagram. This page turns each exercise into a workbench where you can try it, then compare with one reasonable answer.

The brief

TripEZ is a trip dashboard for frequent travellers: every booking, grouped by trip, on web and mobile. Here are the requirements in short form. Flip each to see what an architect hears in it.

2

The architect's roadmap

There's no checklist for architecture, but the four dimensions from chapter 1 give you a route. Step through it.

3

Step 1: Pick the driving characteristics

Choose up to seven, then mark your top three (tap a chosen one again to star it). Every pick should trace back to a requirement.

4

Step 2: Actors, actions and components

Using the actor/action approach from chapter 4, assign each action to whoever performs it. Correct matches light up the logical components they suggest.

Logical components only. Databases, queues and user interfaces belong to the physical architecture in step 5.

5

Step 3: Choose a style

Your choices are the three monolithic styles you know so far. Set how much each characteristic matters for TripEZ and watch the fit scores move. The style ratings are rough versions of the chapter 6–8 charts.

StyleGood for TripEZWorries
LayeredCheap, simple, quick to ship in six monthsEach partner integration's logic is smeared across layers
Modular monolithModules like Trips, Updates, Reporting match the domainIntegration still needs its own internal structure
MicrokernelEach travel partner becomes a plugin; adding one leaves the core aloneMonolith means one bad deploy can affect uptime

Step 4: Write the ADR

Pick a style. The decision record fills itself in, including the consequences, because every decision has some.

Step 5: Sketch the physical architecture

  
Now combine all four dimensions: user interfaces, deployable units, databases and how they connect. All three styles are viable; switch to compare sketches.

  

    

    

    

  

  
No right or wrong answers. In architecture the question is whether you can justify your trade-offs, not whether you matched the back of the book.

What this exercise teaches

  

    
Ask moreRequirements are never complete. Talk to stakeholders.

    
Trace itEvery driving characteristic ties to a requirement or business need.

    
Logical firstNo databases or queues in the logical view.

    
Style fitWeigh the style's ratings, the domain and your top characteristics.

    
Hybrids are normalMixing styles is fine if it still meets the critical characteristics.

    
Write it downAn ADR records the choice, the reasons and the consequences.
