Chapter 1

Understanding the structure behind every successful software system — what it is, its four dimensions, and how it differs from design.

Think of a House

Software architecture is like the structure of a home. The shape, the number of rooms, the floors, the layout — these are architectural. The paint color, the furniture, the rug? That's design.

Key insight: Architecture defines the structure — the hard, expensive-to-change stuff. It's the building plan of your software, represented as lines and boxes showing services, databases, and communication protocols.

All four are required. Saying "our architecture is microservices" describes only one dimension — the style — and leaves too many unanswered questions. You need all four pieces to complete the picture.

Dimension 1: Architectural Characteristics

These are the capabilities your system needs — scalability, testability, availability, performance. They form the foundation of every other architectural decision.

Architecture vs. Design

Architecture is about structure. Design is about appearance. Most decisions fall on a spectrum between the two.

● Architecture

  • How services communicate
  • Number of services & databases
  • Which services access which DBs
  • System deployment structure

● Design

  • Color of the UI
  • Field placement on screens
  • Class structure & patterns
  • Type of flooring (carpet vs wood)

Where Does Your Decision Fall?

Three factors help you determine if a decision is more architectural or more design-related:

🎯

Strategic vs Tactical

Long-term vision = architecture. Quick, independent choice = design.

💪

Level of Effort

Hard to change later = architecture. Easy to change = design.

⚖️

Significance of Trade-offs

Major pros/cons = architecture. Minor trade-offs = design.

Strategic Test: 3 Questions

1 How much thought and planning does it need? Minutes = tactical. Days/weeks = strategic.
2 How many people are involved? Solo = tactical. Many stakeholders = strategic.
3 Is it a long-term vision or short-term action? Living with it forever = strategic.

Chapter Summary

1. Software architecture is about structure, not appearance. It's the building plan of your system.
2. Four dimensions define architecture: characteristics, decisions, logical components, and style. All four are required and interconnected.
3. Architecture and design exist on a spectrum. Three factors determine where a decision falls: strategic vs tactical, level of effort, and significance of trade-offs.
4. Knowing where a decision falls on the spectrum helps determine who should make it — the architect, the dev team, or both together.
