Chapter 2

What capabilities must your architecture support? Learn to identify, categorize, and balance the building blocks of every software system.

Meet Lafter: A Case Study

Sillycon Symposia is building Lafter — a social media platform for tech-conference-going comedians. Before jumping into design, we need to figure out what the architecture must support.

📋 Lafter Requirements

  • Hundreds of speakers, thousands of users
  • Long-form "Jokes" and short-form "Puns" (max 281 chars)
  • "HaHa" (strong approval) and "Giggle" (mild approval) reactions
  • Speakers get special icons and can host forums
  • International support, very small support staff
  • "Bursty" traffic — extremely busy during live conferences
Don't skip to design. You need to analyze architectural characteristics and logical components before choosing an architectural style. Otherwise, you're putting the cart before the horse.

Defining Architectural Characteristics

Architectural characteristics are the nondomain design considerations — the capabilities your system needs beyond just "what it does." They're defined by three edges of a triangle.

🏗️ Domain (Requirements)

  • What the app should do
  • User stories & features
  • Business logic

⚡ Nondomain (Characteristics)

  • How well it operates
  • Performance, scalability
  • Operational & structural criteria

Characteristics Influence Structure

The characteristics you choose directly shape your architecture. Consider Lafter's promotions feature — does it live in one big app or its own service?

Monolithic

Change promotions → redeploy everything

Distributed

Change promotions → redeploy only that service
Structure follows characteristics. If deployability matters, a distributed architecture lets you update pieces independently. The choice depends on what characteristics matter most.

The Three Dangerous Properties

Architectural characteristics are tempting — but supporting too many leads to overengineering. They're dangerous because they are:

🔗

Synergistic

Changing one affects others. More security → less performance. They're interconnected.

🌊

Overabundant

New ones appear constantly. Cloud elasticity didn't exist a decade ago. The list never stops growing.

🏷️

Unstandardized

Different orgs use different terms. "Performance" and "responsiveness" might mean the same thing.

Beware RDD — Resume-Driven Development! It's fun to play with new tech, but supporting too many characteristics adds needless complexity. Pick the fewest you need, not the most you can imagine.

Explicit vs. Implicit Characteristics

Some characteristics are spelled out in requirements. Others you have to uncover yourself using domain knowledge.

📝 Explicit

  • Stated in requirements docs
  • "Must handle 10,000 users"
  • "Must be available 99.9%"
  • Business stakeholders ask for them

🔍 Implicit

  • Not in requirements, but essential
  • Security (never design insecure)
  • Maintainability, internal structure
  • Domain knowledge reveals them
Use your knowledge. A high-frequency trading firm might not say "transactions must complete within milliseconds" — but every architect in that domain knows it's critical. Uncover the implicit ones during analysis.

Where Do Characteristics Come From?

Architectural characteristics emerge from three distinct sources — not just requirements documents.

📄

Problem Domain

"Thousands of users" → scalability, concurrency, elasticity. Translate requirements into characteristics.

🏢

Environment

Fast-moving startup? Prioritize agility. Big enterprise with M&A plans? Choose open protocols.

🧠

Holistic Domain Knowledge

University sign-ups won't be evenly distributed — Type A students rush, procrastinators wait till the last minute.

Keep asking "But why?" When users bring solutions instead of requirements, imitate an annoying toddler. The US Air Force wanted Mach 2.5, but really needed to flee a fight — which led to the F-16's maneuverability instead.

Finding the Balance

More characteristics = more complexity. The magic number is 7 — based on research into how many items people can effectively reason about.

❌ No Characteristics
Expensive rework when you discover the system can't scale
✅ Good Balance (~7)
Enough to guide structure without overcomplicating
❌ Too Many Characteristics
Overengineered ivory-tower architecture, wasted effort
When stakeholders say "all of them!" — push back. Every characteristic adds complexity. Use the magic number 7 as a guideline: pick 7, then highlight the top 3 most critical.

Chapter Summary

1. Architectural characteristics are nondomain capabilities — they define how well a system operates, not what it does.
2. They're defined by three edges: nondomain, influences structure, and critical to success.
3. Watch for three dangerous properties: synergistic, overabundant, and unstandardized. They make overchoosing risky.
4. Look at three sources: the problem domain, your environment, and holistic domain knowledge.
5. Limit to 7 characteristics, highlight the top 3. Translate business language into measurable architectural terms.
