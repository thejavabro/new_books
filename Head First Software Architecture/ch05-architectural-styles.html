There are more styles than anyone can count. Two simple questions sort almost all of them: how is the code divided, and how is it deployed?

The style map

Like houses, software styles come in endless variety. To make sense of them, place each on two axes: partitioning (split by technical role or by business domain) and deployment (one unit or many). The rest of the book visits each corner. Click a style.

Partitioning: technical vs. domain

A fancy restaurant splits work by technical role: host, server, chef, busser. A food court splits by domain: a pizza place, a salad bar, a burger joint, each doing its own serving and cleaning. Software can be split either way too.

Deployment: one unit or many

  
A monolith deploys every component as one unit running in one process (one JAR, one executable). A distributed system spreads components over many units, each its own process, talking over the network. Try four everyday events on both.

Scorecard

  
Monoliths are easier to start, understand and debug, and cheaper at first, which makes them great for getting to market fast. Distributed systems scale, tolerate faults and stay modular as things grow, at a high price in complexity. Tap a row for the reason.

Recap on one page

  

    
Too many styles to countLearn the categories and you can place any new one.

    
Technical partitioningLayers by role: presentation, services, persistence. Fits specialised teams.

    
Domain partitioningSplit by business area. Aligns code with the problem.

    
MonolithicOne unit: simple, cheap, quick to market, easy to debug. Scales all-or-nothing, one bug can sink it.

    
DistributedMany units: scalable, fault tolerant, modular. Expensive, complex, at the mercy of the network.

    
No "better"Every quadrant has pros and cons. Choose by characteristics and domain.
