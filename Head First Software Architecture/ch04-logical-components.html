Adventurous Auctions goes online

A company auctions rare trips (safaris, Everest base camp) in rooms around the country. Now it wants online bidders too. The system must mix in-person and online bids, handle thousands of bidders, store card details, stream live video of the auction, decide which online bid came first, then charge and notify the winner.

2

Logical vs. physical architecture

A logical architecture shows the functional building blocks and how they depend on each other. It knows nothing about databases, services or protocols. A physical architecture maps those blocks onto services, databases, gateways and user interfaces. Same system, two views.

Only components and their dependencies. No technology anywhere.

3

The four-step loop

Creating a logical architecture is a cycle you keep running for the life of the system. Every new feature goes round again. Skipping it is how a tidy system becomes a mess. Click a step.

4

Step 1: finding the first components

Early on it is a guessing game, and that's fine. Start with a few "empty jars", one per major thing the system does, and refine later. Two techniques take some of the guessing out.

Workflow approach: follow the user's journey

Actor/action approach: who does what?

Useful when several kinds of user (actors) exist. List each actor's actions, then group actions into components. The system itself counts as an actor. Pick an actor.

The two approaches combine well: actor/action finds the actions, workflow puts them in order. It's close to event storming, minus the events.

5

The entity trap

It is tempting to make one component per thing (Auction, Bidder, Trip) and call it "Auction Manager". Those components become dumping grounds. Names ending in Manager, Handler, Supervisor or Controller are warning signs. Drop requirements in and watch.

Coupling: who depends on whom

Two components are coupled if changing one might force a change in the other. Afferent coupling (CA) counts incoming dependencies: how many others rely on me. Efferent coupling (CE) counts outgoing ones: how many I rely on. Total coupling CT = CA + CE. Click any component.

Memory aid: afferent = arriving. Doctors call nerves carrying signals to the brain afferent nerves.

10

The Law of Demeter

Also called the principle of least knowledge: a component should know as little as possible about the rest of the system. Demeter, the harvest goddess, grew the grain but never knew what mortals did with it. In the order system above, Order Placement knows the whole process, including what to do when stock is low. Move that knowledge to Inventory Management and compare.
