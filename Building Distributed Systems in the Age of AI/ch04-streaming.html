Streaming Architecture

Services deliver functions; using them leaves a trail of events. Streaming systems turn that trail into fresh, continuously computed facts, which is exactly what AI applications are hungry for.

1

From serving requests to reacting to data

A service waits. An actor sends a request, the service processes it, and responds; with no request, it sits idle. Its database holds the latest snapshot of reality, and services call each other in graphs that often loop (checkout asks the voucher service to validate a gift card; the voucher service watches orders to deactivate redeemed vouchers). A streaming application is a continuous dataflow that reacts whenever data arrives: a directed acyclic graph of operators between sources and sinks.

Using services leaves a trail of events. Captured, that trail powers new services: demand prediction, pricing, process mining, and above all AI, which needs huge volumes of fresh data. (Every CAPTCHA traffic light you clicked fed a supervised training set.)

2

Windows: making the endless countable

Bounded streams end (replaying a log, a batch source). Most streams are unbounded: data has always arrived and always will. How do you average something that never stops? You cut the stream into windows by event timestamp and aggregate each one. Pick a window type.

3

Event time, watermarks and late data

Every event has two times: when it happened (event time, stamped on the message) and when the pipeline sees it (processing time). Slow replicas and flaky networks make events arrive late and out of order. Processing time is fine for rough counts. When correctness matters, windows close by event time, using a watermark: "we probably won't see anything older than X any more". Events behind a closed window are late. Late events:
Correction has a price downstreamWith allowed lateness, a window fires at the watermark and fires again with a corrected result when a late event turns up. Consumers must be ready for numbers that change after they were reported.

Why AI cares: fresh data

6

A streaming mindset on a small budget

Streaming doesn't require a heavyweight stack. The book sketches near-real-time insights for an online shop with familiar tools: browsers send beacons to a small collector that validates against schemas and appends to a Redis stream; a Node service with RxJS reads it and runs windowed operators; an embedded DuckDB holds working state; results go to PostgreSQL for a simple dashboard.
