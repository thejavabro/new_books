What counts as AI keeps moving

The Kubernetes control plane fits the classic definition of an agent: goals written as manifests, perception through API observations, autonomous action to reconcile actual with desired state, and many controllers coordinating through shared state. Yet nobody calls it AI. Its rules are hand-coded; it can't learn, adapt its policy or reason over unstructured context.

"AI" has always been a moving label: once we understand a mechanism well enough to build it reliably, it becomes just software. Slide through time.

What matters insteadWhatever the label, systems that make autonomous decisions at a volume no human can review need governance, oversight and accountability. The useful question is not "is it AI?" but how deep does the intelligent part reach into the system.
2

Three depths of AI in a system

Three depths are visible in production today. They add up rather than compete: a level 3 system contains levels 1 and 2. Switch between them.

Which level is it?

3

Level 1: a model as a function

The engineer decides when the model is called, with what, and what happens next. The model may be stochastic, but the program's shape is constant. That still needs output validation, fallbacks, monitoring and quality thresholds. A sketch of my own:

def handle_review(text):
    label, score = classify(text)   # model call
    if label == "NEG" and score > 0.8:
        alert_team(text)
    store(text, label)
# the flow never changes;
# only the label varies
The real boundaryNot model size, not output variability, but who controls the execution flow. A chatbot that receives, generates and replies, even with app-driven RAG, is still level 1. Give the model tools and freedom to choose, and it becomes level 2. Not every AI use case must be agentic.
4

Level 2: the model runs the show

Now the model decides which tools, which parameters, what order, when to stop. The question shifts from "is the output correct?" to "was the goal achieved?", which is often subjective. Cost and latency per request become unpredictable, which makes pricing plans hard. So level 2 needs budget controls. Run 200 simulated requests.

Start at the depth the problem needsThe jump from level 1 to 2 is the biggest qualitative shift: from AI as a capability to AI as a decision-maker. Before going agentic, check whether a model call, a rules engine, a workflow or a retrieval pipeline would be safer, cheaper and easier to govern.
5

Level 3: from hunting to herding

Humans moved from hunting each meal to keeping herds: from solving instances to building capability. A computer isn't a solution to one problem; it's a tool for a million. At level 3, AI produces reusable artifacts (code, models, configurations, workflows, even level 1 or 2 systems) that solve a class of problems. Nothing fully embodies it yet, but a spectrum is visible. Step along it.
