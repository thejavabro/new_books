1

Capture the message, not the chart

In lecture-based notes the author rarely copies a whole diagram. Diagrams are dense and slow to draw accurately, and every second spent drawing axes is a second not spent listening. Most diagrams carry one overall message, like "sales are increasing" or "it took three years to recover from the recession." Capture that. Still, sometimes a diagram is the clearest way to make information stick, and anything that improves retention is a win. Compare.

4

Show, don't just tell

"65 percent of our web traffic came from mobile, 20 percent from desktop, and 15 percent from tablets." Heard as a string, it's gone in a minute. The presenter is telling; you can show. Drag the sliders and watch how the picture makes the gap obvious in a way the sentence doesn't.

5

How to choose a diagram

When the presenter gives no diagram, you pick one. Four steps:

  1. Goal: what is the message, and which type shows it best?
  2. Data: what must be shown, and in how much detail?
  3. Organize and categorize the data.
  4. Try alternatives if nothing is obvious; edit, refine, simplify.

The book's exercise gives three sets of information. Choose a diagram for each and see it drawn; then try another type and judge which is clearer.
