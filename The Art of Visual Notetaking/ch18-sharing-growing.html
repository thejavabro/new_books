Sharing, Improving and Going Pro

The last step of visual notes is sharing them. Then you track your own habits, keep practicing, and maybe turn it into paid work.

Sharing is the last step

Finished and refined? Not quite done. The last true step of visual notes is sharing them. The author anticipates three objections and answers each. Open them.

Her own proofShe is a professional sketch artist because she risked drawing silly cartoons on an office whiteboard. She didn't think people would like them. You never know what sharing might lead to.
2

Capturing the best image

People can only read what they can see. Natural daylight beats indoor light; if light is poor, take several shots with and without flash and compare (flash can wash out colors, but saves you in a pinch). Shoot straight on, edges parallel to the frame, no shadow of yourself across the page. Use normal or HD mode, tap to focus, take at least three shots, stitch big canvases from several photos, or scan small ones. Set up the shot.

PostingLightly edit (brightness, contrast) so it's easy to read. When posting, hashtags like #sketchnotes and #visualnotes help others interested in visual notes find your work.
3

The value of sharing: community and a growth record

Sharing connects you with a worldwide sketchnoting community that loves to learn from and help each other, and gives you feedback when you feel stuck. It also records your growth. The author rarely notices improvement page to page, but sees it clearly sketchbook to sketchbook, and followers notice changes she can't. Scroll back through a year of posts.

4

When should you share?

Memory from hearing alone fades minute by minute, so get notes out fast. The author aims for within 30 minutes of a session ending. After class, post right away; after church, the same day; at a conference, before it closes. But some events are not public: invitation-only, closed-door, internal or proprietary content. Ask the organizer, always clarify what you may share, and never post anything that could compromise anyone's identity or safety. When unsure, she asks herself three questions. Try a case.

Sometimes you just choose not toThe author once sketched during a medical procedure. She was proud of the work but disliked the subject, and kept it private so as not to attract similar jobs. A disclaimer is one way to share content you don't personally endorse. And people value visual notes: some sell copies after events.
5

Improving your craft: track your trends

As your style emerges, watch your habits. Do you take lots of notes early and taper off? Write huge? Draw instead of listening? Self-awareness helps you refine skills and plan layouts. Keeping the same tools and canvas while practicing makes trends visible. Rate your last session.

PracticeRegularly, even daily. You may not see a change right away; trust that you are improving. And share your progress.
6

Tips for left-handed notetakers

Lefties drag their hand across fresh ink. You can still be a great visual notetaker; it takes new habits. Switch tips on and watch the smudge meter.
