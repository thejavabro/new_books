Your Visual Library and Visual Metaphors

A ready stock of quick drawings saves you in the moment. Visual metaphors go one step further, turning an abstract idea into a picture people instantly get.

Your visual library

Take visual notes regularly and some drawings keep coming back, because they fit so many situations. That mental bank of images you can draw on demand is your visual library. It saves you from looking up references mid-talk when something comes out of the blue. Here are the images the author says she uses most. Tap the ones you could already draw without thinking.

2

Building and keeping it fresh

"How do I know what to fill it with if I never know what I'll need?" Learn to draw in general; start with dots, lines and shapes; go for recognizable. The exercise: draw the 10 things closest to you right now, then things from home and work, then draw for whole industries. What would you draw for science? Healthcare? Marketing? Update for relevance: the phone

Everyone used to draw a flip phone; now it's a flat rectangle. Watch hairstyles, tech, fashion and skylines for the same kind of drift.

3

Know it five ways

Professionals especially need variety: clients don't want notes that look like everyone else's. Easy levers are size, proportions, direction, perspective, style and color. The book's exercise: list your 10 most-used images, draw each, then redraw each until you have five versions. Pick an item.

4

Visual metaphors: your first idea is usually best

Art school teaches that your first idea is never your best, because art aims to be unique. Visual communication is the opposite: you want common ground, so the first, most obvious idea is usually right. Visual metaphors build that common ground. They can draw a metaphor the speaker used, or one you devise to make the point clearer.

Do you have to? No. You can capture exactly what is said. But a metaphor is a way of leveling up: you aren't adding content (no extra opinions or bullet points), you are arranging the content with images that create understanding. Ask: "What would I draw to represent this topic?"

Presenter: "At our company we cultivate and invest in our team. We hire only the best and quickly remove what's toxic. Our culture is fueled by happiness and helpfulness."

5

Devising metaphors: practice

Tips from the book: trust your first idea; pick key words that inspire a picture (above, "cultivate" suggested a garden); try word associations and synonyms; don't force it, since metaphors should be obvious and simple; and never let it distract you from listening. Pick the clearest metaphor for each idea.
