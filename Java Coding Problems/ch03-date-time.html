Pick the right java.time type first

Most date bugs come from using a type that carries too much or too little. java.time (JDK 8) splits the world into human time (dates on a calendar, wall-clock times, maybe with a zone) and machine time (an instant on the global timeline, and amounts of time). Every class is immutable and thread-safe, unlike Date and Calendar, which the chapter still converts from (P69, P86). Open a card.

2

Good morning, wherever you are

P68 greets a friend in another time zone. Convert now into their zone, then map the hour to a period: night 21:00–5:59, morning 6:00–11:59, afternoon 12:00–17:59, evening 18:00–20:59. Before JDK 16 you write that mapping yourself; since JDK 16 the pattern letter B prints a locale-aware period like "in the morning". Drag the clock.

LocalTime lt = LocalTime.now(ZoneId.of("Australia/Melbourne"));       // before JDK 16: map lt yourself
String s = ZonedDateTime.now(zone).format(DateTimeFormatter.ofPattern("yyyy-MMM-dd [B]"));
// "2024-Mar-14 in the evening"   (JDK 16+)

4

Counting between two dates

ChronoUnit.X.between(a, b) counts complete units, truncating. That is why weeks or quarters between two dates can surprise you by one (P85, P87). Period.between splits the gap into years, months and days instead.

5

Split a date-time range into equal pieces

P79: take the Duration between start and end, divide it by n, and keep adding that step. The last piece absorbs any leftover nanoseconds so the end is exact.

Duration step = Duration.between(start, end).dividedBy(n);
List<LocalDateTime> cuts = Stream.iterate(start, t -> t.plus(step)).limit(n).toList();
6

Stopwatches and clocks

Two very different "nows" (P77). System.nanoTime() is monotonic: it never jumps, has no meaning as a date, and is the right tool for measuring elapsed time. Instant.now() is wall-clock time; it can jump when the OS syncs with NTP, so durations measured with it can even be negative.

Clock.systemUTC() vs Clock.systemDefaultZone() (P80)Both return the same instant. They differ only in the zone attached, which matters when you ask the clock for a LocalDateTime: LocalDateTime.now(Clock.systemUTC()) gives UTC wall time, the other your machine's zone. Inject a Clock (or Clock.fixed) and your time code becomes testable.
