Becoming an Effective Leader: Roles and Traits

Leadership is not a title. Management produces order; leadership produces movement. This first half of chapter 7 looks at the leadership roles in engineering teams and the traits that make people in them effective.

1

Management produces order; leadership produces movement

You don't need a manager's title to lead. Drawing on John Kotter, the book separates the two: leaders establish direction, align people and motivate; managers plan and budget, organize and staff, and control and solve problems. Neither replaces the other, and the best managers lead too. Sort these activities: tap one, then a side.

2

Tech lead, engineering manager, tech lead manager

After a few years as an engineer, two tracks open: technical and managerial. Big teams may have several tech leads, architects, managers and a product manager; small teams merge roles. Pick a role to see where its time goes and what it owns. (Time splits are illustrative.)

    One way the roles can fit together in a larger team. In small teams, a single TLM may cover most of this.

    3

    The development abstraction layer

    An underrated management job, in Joel Spolsky's phrase: build the "development abstraction layer". If a developer is personally fighting for repository access or a firewall exception, the abstraction has failed. Turn the layer on and watch where the blockers go.

    4

    Critical traits: expertise, agility, communication

    Three traits matter most for tech leaders. Technical expertise: solid foundations for the project (a web veteran leading a first mobile app may struggle), and having helped someone crack a hard problem within the last three months. Agility: learning, unlearning and acting fast on change. An engineering director with 50+ engineers on three continents met the switch to remote work with one-on-ones and a team poll on norms, then adapted again when productivity dipped, giving more autonomy rather than more control. Communication: understood the first time, tailored to the audience, listening as much as talking.

    The launch that forgot one audienceFor a developer-focused launch, the author's team looped in product, marketing, PR, business development and leadership, and forgot developer relations. DevRel scrambled at the last minute. The lesson: over-share internally, and check your message is complete.

    The last trait can't be self-assessed by questions. You ask people who'll be honest, then compare. Rate yourself on a few traits, then enter how your team rated you (from a survey or candid feedback).
