Architecture Domains

TOGAF splits the enterprise into four architecture domains. They look like layers on a diagram, but they are not the same thing, and mixing them up causes real confusion.

1

Four domains, one enterprise

Whatever role an architect plays, they work in one or more architecture domains. TOGAF names four. Data and Application together are called the Information Systems Architectures. Tap a domain.

2

Where did the information domain go?

TOGAF 7 knew only Technology Architecture. TOGAF 8 introduced the familiar four, but called one of them "Data or Information Architecture". Those words later vanished. With BIZBOK now referenced, which stresses information, the author expects Information Architecture may return.

3

Data + context = information

TOGAF's Data Architecture knows the data entity but no information concept. BIZBOK describes information concepts in much more detail, and the two are not comparable: data alone carries little meaning. Add the context.

4

Do you need all four domains?

A complete Enterprise Architecture covers all four domains. Time and money often say otherwise, and architecture descriptions are purpose-driven: if the goal is to compare technology options and core processes won't change, a full Business Architecture may be unnecessary. But skip a domain and, by definition, what you have is not a complete EA. Choose what to include.

5

Domains are not layers

People have confused the two for years. Layers are levels of abstraction inside one system: presentation, business logic, data access, infrastructure, talking only to neighbours through interfaces. Domains are broad areas of concern across the whole enterprise. Some concerns cut across every domain: security, motivation and governance. Flip between them.

Security is not just ITThe ACMM maturity model lists IT security as one of its nine elements, which misses that security cuts across every domain, business processes included.
6

Which domains does each role cover?

Roles map onto domains. Pick a role and see its scope. Day-to-day practice varies, but expecting a Solution Architect to fix your Business Architecture is a false expectation.
