Enterprise Metamodel

Before you draw a single diagram you need a vocabulary: which kinds of things exist in an enterprise, and how they are allowed to relate. That vocabulary is the Enterprise Metamodel.

1

Five sections, 36 entities

Every artifact the ADM produces is built from a small set of entity types. TOGAF calls the set the Enterprise Metamodel and offers a Foundation version to start from. It has five sections: one of General Entities that can relate to anything, and one per architecture domain. Note the word "section": this is a metamodel, not a layered architecture diagram. Tap any entity.

The business service is the hingeData, application and technology entities all connect upward to the business service entity in the Business Architecture section. That is where "what the business does" meets "what supports it".
2

Principles frame, requirements detail, constraints limit

Three general entities do most of the steering. A principle is like a clause of a constitution: abstract, stable and binding. A requirement details it into a concrete business need that a work package must meet. A constraint, often brought by a stakeholder, limits what can be done. Step through the example.

3

From driver to measure

TOGAF keeps motivation concepts in the Business Architecture section (ArchiMate puts them in a separate motivation group). Follow the book's healthcare example along the chain. Toggle a link off to see what goes missing.

4

Capability versus business capability

A capability is simply an ability. An airplane can carry people from A to B, and so can a car; what is special about the plane is that it can fly. A motorcycle can weave through a traffic jam, though only with a rider. A business capability adds something crucial: it exists to achieve a specific purpose or outcome. Having an ability says nothing about its usefulness; purpose makes it valuable.

5

Gaps between states

A gap is the difference between two architecture states, and work packages (initiatives) close them. Application A lacks functions the objective needs, so it will be replaced by B, but not overnight. Drag through the states.

6

Logical first, physical later

The three lower sections share one pattern: a service that something offers, a logical component that says what is needed without naming a product, and a physical component that realizes it with something bought, hired or built. Data is the odd one: a data entity, logical data component and physical data component. Pick a domain and flip between logical and physical.

7

Tailor it in the Preliminary phase

The Foundation metamodel is a starting point. During the Preliminary phase you build an organization-specific version: drop what you don't need, add what you do. Then it feeds the Content Framework, which feeds the ADM's deliverables, and what you learn flows back. Many architects skip this step. Try tailoring the metamodel in the first diagram with the buttons below, then check the flow.

Add a stakeholder entityTOGAF quietly uses organization unit to mean stakeholders. The author recommends adding an explicit stakeholder entity, as ArchiMate has, since stakeholder catalogs use it constantly. He also suggests business collaboration, interaction, interface, resource, risk and value, which the Business Model Diagram needs.
