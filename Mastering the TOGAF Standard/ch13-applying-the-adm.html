Applying the ADM

The ADM is a template, not a script. This chapter shows the ways to run it: by purpose, by style, in iteration cycles, and split across partitions of the enterprise.

1

One method, many situations

The ADM is a step-by-step approach, which is not the same as a waterfall. Its tailorability lets it handle different process styles and specialized architectures such as security. The book focuses on three applications that cover most situations: using TOGAF with different architecture styles, applying iteration, and partitioning the architecture.

2

Adapting to an architecture style

Service-oriented, microservices, zero-trust: an organization often has several styles in its landscape at once. The recipe: identify the style's unique characteristics, then decide how to address them, usually by adapting models, viewpoints and tools rather than changing the framework. Pick a style.

A dominant style may even send you back to the Preliminary phase to adjust the Architecture Capability. Style-specific reference models and maturity models help.

3

The ADM graphic is not a flowchart

The famous picture is a stylized view of information flows, not a sequence of activities. Whenever an architect works on a roadmap, they are "in" Phase E, consuming its inputs and producing its outputs, regardless of what came before. Compare the two readings.

4

Three ways to iterate, four iteration groups

TOGAF names three ways to iterate: across the whole cycle (for different levels or scopes, such as enterprise then project), within a cycle (looping between phases), and to manage the capability (Preliminary and A). In practice this gives four iteration groups. Pick one and watch the loop.

5

Baseline first or target first?

The Architecture Development group can start from the current state or from the desired one. Target first is common in practice. Step through the iterations of each.

6

A worked example: one budget, two initiatives

Two goals: implement new regulation, use human resources efficiently. Initiative A hires staff with a new job profile, needing the Inflow Support capability. Initiative B re-evaluates job profiles and replaces the compensation application. Both cost about the same, and the budget can be spent only once. Both are worked out in B and C, then presented to the CIO in Phase F, who weighs them with Alternatives and Trade-Offs. Then another development iteration refines the chosen one. Weigh it yourself.

7

Partitioning: divide the work, not the picture

Big organizations can't build architecture as one monolith. Partitioning splits it into parts with their own stakeholders, policies and compliance needs, so teams work in parallel, at different times, incrementally. Like agile, it breaks work down. The danger: pieces that don't fit together. Change the number of teams and toggle the shared definitions agreed in the Preliminary phase.
