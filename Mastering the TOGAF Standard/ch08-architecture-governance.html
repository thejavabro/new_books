Architecture Governance

An architecture nobody follows is a drawing. Governance is how an organization makes sure the plans are used, deviations are decided on deliberately, and the board that decides is set up well.

1

Who steers the architecture

Architecture governance turns an organization's architectural needs into a coherent set of policies, processes, procedures and standards, and checks that the vision lines up with real business demands. It is a shared responsibility of business leaders, not just a CIO's job. TOGAF offers a reference Governance Framework; treat it as a starting point. Compare it with the author's adjustment.

Where does the CIO belong?Information is owned by the business, so the author argues the Chief Information Officer belongs on the business side. The CTO fits in IT. Yet in nine of ten organizations, and in TOGAF's own picture, the CIO sits in IT.

Implementing governance in ten steps

Phase G of the ADM puts governance into practice, but first it has to exist. The book's ten pragmatic steps; tap each to see what it involves and tick it off.

3

A board, not a meeting of architects

"Board" refers to the boardroom. An Architecture Board is a cross-organization body of senior executives that decides strategic architecture questions and oversees the implementation of strategy. In practice, nine times in ten it is a group of IT architects debating designs, so strategic decisions get made elsewhere and architecture stays reactive. Assemble a board and see what it can cover.

Size and mixFour or five standing members, never more than ten. Not exclusively C-level: an all-executive board can stay too high-level for implementers to act on. Bring in unit managers and experts per topic as non-permanent guests so issues get strategic, tactical and operational attention. Assign each member a set of stakeholders to represent.
4

CEO, CIO or CTO: who leads changes the scope

New legislation demands a different organizational structure with matching processes, information flows and applications. How much of that the architecture can address depends on who leads the board.

Small and flat organizations need a board too, perhaps more: they are often pragmatic about rules. No C-titles required. What matters is sponsorship from the highest level available.

5

When to set one up, and what it is for

Typical triggers: a new CIO or CTO, a merger or acquisition, restructuring after big change or fast growth, or the realization that strategy isn't being implemented, or is implemented badly. Rotation with continuity

Rotating members spreads ownership and relieves busy executives. The risk: the Business Architecture lurching from one set of ideas to another. The fix is fixed terms that end at different times. Drag through the years and compare.

7

Ten steps to set up a board

From the author's practice

At a healthcare regulator, the "Architecture Board" was all technicians; a fierce debate about a new backup solution was the highlight of the week. He began collecting strategic input from management teams in a new advisory group. As managers saw their input acted on, participation grew. The new group was eventually renamed Architecture Board; the old one became the Architects' Consultation. Nobody lost a forum, so there was hardly any resistance.
