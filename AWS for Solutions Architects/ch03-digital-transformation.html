Leveraging the Cloud for Digital Transformation

Moving to the cloud means choosing a service model, a migration plan for every application, and changing the organization. This chapter covers all three, plus the architectures that keep things running.

1

Three service models: who manages what

Cloud services sit at different heights of the technology stack. With IaaS (infrastructure as a service) AWS runs the hardware, network and virtualization, and you manage the operating system up. With PaaS (platform) AWS also runs the OS and runtime, and you bring code and data. With SaaS (software) you just use the application. Pick a model to see the split.

You manageAWS manages
Rule of thumbLet AWS manage as much as possible. Drop down to PaaS or IaaS only when you need software the higher-level service doesn't support, or when the managed option costs more at your scale (some heavy users find a self-run Presto cluster cheaper than Athena).

Migration in three phases

4

The 7 Rs: one strategy per application

Not every application should move the same way. AWS names seven patterns, and the book adds an eighth, revise (fix the code before moving). The book's analogy for rehost: moving house without sorting your stuff. It's fast, but you pay to move things you don't need. Read each scenario and choose the best R.

5

Digital transformation, not just migration

The book defines digital transformation as using the cloud and other technology to create new business flows or change existing ones. It is a chance to rethink team structures, business processes and how features get built. Lifting servers into AWS without changing anything else is migration, not transformation. Retailers that didn't adapt went bankrupt; firms like TGI Fridays (mobile ordering through virtual assistants) changed how customers deal with them.

One-way or two-way door?

Tip 6: decisions you can easily undo (two-way doors) should be made quickly. Decisions you can't take back (one-way doors) deserve more data and care. Sort these.

6

The Cloud Adoption Framework

The AWS Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF) gives everyone a shared mental model for transformation. It runs in four iterative phases and targets outcomes such as lower risk, better performance, more revenue and higher productivity. Tap a phase.

7

Four architectures for availability

Real reliability comes from redundancy: keeping copies on different devices in different places. (S3 Standard, for example, stores data redundantly across at least three data centers.) Choose an architecture, then fail nodes by tapping them.

8

Chaos engineering, RTO and RPO

Plan for failure with two targets. RTO (recovery time objective) is how long a resource may stay unavailable. RPO (recovery point objective) is how old restored data may be, that is, how much recent data you can afford to lose. Drag the backup interval and the restore time to see what you would actually achieve.

Objectives are only hopes until tested. Chaos engineering breaks systems on purpose, under controlled conditions, to find weak spots such as missing fallbacks, retry storms and cascading failures before they find you at 3 a.m.
