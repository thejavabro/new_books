Data Lake Patterns

A data lake keeps all your data, raw and refined, in one cheap, governed place. This chapter builds one in zones, then compares it with the lakehouse and the data mesh.

1

What a data lake is for

Data is everywhere, scattered across databases, logs, files, SaaS apps and devices: silos. A data lake is an architectural approach that brings structured and unstructured data together in one central, cheap, durable repository (on AWS, S3) in its native format, then catalogs, secures and serves it to every kind of analytics. A small start-up may not need one. Once data is a strategic asset, executives no longer ask "do we need a data lake?" but "how do we build one?"

2

Zones: from raw ore to gold bars

Lakes are divided into zones by how refined and trusted the data is, each with its own access controls and retention rules. The book's analogy is gold: ore is mined, washed, refined, cast into bars and finally made into jewelry for particular buyers. Follow one sales record through.

3

Building it with AWS Lake Formation

Lake Formation sets up a secure data lake on S3 in days rather than months. It uses Glue underneath for crawlers, the catalog and ETL, and adds one place to define permissions down to the column, row and cell. The book's steps:

  1. Create an IAM role with the permissions Lake Formation needs.
  2. Create the data lake in the Lake Formation console (register S3 locations).
  3. Ingest from S3, Redshift, RDS and other sources (blueprints help).
  4. Define the catalog: tables, columns, partitions.
  5. Set access and security policies.
  6. Transform with Glue.
  7. Analyze with Athena, Redshift Spectrum, EMR or QuickSight.

Step 5 in action. Pick who is querying the customers table.

5

Measuring a data lake

How do you know the lake is working? The book suggests a starting set of metrics. Size alone means little. What matters is how much of the lake is trusted (cleaned and governed) and whether stale data is archived or purged. Move the sliders.

6

Data lake, lakehouse or data mesh?

A lakehouse adds warehouse-style structure, ACID transactions and fast SQL on top of the lake. On AWS that is S3 plus Lake Formation governed tables, Redshift with Spectrum, and open table formats. A data mesh changes who owns the data: each business domain publishes its own data as a product through a standard interface, under shared, federated governance, instead of everything flowing through one central data team that lacks domain knowledge. Weigh your situation.
