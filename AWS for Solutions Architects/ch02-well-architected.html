Why a Well-Architected Framework?

Architects keep asking the same questions: is this reliable enough, fast enough, secure enough, cheap enough? AWS turned thousands of customer reviews into the Well-Architected Framework: a set of pillars, design principles and questions. It does three things for you: teaches strategies for architecting in the cloud, lets you measure a design against best practice, and shows you what to improve.

The free Well-Architected Tool in the console runs a Well-Architected Review (WAR). You describe a workload (environment, Regions, industry), answer each pillar's questions, and get a report of high-risk issues (HRIs) and medium-risk issues with an improvement plan.

Reliability: MTBF and MTTR

Reliability is the chance that something keeps doing its job for a given time. It is not the same as availability: reliability is about how long it works between failures, availability about how much of the time it is up. Two numbers tie them together: Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF) and Mean Time To Repair (MTTR). Availability ≈ MTBF ÷ (MTBF + MTTR). Self-healing systems attack MTTR: if a replacement starts automatically in a minute, failures barely register.

Lenses for specific workloads

The framework is general. Lenses add questions for a particular kind of workload or industry. By April 2022 AWS had 13, and each lens still checks the workload pillar by pillar.

6

The certification map

Certifications build credibility with employers and clients. AWS groups them by level and role. Tap a box to see who it is for. You no longer need an Associate certification before sitting for a Professional one, but the book strongly recommends taking two or three Associate exams first, since they overlap.

7

How long will it take?

The book's rough budgets, assuming no prior AWS experience: 15 to 25 hours for Cloud Practitioner, 70 to 100 hours for your first Associate exam (then 20 to 40 more for each of the other two), more again for Professional, and 40 to 70 hours for most Specialties. Studying a little every day beats long gaps. Set your weekly hours.
