Kickstart Your DevOps Journey

DevOps is not a tool or a job title you install. It is a way of working that joins the people who build software with the people who run it, and leans on automation to turn a slow, risky release into a steady loop.

1

DevOps is a way of working, not a product

An application has two halves of a life. Development writes and tests it. Operations deploys it onto servers and keeps it running. In many organisations those two groups sit on either side of a wall: developers "throw" a release over, operations catch it, and every handover is slow and risky. DevOps is the link between the two halves: shared goals, shared tooling and a lot of automation, so that development, testing and deployment live under one umbrella.

Everything starts with the applicationA DevOps engineer usually doesn't write the application, but must understand how developers work, what services the app talks to, how it should be tested, which servers (on-premises, cloud or containers) it runs on, the Linux under those servers, and the network between them. Much of that lands on your desk.

The book returns again and again to the same loop: continuous development, testing, deployment and monitoring. Each pass is a repeat, but each repeat should be a little better than the last. Pick a phase to see what happens there.

3

Agile and DevOps: different focus, same values

The two ideas were born separately and are now almost always mentioned together. Agile delivers software in small iterations so customers can try something early and say what they think. DevOps is the set of internal practices that makes shipping those iterations cheap and safe. Agile wants short iterations; that is only realistic with the automation DevOps brings. Adopting both at once: seven steps

Combined, Agile gives DevOps clear priorities, and the agile need to ship often justifies the up-front cost of automation. The chapter suggests a simple seven-step path. Step through it and watch the organisation change shape.

Plan, code, build, test, release, deploy, operate, monitor, repeat

The full cycle has eight stops. A change enters at Plan and, if it survives every stop, reaches users at Deploy. Operating and monitoring it generate the feedback that feeds the next plan. Step through and read what a DevOps engineer does at each stop.
