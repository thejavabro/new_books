Dataset Engineering

Finetuning is easy; getting the data is hard. What good data looks like, how much you need, how to make more of it with AI and how to clean what you have.

1

Data is the differentiator

As fewer companies can afford to train models from scratch, data becomes the way to stand out. Dataset work has grown from a side task into dedicated roles (labellers, dataset creators, data quality engineers). And there's no cure-all: most of it is careful, unglamorous toil.

Model-centricHold the data fixed; improve the model (architecture, size, training technique). Classic benchmarks worked this way: given ImageNet, train the best model.
Data-centricHold the model fixed; improve the data (processing, better datasets). Andrew Ng's 2021 competition and DataComp (2023) asked entrants to build the best dataset instead. Real progress usually needs both.

This chapter is about post-training data (instruction and preference data) because that is what application developers usually work with. Different training phases need different data, but the same curation principles apply.

2

Data curation: quality, coverage, quantity

Training data should show the behaviours you want the model to learn, and the format matters: chain-of-thought examples (step-by-step responses) are slow to write, so they are rarer; tool-use examples need special formats and are hard for humans to write well, since what's efficient for people (clicking through search results) isn't what's efficient for a model (calling an API); multi-turn data needs purpose-built scenarios. Curation is also about removing data that teaches bad habits: if your model adds unsolicited suggestions, check whether the training data does.

What high-quality data means

A small amount of good data can beat a large amount of noisy data. The Yi team found 10K carefully crafted instructions beat hundreds of thousands of noisy ones. LIMA, a 65B Llama finetuned on 1,000 carefully curated examples, gave answers equivalent or strictly preferred to GPT-4's in 43% of human-judged cases, though so few examples left it less robust than production models. 4

Coverage: the right mix

Data should cover the range of problems users will bring: short and long instructions, typos, different topics, languages, output formats. Which axes matter depends on the app; a French-to-English tool needs no language diversity, but a chatbot needs breadth. Meta said Llama 3's gains over earlier versions came mostly from data quality and diversity, not architecture.

Llama 3 domain mix by training phase

How do you choose a mix? One simple approach is to match real usage. Another is to run experiments with small models and extrapolate, as Meta did. Zhou et al. (2023) found that a 7B model trained on data that was both high-quality and diverse beat versions trained on data with only one of those traits.

5

Quantity: how much is enough?

Anything from a few hundred to millions of examples. What drives it: the finetuning technique (full finetuning needs orders of magnitude more than PEFT), task complexity (sentiment is easier than financial Q&A), and the base model (the closer it already is, the fewer examples). OpenAI's finetuning guide: with 100 examples, stronger models end up much better; with 550,000, all five tested models perform similarly. So: little data → PEFT on a strong model; lots of data → a smaller model can be enough.

Start small

Before investing in a big dataset, try about 50 well-crafted examples. A clear improvement suggests more data will help more. No improvement may mean bad hyperparameters or data problems rather than a hopeless task. Finetune on 25%, 50% and 100% of your data and plot the gains to estimate what more data will buy.

Stretch scarce good data

Finetune first on cheaper data, then on the precious set: self-supervised → supervised (raw legal documents, then legal Q&A); less-relevant → relevant (general sentiment, then product reviews); synthetic → real (synthetic medical reports, then a few real ones).

Acquiring and annotating data

The best source is usually your own application: user content, usage logs, feedback. A data flywheel that turns usage into better data is a real advantage because that data matches your distribution exactly. Check public datasets first (Hugging Face, Kaggle, Google Dataset Search, government portals and others), read their licences, and know where the data came from. In practice you mix and match:

Annotation guidelines are the hard partWhat makes a response a 3 rather than a 4? Can a correct but unhelpful answer score well? Some teams, LinkedIn among them, say writing guidelines was among the hardest parts of their pipeline, and people sometimes give up halfway. The same guidelines serve your evaluation data (Chapter 4), so evaluation examples can seed training data and vice versa.
7

Data augmentation and synthesis

Augmentation derives new data from real data (flip a cat photo, and it's still a cat). Synthesis makes data that mimics real data. The line blurs, and the chapter uses "synthesis" for both. Synthetic data won't fully replace human data, but it can improve the data trio of quantity, coverage and quality, help with privacy and enable distillation.

Traditional: rules and simulation

Template generator

Fill a template's slots from lists, or a library such as Faker, to get structured documents like transactions, invoices or contracts. Fraud models are often first trained this way, then on real data.

Swap to reduce bias

Swapping gendered words, names or roles adds counterexamples so the model doesn't learn spurious associations. Perturbation (adding noise; BERT replaced 1.5% of tokens with random words) can improve robustness too.

Simulation generates data where real experiments are costly or dangerous: self-driving cars, robots, tool-use action sequences, rare events such as a bankruptcy or a manufacturing defect.

AI-powered synthesis

AI can simulate API outcomes, play against itself (self-play, used for game bots such as OpenAI's Dota 2 bot, or agents negotiating with each other), paraphrase ("How can I change my password?"), and translate. MetaMath rewrote 15,000 MATH and GSM-8K examples in different ways to make almost 400,000. Most synthetic data goes into post-training: pre-training data already contains growing amounts of AI text, and post-training data is the expensive kind to collect by hand.

Back-translation as a quality check

Instruction synthesis

Generate instructions from topic and keyword lists, templates or seed examples, then responses. Alpaca started with 175 human seed tasks and had text-davinci-003 generate 52,000 (instruction, response) pairs. UltraChat first had AI generate topics, then multi-turn dialogues. Reverse instruction goes the other way: take good long-form human content and have AI write the prompt that would produce it, so the response has no AI hallucinations.

Bootstrapping and long context

Li et al. (2023): train a weak model on seed data, use it to write instructions for existing high-quality content, finetune on that, repeat. For long context: split long documents into chunks under the current limit (say 8K), generate (question, answer) pairs per chunk, then train with the whole document as context.

8

Verifying synthetic data, and its limits

Check synthetic data like any AI output: functional correctness where possible (which is why code is the most commonly synthesized data: it can be executed) and AI judges otherwise, scoring 1 to 5 or classifying good vs bad. Other tools: factual-consistency checks, a classifier that tells synthetic from real (a well-built example should fool it), topic detection to drop off-topic examples, and heuristics (drop repeats, too-long or too-short instructions, identical instructions with different responses).

9

Model distillation

In distillation a small student learns to mimic a large teacher, usually from the teacher's outputs. DistilBERT was trained from scratch to be 40% smaller while keeping 97% of BERT's language understanding and running 60% faster. Alpaca finetuned Llama-7B on text-davinci-003 outputs and behaved similarly, at 4% of the teacher's size. Synthetic instruction data is often paired with LoRA.

Not all synthetic training is distillationDistillation implies the teacher is the gold standard. You can also train a student that ends up better than the data's source (as with reverse-instruction bootstrapping). And check licences: many forbid using outputs to train competing models. The Llama 3 team noted that training indiscriminately on a model's own generated data doesn't help and can hurt; with verification, using only checked synthetic data, they kept improving a model on its own output.
10

Processing: inspect, dedupe, clean, format

Process in whatever order saves compute (for example, run cheap deduplication before expensive cleaning), try scripts on a subset first, and don't modify data in place: keep the original.

1 · Inspect

Plot token, input-length and response-length distributions; break them down by source, time and annotator. A Microsoft team compared (verb, direct-object noun) pairs and response lengths between GPT-4 and GPT-3 generated data: GPT-4's were more diverse and longer. Check inter-annotator disagreement. Then look at examples manually: in the author's experience 15 minutes of staring at data can save hours of headaches, and Greg Brockman called manual inspection the activity with the highest value-to-prestige ratio in ML.

2 · Deduplicate

word-set similarity, toy data

Duplicates skew the distribution and can leak test data into training. They come at many levels: whole documents, inside documents, across documents. Methods: pairwise comparison (exact, n-gram, fuzzy or semantic; expensive), hashing into buckets (MinHash, Bloom filters), or reducing dimensions first.

3 · Clean and filter

Strip extraneous formatting (HTML tags, Markdown). Databricks found removing extra Markdown and HTML tokens improved accuracy by 20% while cutting input length by 60%. Remove non-compliant data (PII, sensitive or copyrighted content, toxic data) and low-quality data. If you have more data than you can afford, use active learning or importance sampling to keep the most useful examples.
