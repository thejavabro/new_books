The book's closing argument

Security and reliability overlap heavily in their technologies and practices. They are inherent properties of a system, so they belong to everyone in the lifecycle, not to separate departments. As more of the world (remote surgery, research drones, robots at disaster sites) depends on connected systems, trust rests on both. Tap the threads the authors pull together.

Appendix A: a disaster risk assessment matrix

Rank the risks you face by probability × impact, both on a 0 to 1 scale, then plan for the highest first. Values depend on what you do and where you are: an office in Los Angeles is likelier to see an earthquake than one in Hamburg, so multi-site organizations may want one matrix per location. The risk list below follows the appendix; the starting values are only examples. Adjust them for your organization.
