Understanding Adversaries

Every attack traces back to a motivated person. Knowing who might come for your system, and why, tells you which defenses to build first.

Why people attack

Adversaries are people, so start with their reasons. The chapter lists eight motivations. One attacker can hold several at once: a single government operator has been accused of ransomware for money, an attack meant to coerce a film studio, and intrusions into power utilities.

3

Insiders: where security and reliability meet

Anyone with trusted access is an insider: employees and executives (first-party), outside developers, open source contributors and partners (third-party), and even the family member who borrows a work laptop (related). Most outages are also caused by insiders, by mistake. So the same controls protect against both.

Insider threat brainstorm deck

The chapter suggests mixing an actor, a motive, an action and a target, and asking "could this happen here?" It works as a quick card game.

5

Walk an attack down the kill chain

Frameworks such as the Cyber Kill Chain and MITRE ATT&CK break an attack into stages so you can line defenses up against each one. Turn defenses on and run the attack. You only need to break the chain once, but it is safer to have more than one chance.

6

Study methods, not just names

Threat intelligence (written reports, indicators of compromise, malware analysis) tells you how real attackers behave. Tap the four reminders the chapter gives for risk assessment.
