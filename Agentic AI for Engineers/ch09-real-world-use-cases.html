Real-World Use Cases

Same technology, very different jobs. In finance agents bring speed and vigilance, in healthcare they must move cautiously, in factories they buy flexibility, and in public services they must explain themselves. This chapter tours the domains, the ways each can go wrong, and how to pick a use case that is actually worth an agent.

From text generators to physical agents

Older models were only as good as their labelled examples. LLMs generalise, and each step since has added a capability. Step along the path.

2

AI agents vs agentic AI

Chaining agents is not enough. A fixed assembly line (transcribe → translate → summarise) is still a set of narrow AI agents. It becomes agentic when something reasons about what to do next, picks the sequence and loops back when results fall short. Run both on "summarise recent battery research".

5

Healthcare: suggest, flag, escalate

A triage assistant cross-checks symptoms, vitals and history and suggests a level. A nurse confirms every score. When the agent is unsure, the safe default is to escalate, not to guess. Set the confidence threshold and send patients through.

6

Finance: continuous vigilance, with brakes

Rules catch known fraud; agents can reason about new patterns. High confidence freezes the payment for review, medium confidence alerts the fraud team, low passes. Move the freeze threshold and watch the stream.

7

When many fast agents react to the same signal

Several trading agents see the same dip and all sell, which deepens the dip, which triggers more selling. A circuit breaker and position limits break the loop.

8

Manufacturing: fix it before it breaks

Sensors stream vibration, heat and power. A maintenance agent spots drift days before failure, books a service window and orders the part. Choose what the agent is told to optimise.

9

Customer service: a copilot beside the human

While the customer talks, the copilot pulls policy, checks history and drafts a reply. The human handles tone and judgment, and the agent knows its limits. Play the call.

10

Cybersecurity: vigilance without the alert storm

An overeager detector blocks legitimate work and buries analysts in alerts, so the real attack gets dismissed as another false alarm. Tune the sensitivity and see what the analyst actually reads.

11

Public sector: no voice lost, every decision explained

Governments receive thousands of consultation responses. An agent clusters them into themes, quotes representative voices, and hands experts an auditable summary to check before any policy move. Cluster the responses.

12

Systematic errors are worse than random ones

Different people make different mistakes. An algorithm makes the same mistake every time the pattern appears. Compare 40 chest-pain cases.

13

Is this a job for an agent?

Ask four questions of a candidate process. If it is predictable end to end, ordinary automation is cheaper and safer. Pick a candidate or answer the questions yourself.
