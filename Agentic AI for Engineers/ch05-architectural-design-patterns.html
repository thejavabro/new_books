Architectural Design Patterns

A brilliant model with no structure is a freelancer with no manager. Architecture is what makes it dependable. This chapter separates patterns (how one agent thinks) from topologies (how several agents are wired), walks through each with live examples, and ends with the production plumbing where real failures happen.

Function, tool, agent

The same request, "help me prepare for a client pitch", handled at three stages of maturity. Watch how much of the work moves from you to the system.

2

Two separate design choices

Patterns describe what happens inside one agent's head: ReAct, reflection, plan-execute-reflect. Topologies describe the org chart: pipeline, supervisor and workers, hybrid, parallel. They are independent, so you pick one of each. Click a cell.

Pick a pattern × topology combination.

3

The single-agent loop, with and without memory

The simplest agent perceives, reasons, acts, repeats. Stateless, it is fast and predictable but starts from scratch every time. Add short-term memory (this run's facts) and long-term memory (verified knowledge kept across runs) and it gets better with use. Ask the same tutor agent twice.

4

Planner, executor, reflector

A jazz trio instead of a soloist. The planner writes the score, the executor plays it with tools, the reflector checks every result before the next step, and a router decides whether to continue or synthesize. Step through a research question; switch on "bad search result" to see the reflector earn its keep.

5

Tool-augmented reasoning: ReAct with retrieval

The observation step is the magic: it grounds the next thought in evidence instead of letting the model spin an answer from memory. A billing question answered with a retrieval tool over policy docs plus a usage-log API.

6

Four topologies, animated

Topology is who talks to whom, in what order, with what authority. An orchestrator above the wiring routes artifacts, schedules parallel work, enforces contracts and leaves a replayable trace.

7

Inside the hybrid: one request, three governed pipelines

"Update my direct deposit, add my spouse to health benefits, and my laptop is overheating." The controller fans out three pipelines under one budget. Only the payroll commit step can write, and only with approval. Break a system and watch the circuit breaker.

8

Start simple, grow on evidence

Single agent first. Add a reflector when answers wobble. Add a planner when tasks have many tool-driven steps. Go multi-agent only for genuinely separate expertise, parallel work or strict governance boundaries. Answer the questions.

Most failures happen around the model, not inside its reasoning. Four drills.
