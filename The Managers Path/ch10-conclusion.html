1

Manage yourself first

The book ends where every level of management starts: to manage others well, you have to manage yourself. Know how you react, what inspires you, and what drives you crazy. Everything else is built on that.

2

Take your ego out of the story

Great managers are good at working through conflict, and that means seeing past the story you're telling yourself. People drawn to management often have strong views; that decisiveness helps, until you forget your interpretation is only an interpretation. Try separating story from facts.

Tools the author found helpful: a meditation practice for noticing your own reactions, and a daily habit of free writing that always ends with two words, "Get curious."

3

The path, in one picture

From being managed to running an organization. Each step adds scope, and each one asks you to let go of something that made you successful at the step before. Click a step to revisit it.

4

Get curious

The closing advice: look for the other side of the story. What is the other person trying to do? What do they value? What do they need? Then apply that same curiosity everywhere.

Ask questions, and be willing to be proven wrong.
