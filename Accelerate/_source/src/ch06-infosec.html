DevOps is a poor name in one way: it leaves out testing, product management and information security. Security teams are often tiny and called in only at the end, when changes are painful and expensive. One industry estimate for large companies:

Many developers also don't know common risks such as the OWASP Top 10, or how to prevent them. One reviewer at the end can't catch it all.

Picture the delivery lifecycle as a line from design on the left to production on the right. Shifting left means moving security from a separate gate at the right end into every step. Drag security along the line.

Infosec teams simply don't have the capacity to review every change when deployments are frequent. Increase the deploy rate and compare a review gate with security built into the team's daily work.

Teams that shift left do better at continuous delivery, which in turn improves delivery performance. And security itself improves: high performers spent half as much time remediating security issues as low performers.

US federal systems must follow a Risk Management Framework: document how hundreds of security controls are implemented, then get it assessed, a process that can take months to over a year and often starts only when the system is "dev complete".
