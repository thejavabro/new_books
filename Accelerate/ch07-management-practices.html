Management Practices for Software

Lean management works when its practices are used together. And the heavyweight change approval board, the classic safety net, turns out to slow teams down without making anything safer.

From project management to Lean

Software management has cycled through several schools of thought. This chapter tests ideas from the Lean movement, which grew out of Toyota's approach to building many kinds of car for a small market, and its relentless improvement. Mary and Tom Poppendieck adapted it to software.

2

Lean management: the pieces work together

The research models Lean management with three components (plus lightweight change approval, later in this chapter). The interesting result: WIP limits alone don't strongly predict delivery performance. The effect appears when they're combined. Switch components on and off.

4

Visual displays and monitoring

For displays, the survey asked what information is shown, how widely it's shared and how easy it is to get. Visibility and the communication it enables are the point. For monitoring, the bar is higher than paging people: data from application and infrastructure monitoring should inform business decisions every day.

5

Change approval: the surprising result

Every organization has some way of approving production changes, from "hey, look at this" in a startup to a change advisory board (CAB) taking days or weeks. The survey compared four approaches. Pick one.

Risk management theaterCan an outside board, unfamiliar with a system's internals, review tens of thousands of lines changed by hundreds of engineers and judge the impact on a complex production system? Every developer has made an innocent-looking change that broke things. Box-ticking lets people say "we followed the process" afterwards; meanwhile it adds delays and handoffs.
6

Segregation of duties without a CAB

Regulations such as PCI DSS, and auditors, often require that the person who writes a change isn't the only one who approves it. That doesn't need a CAB or a separate ops team. Two mechanisms satisfy the letter and the spirit. Step through.

The right role for people outside teams is governance, not inspection: monitor delivery performance and help teams adopt practices known to improve stability, quality and speed.

Recap on one page

Three componentsWIP limits that drive improvement, visual displays of quality and work, monitoring that informs daily decisions.
Together, not aloneWIP limits alone barely move the needle; combined with displays and monitoring, the effect is strong.
PayoffsBetter delivery performance, a more generative culture and less burnout.
External approval hurtsCAB or manager sign-off slows lead time, deploys and recovery, with no gain in stability. Worse than nothing.
Do this insteadPeer review (pairing, intrateam code review) plus a deployment pipeline that rejects bad changes.
Auditors are satisfiedRecorded peer approval plus automated deployment gives a complete trail.
