1

Why organizations must accelerate

Banks no longer win by guarding gold in vaults, and shops no longer win on shelf space alone. They win on fast, safe, delightful software. The book names four things every organization has to speed up, and software sits under all of them. Tap a spoke.

Leaders think they're further along than they areIndustry surveys found executives rate their DevOps progress higher than the people doing the work. Two lessons follow: there is more untapped value than leaders realize, and capabilities need to be measured, not guessed, and the results shared upward.

2

Focus on capabilities, not maturity

Maturity models picture improvement as a staircase you climb once. Capability models picture it as a set of dials you keep turning. The book is blunt: maturity models are the wrong tool. Step through the four reasons.

3

What does (and doesn't) predict performance

Vendors push the capabilities their products cover; consultants push what they know. Evidence cuts through that. Some popular explanations for why a team is fast or slow turned out to predict nothing at all. Guess before you peek.

5

24 capabilities, five groups

The research found 24 capabilities that measurably improve delivery performance and, through it, organizational performance. They are easy to define, measure and improve, and they work for every kind of organization the study saw: startups and enterprises, greenfield and mainframe, regulated and not. They group into five families; each is a chapter in this companion.

Recap on one page

Software is the engineDelivery, market engagement, compliance and risk response all depend on it, in every industry.
Capabilities beat maturityContinuous, multidimensional, outcome-based and dynamic, where maturity models are a finish line.

Context doesn't excuse youApp age, who deploys and having a change board don't predict performance. Capabilities do.
