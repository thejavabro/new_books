High performers have more loyal people

People are at the heart of every transformation, and hiring, keeping and engaging them matters more as pressure to deliver grows. To measure loyalty, the team borrowed a well-studied customer measure, Net Promoter Score, and asked it about workplaces.

Low-performing teams: promoters
High-performing teams: promoters

Other research links engagement to business results: companies with highly engaged workers grew revenue 2.5 times as much, and stocks of high-trust workplaces beat market indexes threefold from 1997 to 2011.

2

How Net Promoter Score works

One question, "How likely are you to recommend…?", answered 0 to 10. 9–10 are promoters, 7–8 passives, 0–6 detractors. NPS is the percentage of promoters minus the percentage of detractors. Drag the bars to shape your team's answers.

What eNPS moved withEmployee NPS correlated significantly with: how much the organization collects customer feedback and uses it in design; how well teams can see the flow of work all the way to the customer; and how strongly people identify with the organization's values and goals. Seeing your work's impact on customers builds loyalty.
3

Identity: do people make the organization's goals their own?

The team added a measure of how strongly people identify with their organization. Score your own view; average the items for a single identity score.

The hypothesis: teams practising CD and experimenting with products build better products and feel more connected to their organization. They did. Identity in turn predicted a generative culture and organizational performance. And because identity includes values alignment, it may also reduce burnout (chapter 9). Contrast the old model: requirements handed down, big batches, little control, little connection.

"I hired them from you!"Asked by a Fortune 500 leader where Netflix found its amazing people, cloud architect Adrian Cockcroft answered that he'd hired them from that leader's company. Same people, different environment.
4

Job satisfaction and the right tools

Job satisfaction here means: being satisfied with your work, having the tools and resources to do it, and a job that uses your skills and abilities. Good DevOps technical practices predict it. Why? Automation hands computers what they're good at, rote tasks, and leaves people what they're good at: weighing evidence, thinking through problems, deciding. Sort these tasks.

Diversity: what the survey found

Teams that are more diverse in gender and underrepresented minorities are smarter, perform better and achieve better business outcomes, according to other research. The survey found few diverse teams.

Women among respondents: 5% in 2015, 6% in 2016, 6.5% in 2017. Women leave tech at a 45% higher rate than men, and there is no difference in ability or aptitude for STEM.

Diversity isn't enough without inclusionAn inclusive organization is one where everyone feels welcome and valued for who they are and what they bring, with a shared sense of belonging and purpose. Harassment, microaggressions and unequal pay are things leaders and peers can actively watch for and fix.
