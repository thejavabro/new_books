Delegation: Learn to Let Go

The arithmetic of letting go

Monday morning: an urgent request for a new operations dashboard lands on top of deadlines and performance reviews. Delegation is the way out. Delegation can be planned (chosen ahead of time as a growth opportunity) or unplanned (an emergency hand-off). Either way, it starts with simple arithmetic. Doing ten similar tasks yourself takes ten days. Teaching someone takes, say, three days of knowledge transfer plus a day of shadowing. Adjust the numbers.

Delegation, allocation and substitution

All three share work, but differ in support, ownership and accountability. Allocation is "assign and forget": do this, by then. Delegation hands over the task, the authority it needs and a chance to learn, while you stay accountable and check in. Substitution replaces you entirely: you are no longer accountable. What to delegate, and to whom

Not every task should be handed off. Keep anything highly critical or confidential (C-suite reports, performance reviews, HR matters) and anything that needs your unique perspective. Be careful near hard deadlines on an understaffed team. Good candidates are repetitive work you can templatize, tasks that fit someone's expertise, and work that stretches someone towards their goals. Tap each task to sort it.

Tap a task.

Now choose who. Use what you learned in one-on-ones about motivation and goals, and spread opportunities fairly rather than always picking the top performer. Who should get "own the onboarding of the next new hire"?

4

A framework for delegation

The chapter's framework runs why → what → who → how. The first meeting is a "sales call": explain why this person, how the task links to team goals and their career, and what support they'll have. Then make sure they can play it back to you. Step through.

Before you walk away, checkOutcome clear? Enough authority handed over? Success criteria stated? Do they feel supported? Follow-ups on agreed milestones? Constructive feedback and recognition given? What will you start, stop or continue next time?

Teaching others to delegate

As you grow, your senior people need to delegate too. The author coached a newly promoted staff engineer who was drowning: she handed meeting facilitation to a senior engineer, who then grew by passing on work of their own. Each layer that learns to delegate multiplies capacity. Press to add a layer.

When delegation goes wrong

Taking the task back ("upward delegation") is rarely the answer. Work through the problem together. Tap a failure mode for the fix.
