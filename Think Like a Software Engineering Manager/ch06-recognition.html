Rewards and Recognition

Recognition is cheap to give and expensive to forget. Who gives it, how quickly, and how specifically all change what it does for a team.

Why recognition matters

Six months into her first EM role, the author realised she had never properly thanked her team. Once she started (personal notes, team outings) engagement rose. Recognition is about more than rewards: it builds belonging and purpose, and keeps scarce engineering talent. Timing matters as much as size. Compare a year with one big end-of-year award against a year of small, timely acknowledgements. The curve is illustrative.

Timely, frequent, specific, visible, fair

Ideas for frequent recognitionDid someone just finish onboarding, start a project, hit a milestone, ship something important, or reach a work anniversary? A monthly post celebrating, say, the best new tests can motivate juniors and chip away at tech debt. Just don't overdo it until praise feels automatic.

Forms of recognition

Group recognition celebrates shared milestones: launch parties (even snacks in a room, with cross-functional partners invited), offsites each quarter or half, project swag (one 50-person virtual team across seven teams got its own), challenge coins for phases of big projects, and remote-friendly events like virtual escape rooms or a launch party with food delivery vouchers. Individual recognition can be public (shout-out channels, recognition tools, all-hands) or private, monetary (raises, spot bonuses, points, learning subscriptions) or not (SME status, more responsibility, representing the team, an email that copies leadership).

Ask people early, in one-on-ones, how they like to be recognized. Pick a form for each teammate.
