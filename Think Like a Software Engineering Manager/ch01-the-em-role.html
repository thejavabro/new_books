Exploring the Engineering Manager Role

Engineering manager = engineer + manager. The job is building talent, setting vision, leading projects and raising the bar, and it is judged by business results, delivery speed, morale and operational health.

1

Engineer + manager, and why the role exists

The book opens with a simple sum: engineering manager = engineer + manager. Titles vary (software development manager, SRE manager, senior manager of engineering), but the job is the same: the EM is accountable for both the team and what it ships. In a tiny startup, engineers report straight to the CTO. As the company grows that stops working, and the gaps show up as friction.

The chapter's opening thought experiment: six engineers each finish two tasks a day. Better code reviews, training and cross-training lift each person to three. Nobody worked longer; the system around them improved. Drag the sliders.

Now remove the EM from a growing company. Each card is a friction point the chapter lists. Tap one to see what goes wrong.

2

Five core competencies

Strip the job down and the EM is responsible for "executing the team and the projects". The chapter splits that into five competencies. Click a segment of the wheel.

Ownership, not blameLeading projects means making decisions with partial information and owning them whether they go well or not. The chapter points to the idea of extreme ownership: take responsibility for everything that affects the mission, and be humble without being passive.
3

Leadership versus management

People use the two words interchangeably, but they pull in different directions. Management runs today's machinery. Leadership is a superset: the operational work plus the emotional and strategic side. The best EMs do both. Focus one column at a time.

      How an EM's success is measured

      "You can't improve what you don't measure." The chapter names four areas, and they are linked: a change in one moves the others. Click an area to see what it measures and what it pushes on.

      For development velocity the chapter leans on the four DORA metrics. Classify these readings from a team's dashboard: which DORA metric is each one?

      Watch outLines of code and raw story counts mislead. Story points are a planning aid for the team, not a way to rank individuals. The book's favourite test of success: the team keeps running smoothly while the EM is on vacation.

      Six leadership styles, and choosing yours

      Six fictional EMs, one per style: Alissa (autocratic), Brayden (democratic), Charlie (delegative), Dave (transactional), Eric (transformational) and Frankie (servant). None is always right. Pick a situation and see which styles fit (green) and which carry risk (red). Tap a style to read about it.

      Hybrid is normalYour style depends on you (experience, personality), the team (size, seniority, how they see you), the project phase and the company. Expect to blend styles and to drop one that isn't working.
