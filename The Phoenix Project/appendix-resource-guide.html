Why do DevOps? The numbers

The guide cites the 2012 Puppet Labs "State of DevOps" survey of 4,039 IT organisations. High performers weren't trading speed for stability; they had both.

30×more frequent code deployments
8,000×faster lead time from commit to running in production
2×the change success rate
12×faster mean time to restore service

Speed and stability together

The 2014 study added that high performers were twice as likely to exceed profitability, market share and productivity goals. And the bar keeps rising: ten deploys a day counted as fast in 2009, while by 2012 Amazon reported an average of 23,000 a day.

The manufacturing parallelIn the 1980s, manufacturers fought a chronic conflict between keeping lots of inventory to protect sales and cutting inventory to control costs. Lean practices broke it: average lead times fell from six weeks with under 70% of orders on time to under three weeks with over 95% on time by 2005. The guide argues IT is living through the same shift, with Dev and Ops in the roles of sales and the plant.

The guide also paints the day-to-day picture: deployments become boring, code reaches production months before launch hidden behind a feature toggle, launches are just flipping that toggle for a growing share of customers, and Development spends about 20% of its time making sure work flows smoothly downstream.
