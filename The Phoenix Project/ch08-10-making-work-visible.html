The story so far

Plot, in brief

Bill spends a weekend on slides asking Steve for six more people. He gets thirty minutes, half of them taken by Sarah, and a flat no. Back in the change room, the call for change requests has produced hundreds of index cards. Bill, Wes and Patty decide what deserves scrutiny and what doesn't.

A credit-card outage hits every store. On the bridge call every team insists it changed nothing, and then Brent quietly fixes something without telling anyone. Bill lays down rules for incidents. At the next CAB, approvals take seconds, and a glance at the board shows nearly half the week's changes piled onto Friday, launch day. Bill then spends an afternoon watching Brent work and sees why everything waits on him.

Not every change needs a committee

Faced with a table of cards, the team doesn't review them all. They apply the 80/20 rule: a small share of changes carries most of the risk. They list the fragile systems (the ones that crash when touched, such as an old app nobody dares patch), require a real review for those, ask medium-risk changes to talk to the people affected, and pre-approve routine ones. Sort these cards the same way.

3

A board that shows a collision before it happens

Once every change is on a card and every card is on a calendar, a problem nobody could see becomes obvious: 173 changes are scheduled for Friday, the same day Phoenix deploys. Patty asks, like air traffic control, who will change their flight plan. Try it.

Friday also has the fewest people free, because everyone is needed for Phoenix.

A week later, it pays offAn engineer reports that two groups were about to change the same database and application servers at the same time. One of them spotted it on the change wall and they sequenced the work. Prevention is quiet: you rarely see the disaster you avoided.
4

Running an outage like a drill, not a mob

The credit-card outage shows the old habits: no list of recent changes, teams defending themselves, and a fix applied silently by the one person who knew where to look. It worked, by luck, and nobody learned what broke. Bill's rules: the change list must be at hand for every major incident, any action during an outage is announced and discussed before it's taken, and the team practises with fire drills every two weeks.

    A day in the life of Brent

    Bill sits near Brent's desk and watches. Brent is good and wants to help. That is exactly the problem: people call him directly instead of opening tickets, he drops what he's doing to solve their issue, and the Phoenix tasks wait.     Protecting the person everything depends on

    "Processes are supposed to protect people," Bill says. The team designs a set of measures. They're half carrot, half stick, and they all aim at one thing: stop pulling Brent into work only he can do. Tap each one.
