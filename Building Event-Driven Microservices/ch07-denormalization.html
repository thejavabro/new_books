Denormalization and Eventification

Raw table rows make poor events. Eventification joins and reshapes them into the business entities consumers actually want, either at the outbox or in a dedicated service.

1

Relational streams make every consumer join

Relational databases resolve relationships at query time, with all the data at hand. Event-driven services work best when everything they need is inside the event. Streaming joins exist, but they are harder, slower and pricier than joins over static tables. CDC from a normalized schema hands every consumer the same join homework.

3
Denormalize, and every change must flow through

  
If you copy merchant data into each enriched item, then when the merchant changes, every one of its item events must be re-emitted. Otherwise the stream stops reflecting reality, permanently.

  

    
    

    

    

  






  
4
What goes in the event, and what stays out?

  
Two factors dominate: frequency of change and size. Inventory can change constantly (picture a Black Friday sale); the top 100 reviews are large. Join either into the item and every change re-emits the whole enriched item to consumers who may not care. Join both and frequency multiplies size. Choose what to include.

  

    

    
    

    

  

  
Event and data bars use a log scale. Illustrative sizes: item and merchant fields ~0.5 KB, reviews ~40 KB. The producer could also batch inventory into periodic updates (every 5 or 30 seconds) at the cost of latency. The book's resulting design keeps merchant data in and leaves inventory and reviews out for consumers to join if they want.






  
5
Slowly changing dimensions

  
A merchant's name or status rarely changes, but it can, at any moment. Analytics calls this a slowly changing dimension. Of the many SCD types, two matter for streams. Toggle the premium status and watch the events.
