Designing Events

The shape of an event decides how hard it is to consume. State events carry the whole picture; delta events carry only the change. Choosing well saves every consumer a lot of pain.

1

Two basic kinds: state and delta

Every change has three moments: an initial state, the delta that changes it, and the final state (which becomes the next initial state). Events model either the level or the edge.

2

State events and event-carried state transfer

A state event's key identifies the entity; its value carries the entity's whole public state, like a database row. Think Item, Order, User; Truck, Package, Driver. They give you ECST (state carried to every consumer), easy materialization and strong decoupling from the producer's internal logic. And consumers can still see what changed by comparing with the previous event for the key. Edit the item and publish.

3

Current state, or before and after?

Change-data capture tools often emit before/after events. They show a transition in one event, but they cost more than they give. Compare how a user moving country, then being deleted, looks in each style.


  

  
The verdictCurrent-state events win for streams: lean, simple, compactable (a compacted stream holds about one event per key), and they hide why things changed, so nobody couples to the producer's internal transitions. Consumers keep the slice of state they care about to detect changes; disk is cheap.






  
4
Deltas inside a domain: event sourcing

  
Delta events describe a change, named as a past-tense verb: itemAddedToCart, orderShipped, userMoved. Event sourcing records every delta in an append-only log and rebuilds state by applying them in order, an alternative to CRUD, which overwrites state in place. Step through the book's refrigerator idea both ways.

  

  

    
Why people choose itCRUD hits the store on every call, which slows under load, and concurrent writes to the same entity conflict. Event sourcing appends fast, and writing and aggregating scale separately.

    
What it costsIt is eventually consistent, and with concurrent writers you can't tie the final state to your own append. Running it over a remote broker adds latency and a dependency. CRUD gives read-after-write consistency and is familiar.

  

  
The real danger is exposing those private deltas to other domains. Any event you expose becomes part of a public contract.

6
Deltas inside, state across boundaries

  
Use deltas within a bounded context, for event sourcing or between tightly coupled services one team owns. Publish state for everyone else. Tap a context.

  

    

    

  

  
Arguments you'll hear"Duplicate state wastes disk." "Consumers will know what it means." "I only care about one transition, just publish a custom event for me." Disk is cheap; engineers fixing tangled delta coupling are not.






  
7
Hybrid events: state with a hint of why

  
A hybrid is a state event carrying a bit of delta, such as how a user signed up. It saves four separate sign-up events, but exposes a private detail that can drift in meaning. Ship the mobile app and see which consumers survive.

  

    

    

    

  

  
Risk grows with the complexity of the delta and how likely its meaning is to change. Sign-up method is immutable and simple, so the risk is low but not zero. Document the enum in the schema, and remember it's now in your data contract.






  
8
Measurement events

  
A measurement is a complete record of an occurrence at a moment: an ad view, a click, a temperature reading. They feed aggregations ("most viewed page?"), they may be lossy (ad blockers eat web analytics), and they can drive time-sensitive reactions. Whether old readings matter depends on the use.

The author recalls collecting crash and dropped-call measurements from developer BlackBerry devices: a strict schema saved endless post-processing pain, and completeness mattered more than speed, since reports could arrive weeks late from devices that had been offline.






  
9
Notification events and the race they lose

  
A notification says "something happened, look here" with a link. Fine for a phone buzz. As a way to share state it fails, because the resource behind the link keeps changing. An order goes SOLD → PARTIAL_RETURN → FULL_RETURN. Your consumer is lagging.

  

    
    

    

  

  
It also doubles the producer's job: publish events and run a query API with access control and scaling. Just put the state in the event.






  
10
Designing for the right to be forgotten

  
GDPR Article 17 gives people the right to have personal data deleted without undue delay. Streams are immutable. The design answer: encrypt sensitive data, whole events or chosen fields, with a key per user (or account) held in a key-management service. To forget someone, delete their key. That's crypto-shredding; check with your lawyers that it satisfies your obligations.
