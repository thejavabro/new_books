Basic Producer and Consumer Microservices

The simplest event-driven service is just a loop over a consumer client. It is a good fit more often than you might think, and it has clear limits.

1

The basic producer/consumer (BPC)

A BPC microservice uses only plain consumer and producer clients: consume, apply logic, produce. Clients exist in nearly every language, the whole bounded context lives in one simple application, and it containerizes easily. What you don't get is everything frameworks add. You could build those yourself, but that's a big job, so check the fit first.

Because moving local state between instances is hard without a framework, BPCs usually use external state stores, which give every instance uniform access plus backup and recovery.

2

Integrating legacy systems with a sidecar

A legacy shop frontend gets product and inventory data by a scheduled batch copy from another monolith's replica. You can't safely touch its code. A sidecar BPC, deployed as part of the same unit (a Kubernetes sidecar, or even the same container), consumes the Product Info and Product Inventory streams and upserts them into the frontend's data store.

3

Order doesn't matter, completeness does: gating

A book can go to the printer only once its contents, cover art and pricing all exist, in any order. Each incoming event is materialized into its table and then looks up the others. When all three are present, the book is released. No event scheduling needed, which makes it a natural BPC job. Click to produce events.

A human approval can be one of the gates too (Chapter 17's newspaper example).

4

When the data store does the heavy lifting

If the hard part lives in a specialized store or model, the service just shuttles events in and results out.

Product classificationScraped products stream in; for each, the BPC asks a batch-trained machine-learning categorizer and emits the category. Training happens offline; classification is a per-event lookup.
Nearest retailersUser behaviour events are matched against a geospatial store to pick nearby retailers' ads.
5

Scale processing and storage separately

A service aggregates each user's views into 24-hour sessions (key: user and timestamp, value: product IDs) in an external key-value store, emitting and flushing each finished session. Traffic follows people's sleep cycle; the set of users who might log in doesn't. Drag through the day.
