Principles of microservice deployment

A few dozen services can survive with bespoke deployments. Beyond that you need a standard process. Tap each principle.

A warning signIf deploying one service regularly forces other teams to deploy in lockstep, your bounded contexts are probably wrong. The exception is a few tightly coupled services inside one context, owned by one team.

CI, continuous delivery and continuous deployment

CI merges changes, then builds, tests, lints and checks schemas, and produces a deployable container. Continuous delivery keeps the build deployable, but a person presses go. Continuous deployment ships automatically. That is hard for stateful services, where a deployment may mean rebuilding state. The CMS takes the container from the registry and runs it on commodity hardware, with special pools for GPUs or fast disks. Rolling update

Only the deploy step changes: replace one instance at a time, so processing never stops. Old and new logic run side by side while it happens. Prerequisites: no breaking change to state stores, internal topology or internal schemas, and no replay of inputs. Changing the topology by accident is the classic mistake. Good for consuming new optional fields, adding an input stream, or bug fixes that don't need reprocessing.

Blue-green deployment

Mostly used for request-response services. Bring up the new copy (blue) with its own data store, consumer group and addresses. Let it catch up on its input streams, then shift traffic over gradually while you watch errors, lag, resource use and autoscaling. Keep the old copy (green) idling so you can switch back instantly.

Careful with a shared output streamIf both blue and green write to the same output stream, they can overwrite each other or emit duplicates. Use a rolling update or a full stop instead.
