Reliability

How sure can a producer be that a message is safe, and how sure can a consumer be that it sees each one once? Acknowledgments, in-sync replicas and transactions are the dials.

1

Durability, availability, consistency

Reliability breaks into three goals. Durability: data, once stored, stays stored. Availability: producers can write and consumers can read, even when something breaks. Consistency: the data in the cluster is exactly what was intended, no gaps, no duplicates, right order. Replication buys the first two fairly easily. Consistency is harder, and Kafka offers three tools for it: acknowledgments, idempotent writes and transactions. Sometimes you must choose: keep writing during a bad failure, or refuse writes to stay correct?

2

Acknowledgments and min.insync.replicas

TCP confirms that bytes arrived, not that the application stored them, so Kafka has its own acknowledgment set by the producer. acks=0 is like UDP: fire and forget. acks=1 waits for the leader. acks=all (same as -1, the default since Kafka 3.0) waits until every in-sync replica has the message. The topic setting min.insync.replicas adds a floor: with acks=all, the leader refuses writes if fewer replicas are in sync. Set it up and send a message.

Brokers up (broker 1 leads):

The authors' defaultReplication factor 3 with min.insync.replicas=2 and acks=all: one broker can be down and writes continue safely. If you need to lose two brokers and still write, use replication factor 4 with min ISR 2. Leave min ISR at 1 and acks=all silently behaves like acks=1 when only the leader is left.
3

At most once, at least once, exactly once

What if the message was stored but the acknowledgment got lost on the way back? The producer must assume failure and resend, so the log now holds it twice. Fine for a temperature reading; a disaster for a bank transfer. Step through the three guarantees.

4

Transactions: all partitions or none

Idempotence protects one partition. But a consumer that reads, processes and writes elsewhere touches several partitions, and committing an offset is itself a write. Bob sends Alice 10: debit Bob and credit Alice must both happen or neither. Kafka transactions write to several partitions atomically using a variant of two-phase commit: like reserving a hotel room with a call-back deadline, booking the flight, then confirming or cancelling the room. A transaction coordinator broker writes commit or abort markers; aborted messages stay in the log but are skipped.

Overhead is per partition per transaction, not per message. When transactions were introduced it was measured at about 3% for 100 ms transactions and roughly 3 ms (30%) for 10 ms ones. The bar uses that rough rule.

5

Followers, failover and preferred leaders

Kafka pushes work to clients wherever it can. To the leader, followers are just consumers with a special fetch request; the offsets they report tell the leader who has what. Step through a broker failure and return.

Unclean leader election: last resort onlyIf no in-sync replica is left, the partition stays offline. kafka-leader-election.sh --election-type unclean (or unclean.leader.election.enable) lets an out-of-sync replica lead, which loses whatever it missed. The authors strongly advise against enabling it on the broker; in a real emergency, use it for specific topics only.
