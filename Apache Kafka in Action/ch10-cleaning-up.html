Cleaning Up Messages

A log can not grow forever. Kafka either drops old segments (retention) or keeps only the newest value per key (compaction), and tombstones let keys disappear for good.

1

Why delete anything at all?

Keeping every message forever sounds attractive until you hit three walls. Storage: the log grows without end. Performance: the bigger the log, the longer a new consumer needs to read it, much of it irrelevant. And most important, need and law: data you no longer need or may no longer keep must go, while data you still need must not vanish by accident. Kafka has two tools, set per topic with cleanup.policy (cluster default log.cleanup.policy=delete), and they can be combined (delete,compact).

delete: log retention

Drop whole segments once they are old enough (or the partition is too big). Cheap to run; ideal for privacy deadlines, sensor readings, application logs, or plain messaging. But it can't tell important from unimportant: old means gone.

compact: log compaction

Keep at least the newest message for every key; drop older ones with the same key. Ideal for "current state" data like customer addresses. Requires keys, and costs more because the cleaner must scan the log.

2

Retention works on whole segments

Two triggers: retention.bytes (partition size, off by default with -1) and retention.ms (7 days). The catch: a segment is deleted only when its newest message is older than the retention period, and the active segment keeps receiving messages. So the real maximum age is about segment.ms + retention.ms plus the check interval (log.retention.check.interval.ms, 5 minutes). Suppose regulation says no message may live longer than 7 days:

Compaction lab

Compacted topics reject messages without a key (InvalidRecordException). Produce price updates with keys, then let the cleaner run. Watch three rules from the chapter: the active segment is never compacted; offsets and order are preserved (gaps appear, and a read at a missing offset just returns the next one); and a tombstone (null value) deletes a key for good after delete.retention.ms (1 day).

4

When does the cleaner compact?

The cleaner wakes every 15 seconds (log.cleaner.backoff.ms) but doesn't scan everything every time. A partition qualifies if it has uncompacted messages older than max.compaction.lag.ms (default: practically never), or if its dirty ratio (never-compacted part of the log divided by the whole) is at least min.cleanable.dirty.ratio (0.5) and those messages are older than min.compaction.lag.ms (0). The dirty ratio also decides which partition goes first. Lower ratios save disk but keep brokers busy reading whole partitions.
