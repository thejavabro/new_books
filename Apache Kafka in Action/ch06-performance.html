Performance

Throughput and latency pull in different directions. Partition counts, producer batching, compression and consumer fetch settings decide where a cluster sits between the two.

1

Bandwidth, latency, and resources

"Fast" means different things. Bandwidth is bytes per second. Latency is how long one message takes end to end, and users usually feel latency far more. The authors' joke makes the point: a truck full of microSD cards has staggering bandwidth. Play with it.

A third measure is often forgotten: resource friendliness. Money and energy are limited. In the authors' training courses, people take a single modest machine from 0.2 MB/s to over 200 MB/s purely by configuration. Kafka is built for speed from the ground up: a simple log with predictable sequential access (fine even on spinning disks), no interpretation of message contents, and as much work as possible pushed to the clients.

2

How many partitions does a topic need?

Partitions decide how far a consumer group can scale: at most one consumer per partition. So start from the bottleneck, usually the consumer. If processing one message takes 100 ms, one consumer handles 10 per second; a peak of 100 per second needs 10 consumers and so 10 partitions. Then add headroom and prefer numbers that divide nicely.

3

Changing the count later is expensive

Partitions can only be added. New ones start empty (uneven until retention evens things out), and worse, hash(key) % n changes so keys move and per-key order breaks until old data expires. If order matters, you migrate to a new topic instead. Which path fits?

    Make the migration countSince you are moving anyway, it is a chance to fix names, configuration and formats (often JSON to Avro or Protobuf). And to survive duplicates during the move, give events an ID so consumers can skip what they already processed.
    4

    Producer batching: batch.size and linger.ms

    The producer collects messages per partition into batches. A batch goes out when it is full (batch.size, default 16 KB) or when linger.ms has passed (default 0, send at once). Bigger batches mean fewer requests, better compression and less load on brokers. The authors find the defaults too small and suggest up to 1 MB and about 10 ms.     5

    Compress once, save everywhere

    compression.type (none, gzip, snappy, lz4, zstd) compresses whole batches in the producer. The leader stores the compressed batch as is, followers copy it as is, and only the consumer unpacks it. One compression saves network on two hops and disk on every replica. zstd or lz4 usually give the best balance.

    Relative, illustrative figures for typical text-like data; measure with your own messages. Producers may differ in codec on one topic, but standardising keeps throughput and storage predictable. A consumer that starts mid-batch receives and unpacks the whole batch.

    Brokers: work with the operating system

    A broker mostly moves bytes from a socket to disk and from disk to a socket. Instead of fighting the operating system like many databases do, Kafka leans on it. Step through a write and a read.

    How many brokers?

    Start with three for small setups (replication factor 3 and survive maintenance plus a failure). Beyond that, estimate from storage, throughput and network, balancing too-few-big against too-many-small brokers. The book's example: 1 TB a day, kept 7 days, replication 3, 2 TB disks.

    Disk is only one constraint; also check expected MB/s against network cards, and leave headroom so a lost broker's data fits on the rest.

    8

    Consumer fetch tuning

    Consumers pull, so they can't be overloaded. Two settings trade latency for efficiency: the broker waits until it has fetch.min.bytes of data (default 1 byte) or until fetch.max.wait.ms passes (default 500 ms). Answers contain whole batches.

    Recap and quick check

    Latency matters mostHigh throughput does not imply low latency. Measure both.
    PartitionsSize from consumer speed; 12 is a good default; only ever grow.
    Batchingbatch.size up to 1 MB, linger.ms about 10 ms.
    CompressionPer batch, end to end; zstd or lz4.
    BrokersPage cache, sequential I/O, zero-copy; tune OS limits; start with three.
    ConsumersScale with groups; tune fetch.min.bytes and fetch.max.wait.ms, never to 0.
