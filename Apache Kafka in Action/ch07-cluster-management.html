Cluster Management

Someone has to decide which broker leads which partition and who is alive. Kafka moved that job from an external ZooKeeper ensemble into its own Raft-based controller quorum.

1

Somebody has to be in charge

With one broker there is nothing to agree on: it leads every partition and coordinates every group. Add brokers and questions appear. Who assigns partitions, notices dead brokers and removes them, stores the cluster configuration? Kafka gives that job to one controller. But how is it chosen, and what if it dies or slows down? That is the classic problem of consensus in distributed systems, and Kafka has solved it two ways: with Apache ZooKeeper, and now with its own KRaft.

2

KRaft: a Raft quorum and a metadata log

KRaft is based on the Raft consensus algorithm (Ongaro and Ousterhout, 2014). Kafka processes take a role: controller, broker, or both. Controllers form the quorum and elect one active controller; the rest are hot standby controllers. All metadata goes into an internal topic, __cluster_metadata, which every broker replicates. Kafka's coordination is now a Kafka log. Click controllers to stop or restart them, and make changes.

Recommended production layoutThree or five dedicated controllers plus at least three brokers. Four controllers are no better than three. Combined controller-broker processes save machines outside production; on a laptop a single process can do both.

How ZooKeeper did it, and why it had to go

ZooKeeper looks like a small hierarchical key-value store of ZNodes, with one superpower: all its nodes always agree (via the ZAB protocol, a Paxos relative). Migrating from ZooKeeper to KRaft

ZooKeeper was deprecated in Kafka 3.5 and is removed in 4.0, so the authors urge migrating soon. It works without downtime, but only to dedicated KRaft controllers. Walk through the phases; note where you can still turn back.

5

How a client finds the right broker

A database has one address. Kafka has many brokers, and each partition's leader may be on a different one. Clients start from bootstrap servers, ask any broker for metadata (every broker caches it), then talk directly to the leaders. Since Kafka 0.10 nobody asks ZooKeeper for this any more.

Each broker still needs its own reachable address, because after the metadata response the client connects to the leaders directly. When a leader moves, the client gets an error, refreshes metadata and reconnects after a short pause.

ControllerOne process manages partitions, leaders and broker membership.
KRaftRaft quorum of controllers; metadata in __cluster_metadata; standby takes over instantly.
ZooKeeperSeparate system, ZNode race for controller, slow polling, stale caches. Gone in Kafka 4.0.
MigrationNo downtime; reversible until controllers leave migration mode.
