Consuming Messages

Consumers pull. They ask for data from an offset, remember how far they got in a special topic, and share partitions with their group through a coordinator and an assignment strategy.

1

Fetch requests and where a new consumer starts

After the metadata request, a consumer sends fetch requests to the partition leaders: "partition 0, starting at offset N". Normally the library picks partitions and offsets for you. When a group has no stored offset yet, auto.offset.reset decides where it starts: latest (the default, only new messages) or earliest (from the oldest message still kept). Offsets never change once assigned, so after cleanup there may be gaps; the consumer simply gets the next existing one.

2

Fetch from the closest replica

Brokers can be told where they live with broker.rack (a rack, zone or data center). Originally that just spread replicas across failure domains. Since KIP-392, a consumer that sets client.rack may fetch from a follower in its own rack. Producers still write to the leader.

Try the built-in way. Produce to two partitions, consume with a group, and watch kafka-consumer-groups.sh --describe. Lag (log end offset minus committed offset) is the single most important consumer metric.

4

The rebalance protocol

Consumers in a group must agree who reads what, and coordination is hard. Rather than a separate coordination service per group, the brokers help: one broker acts as group coordinator (chosen by hashing the group ID, so the load spreads). The same protocol also coordinates Kafka Connect and Kafka Streams. True to form, the brokers don't care what is being divided: the clients compute it.

KIP-848, the next-generation consumer group protocol arriving with Kafka 4.0, moves this work to the broker and greatly simplifies it. The authors recommend switching once it is available.

5

Assignment strategies, side by side

partition.assignment.strategy decides how the group leader splits partitions. All members must use the same one. Two topics, prices and sales, are consumed by one group. Add and remove consumers and compare.

Range is for co-partitioned topicsRange gives partition N of every topic to the same consumer, so data with the same key in two topics meets in one consumer. That makes joins possible, but the authors advise doing joins with a stream processing library such as Kafka Streams, not by hand. For plain consumers, cooperative sticky is the recommendation until the new protocol arrives.
6

Static membership: fewer rebalances

Every join and every leave triggers a rebalance, and classic rebalances pause the group. A rolling restart of N consumers therefore causes 2N rebalances. Kafka 2.3 added static membership: give each consumer a stable group.instance.id (a StatefulSet pod name or stable hostname) and a longer session.timeout.ms. A consumer that comes back within the timeout simply resumes its partitions.
