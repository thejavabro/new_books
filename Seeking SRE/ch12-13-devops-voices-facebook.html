Chapter 13

Production Engineering at Facebook

A conversation with Pedro Canahuati, who led the change from a NOC-style ops team to production engineering.
3

From NOC to production engineering

Production engineering rests on two beliefs: operational problems should be solved with software, and the people who build software are the best people to run it. Early Facebook had the industry-standard model; it didn't survive the shift to mobile. Step through the change.

4

Centralized reporting, decentralized seating

Who should ops report to?

Canahuati argues the head of operations should not report to the CEO as a peer of the head of engineering. Instead, ops reports to the most senior engineering leader. Then if operations fails, engineering leadership fails, and "Dev versus Ops" goes away because Ops is part of Dev.

Where do PEs sit?

Production engineers report through a central PE organization but sit with the software teams they work with. Colocation matters more than anything for collaboration. Around 18 to 24 months in, managers ask whether a PE wants to move teams. Mobility is a core tenet.

"If you write code and release it, congratulations, you own it."

Software engineers take primary on-call. Where PEs are embedded, on-call is shared, usually alternating weeks (shorter shifts when the system is hard to run). Only two cases are single-discipline: SWE teams with no embedded PE, and infrastructure that PE builds end to end (server provisioning, automated remediation like FBAR, the L4 load balancer, network-condition testing tools).

5

Bootstrap, scale, awesomize

Services move through three phases, and people tend to love one of them. Few stay with a service through all three, and that's fine: they move to where their strengths fit. Nobody wants to "optimize"; everybody wants to make something awesome.

6

What makes a production engineer

Is your org a "PE org"?Canahuati's quick test: Who is on call (shared and embedded)? Do PEs have strong relationships and technical credibility with SWEs? Is there a balance between operational and feature work? Is leadership accountable for delivering a system that is both feature-rich and stable? And he warns: copy rules strictly and it will fail. Take what fits.
