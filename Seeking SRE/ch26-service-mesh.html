The Service Mesh: Wrangler of Your Microservices?

Matt Klein (Lyft, creator of Envoy): why microservice networking is hard, and how out-of-process proxies became the answer.
1

From monolith to a networking mess

The classic monolith is simple to reason about: a load balancer, stateless app servers, a database. Breaking it into microservices brings real benefits (independent deploys, team autonomy) but moves complexity into the network. Now every team must solve service discovery, retries, timeouts, rate limiting and observability, often in several languages at once. Step through the evolution.

Fat libraries or a sidecar proxy?

One option is to standardize on few languages and ship a "fat" client library (Finagle, Hystrix) to each. That works, but every language needs a reimplementation, upgrades require redeploying every service, and behavior drifts between versions. The alternative: put all of it in an out-of-process sidecar proxy beside each service. The app talks to localhost; the proxy does the rest, identically for every language. Costs of the sidecarAn extra hop adds latency (well under a millisecond for a well-built proxy, but not zero), and it's one more process to deploy and monitor. Applications still need a thin library for context propagation: copying trace IDs and similar headers from incoming to outgoing requests so distributed tracing works.

Eventually consistent discovery plus health checks

Strongly consistent discovery systems (ZooKeeper, etcd) are hard to run and overkill: membership is always a bit stale anyway. Envoy treats the discovery data as eventually consistent and combines it with active and passive health checking. Pick a cell to see the decision.

Data plane and control plane

Data planeThe proxies themselves: they touch every packet, enforcing routing, retries, TLS and metrics. Envoy is a data plane.
Control planeThe system that configures all proxies: discovery data, routes, policies. Istio and similar projects are control planes; many companies build their own.
6

Lessons from rolling out Envoy at Lyft

In 2015 Lyft had a PHP monolith on MongoDB plus a growing set of Python services, and developers didn't trust the network: when something failed, nobody could tell whether the bug was in the app or the network. Envoy was deployed first at the edge, then as a sidecar everywhere, and observability won the developers over. Click the timeline.
