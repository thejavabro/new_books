Chapter 32

Intersections Between Operations and Social Activism

Emily Gorcenski and Liz Fong-Jones: SRE skills apply to organizing, and organizing teaches SREs about who their systems serve.
1

Reliable is not the same as good

A service can meet every SLO and still harm people. The authors argue that engineers, who already think about failure modes and users, are well placed to ask who a system works for, and that the practices of protest organizing and SRE have a surprising amount in common. Gorcenski draws on her experience in Charlottesville in 2017, where a KKK rally (July 8) was followed by the violent "Unite the Right" rally (August 12).

2

Before, during and after: an incident life cycle

Organizing an action looks a lot like managing an incident or a big launch.

Before

Plan, set shared principles, assign roles, rehearse, assess risk. Coalitions agree how different groups will act.SRE: launch reviews, runbooks, game days.

During

Clear roles and communication channels; adapt as conditions change; crisis response when something goes wrong.SRE: incident command, comms, mitigation first.

After

Care for people, debrief, and write your own history before others write it for you.SRE: blameless retrospectives.
3

The St. Paul Principles: coordination across groups

Created for protests at the 2008 Republican convention, the St. Paul Principles let groups with different tactics act together without undermining each other. In paraphrase:

  1. Solidarity is based on respecting a diversity of tactics and each group's plans.
  2. Actions are separated by time or space.
  3. Internal debates stay internal; no public denunciation of fellow activists.
  4. Oppose state repression of dissent, including surveillance and infiltration; don't help the authorities against other activists.
The operations parallelTeams with different practices sharing infrastructure need similar rules: respect each team's approach, isolate blast radius in time or space, resolve disputes inside, and present a common front during incidents.
4

Role differentiation on the ground

Effective actions, like incident response, split work into explicit roles so no one is overloaded. At the July 2017 counter-demonstration, organizers had media, outreach, chant-leader and snack teams plus a planned blockade. 5

Activism inside a company

Fong-Jones describes applying these ideas at work: inclusive documentation, advocating in product decisions, and making sure reliability is measured for all users, not just the median one on a fast connection. Tick the user groups your SLIs actually measure.

The long tailChange is slow. Quoting the spirit of activist Grace Lee Boggs, the authors stress patient, sustained work over one big action, just as reliability comes from steady improvement rather than one heroic project.

Conclusion

David N. Blank-Edelman
6

A pause in the conversation

The editor closes by framing the book not as a final word but as a pause in an ongoing conversation about how to run production systems well. SRE is still young, and practitioners in many settings will keep adapting it. His request to readers: keep the conversation going, share what you learn, and add your own voice.
