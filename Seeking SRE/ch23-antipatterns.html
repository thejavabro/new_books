SRE Antipatterns

Blake Bisset: eighteen ways organizations adopt the SRE label while keeping the habits SRE was invented to replace, each with a motto and an escape route.
1

Learning from what goes wrong

Bisset collects patterns he has watched recur across companies trying to "do SRE". Most aren't stupid: each looked reasonable locally, under time pressure, with incomplete information. The trouble is that they quietly re-create the operations model SRE was meant to replace, then blame the new name when nothing improves. Each comes with a tongue-in-cheek motto; each has a better alternative.

Optimize for recovery, not just failure avoidance

Antipattern 13: organizations pour effort into making failures rare (long MTTF) while recovery stays slow and manual. But in complex systems failure is inevitable, and user-visible downtime is roughly frequency × duration. Compare two investments.

4

Mosquito fleet versus supertanker

Antipattern 15, ungainly governance: heavy, centralized change-review processes turn the organization into a supertanker: stable, but miles to turn. The alternative is a mosquito fleet of small, autonomous teams with clear service contracts between them, each able to change course quickly, where a single failure sinks one boat, not the fleet.
