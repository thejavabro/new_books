The Purpose of Planning

Plans are often wrong, yet we still need them. The trick is to plan in a way that gets less wrong over time and is cheap to change when we learn something.

1

The cone of uncertainty

Early in a project, nobody knows enough to give a precise date. Estimates start wide and narrow as we learn. This shape is called the cone of uncertainty. Slide along the project to see how far off a "20-week" estimate could really be at each point.

12weeks, best case
20weeks, our estimate
32weeks, worst case

Two bad reactions to this cone: don't plan at all (and you can't answer "will it ship in June?"), or plan so hard you believe the plan must be right. More effort up front doesn't move the cone. Only learning does.

4

What makes a plan good?

A good plan is one that people can safely base decisions on: marketing campaigns, training, hiring. It doesn't have to be exactly right. It has to be close enough. Drag the actual delivery month for a project planned at six months and watch the decisions that depended on it.

5

Planning, not the plan

A plan is a snapshot. Planning is the activity. Agile planning puts the weight on the activity and spreads it across the whole project instead of doing it all on day one. Compare where the planning effort goes.

Focused on planningThe insight from planning outlives any single plan.
Welcomes changeA change means we learned something or avoided a mistake.
Easy to changeDrop a feature, shrink one, add people. The date need not move.
Spread throughoutRelease planning, then many rounds of iteration planning.

Redefining success

A popular definition says a project succeeds if it is on time, on budget, with every feature first listed. That rewards a team for ignoring better ideas found along the way. Pick which project you would call the success.
