Prioritizing Themes

"Prioritize on business value" is vague advice. Four concrete factors work better: financial value, cost, new knowledge and risk reduction. Value and cost set the order, knowledge and risk nudge it.

1

Four factors

① Financial valueHow much the theme makes or saves. Chapter 10 models it; chapter 11 offers non-financial stand-ins.
② CostTo build, and perhaps to support. Cost can change over time.
③ New knowledgeAbout the product (what) and the project (how).
④ Risk removedSchedule, cost and functionality risks, business or technical.
2

Turn points into money

Time costs money. A rough conversion makes trade-offs concrete: add up everyone's pay over recent iterations and divide by the points finished.

to build this theme

The product owner's question becomes: "Is this theme worth that much to us?"

Cost also moves over time. Internationalization might take 4 weeks now or 6 later. But doing it now might mean 3 more weeks of rework as needs change, or building something fancier than turns out necessary. The cheapest moment to build is often as late as possible.

3

End uncertainty and means uncertainty

End uncertainty is about what to build. Means uncertainty is about how. Waterfall tries to remove all end uncertainty first, then works on means. Agile reduces both together, leaning toward learning what early, because building the wrong product is the biggest risk.

4

Risk versus value

Risk-first teams may build infrastructure nobody ends up needing. Value-first teams may hit a project-killing risk late. Use both: high value first, risk as the tie-breaker. Click a feature to place it.

5

Combining the four factors

Start by sorting themes on value relative to cost. Then move a theme earlier if building it teaches a lot or removes a big risk. Toggle the nudges.
