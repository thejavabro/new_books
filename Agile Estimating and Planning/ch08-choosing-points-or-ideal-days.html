Choosing Between Story Points and Ideal Days

Both units work. Story points win on team behavior, durability and speed. Ideal days win on being easy to explain and easy to start. The recommendation: points, eased in through ideal days if needed.

2

Points push cross-functional thinking

"I'm on the Napa project, and I'm a tester" beats "I'm a tester assigned to Napa". A single point value for the whole story starts a whole-team conversation. Ideal days often start with each specialty estimating its own slice and adding them up.

3

Ideal-day estimates decay

A programmer learning a new language estimates a small app at 5 ideal days. Months later, now fluent, an app of exactly the same size is estimated at 1 ideal day. Velocity stays flat at 5, hiding a fivefold improvement. Step through.

4

My ideal days aren't your ideal days

A fast and a slow runner agree a trail is 1 km, half as long as another trail. Ask how long it takes and they can't agree, and both are right. Size is shared. Time is personal.

5

Pure size, no awkward comparisons

Because points are abstract, nobody holds them up against the calendar. Teams in ideal days end up defending why they "only" did 8 ideal days in a 10-day iteration. Points also force estimating by analogy, which people do better than absolute guesses, and teams tend to estimate in points faster.

Ideal days8 of 10"What did you do with the other two days?"
Story points21 pts"Great, that's about our average." No calendar to compare with.
6

The recommendation, and a gentle switch

Prefer story points. If a team struggles with abstract size, start in ideal days and slowly change the question you ask. Most teams barely notice the switch. Slide through the weeks.
