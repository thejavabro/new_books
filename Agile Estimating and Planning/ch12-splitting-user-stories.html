Splitting User Stories

Big stories don't fit in an iteration and are hard to estimate. Split them vertically, so every piece is still a sliver of real, testable value. Here are the patterns that work, and two traps.

1

When to split

Split when a story is bigger than an iteration, when it won't fit in the room left in the iteration being planned, or when you need a more accurate estimate. (A 70-point epic was enough for one product owner to say yes: even 100% off, it was worth doing. Had it been borderline, splitting would have sharpened the number.) Drop stories into a two-week iteration with 20 points of room.

2

The splitting lab

Pick an epic and a technique. Each child story is still a vertical slice: something a user could see and test.

3

Operational boundaries across iterations

A complex search screen: dozens of criteria fields, a query builder and a rich results grid. Too much for one iteration, so it was spread across three, with the riskiest part (the grid) moved into iteration two.

4

Trap: don't split into tasks

Splitting into "code the UI" and "write the middle tier" feels natural, but none of those pieces does anything for a user. Fire a tracer bullet instead: a thin slice through every layer.

5

Trap: related changes, and combining stories

Once a story is the right size, don't grow it with "while I'm in there..." changes. Fixing a year-old bug nearby competes for priority like everything else. And the opposite is fine too: combine tiny stories, especially bug fixes, and estimate them together.
