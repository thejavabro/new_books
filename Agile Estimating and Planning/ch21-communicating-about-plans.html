Communicating About Plans

How you talk about a plan matters as much as the plan. Make communication frequent, honest and two-way. Give dates as ranges or with a stated confidence, show features (not tasks) on any Gantt chart, and forecast with three velocities, not one.

Honest version
A good pitch, roughly in Ron Jeffries' spirit: "Right now this looks like a 200-point project, which suggests four to six months. But you'll get working software every two weeks. If you're not satisfied, you can stop. If you're satisfied early, you can stop early. Whenever there's enough to be useful, we'll prepare it for release. You'll know everything as soon as we do."
3

A Gantt chart, done the agile way

Gantt charts earned their bad reputation through task-level micro-scheduling. Drawn at the feature level, they're a fine way to show which features land in which iteration.

4

Forecast with three velocities

Look at up to the last eight iterations and take three numbers: the most recent velocity (what just happened), the mean (the long run) and the mean of the worst three (a realistic bad case). Edit the velocities.

Don't draw a trend line through velocities. If velocity seems to be rising, be pleased but don't plan on it. If it's falling, find and fix the cause rather than planning for further decline.
