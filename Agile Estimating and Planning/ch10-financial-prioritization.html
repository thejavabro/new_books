Financial Prioritization

Most organizations care about the bottom line, so model each theme as a cash flow and compare themes with NPV, IRR, payback period and discounted payback. Follow one worked example: WebPayroll's overnight service.

WebPayroll: next-day payroll

WebPayroll runs payroll for small companies, but needs data three days ahead. The overnight theme lets customers enter data by 5 p.m. and get checks the next morning. It's 150 points; at 20 points per two-week iteration that's 7.5, so 8 iterations (16 weeks). Toggle sources to see how the quarterly cash flow builds up.

What the team costs

Fully burdened labor cost is about 50% above salary (space, equipment, benefits). With two-week iterations, each person costs 1/26 of that per iteration, scaled by time on the project.

4

The time value of money

"I'll gladly pay you Tuesday for a hamburger today" is a bad deal: money now is worth more than money later. The present value is what you'd need to invest today to have the future amount. The rate used is your opportunity cost, the return you give up by not investing elsewhere.

5

Net present value

Discount every quarter's net cash flow back to today and add them up. Slide the discount rate. At 12% a year the overnight theme is worth about ¤46,341 today.

Easy to compute and explain. But two themes with the same NPV can tie up very different amounts of cash.
6

Internal rate of return

IRR is the rate at which NPV hits zero: the return expressed as a percentage, so themes of different sizes compare directly. No need to guess a discount rate. It needs costs first, then gains that stay positive, and more gained than spent.

Most choose B: it earns more per unit of money. Many organizations set a minimum attractive rate of return (MARR) for this reason. Still, context matters: a high-IRR project that ties up your key developer, or that needs two years of investment you can't afford, may lose to a lower one.

7

Payback and discounted payback

How long until the investment is earned back? The payback period is easy to grasp and shows how long money is at risk. It ignores the time value of money, so discounted payback uses present values instead.
