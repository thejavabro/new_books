Re-Estimating

Re-estimate a story only when its relative size has changed. Don't re-estimate just because work went slower than hoped. That's what velocity is for.

1

Meet SwimStats

The running example from here on: a website for competitive swim teams. Coaches manage rosters, plan practices and prepare for meets. Swimmers check results and personal records. Officials enter meet results.

2

Velocity is the great equalizer

Four SwimStats stories, all equally sized at 3 points. After iteration 1, only two are done (velocity 6, not the hoped-for 12). The team is tempted to double the finished stories to 6 each. Watch what happens to the forecast.

Estimates don't have to be right. They have to be consistent. Measure velocity for a few iterations and the schedule hones in by itself. (Consistent doesn't mean random: you can't just roll dice.)
3

When to re-estimate: three scenarios

Six SwimStats stories. Three are charts (1, 2, 3). The team planned 13 points (stories 1, 2, 6) but finished only 1 and 6. Story 1 "should have been at least a 6", and the real lesson is that all charting is about twice as hard as thought. Step through the scenarios.

4

Partially completed stories

"Recommend who swims each event" (5 points) turned out deceptively hard. By the iteration's end the system handles individual events, but relays aren't started. How much credit?

The two best answers: don't have incomplete stories, and keep stories small enough that partial credit never matters.
5

The purpose of re-estimating

Re-estimate as few stories as possible to bring relative sizes back in line, and treat each one as a lesson for estimating the next stories. Sort these situations.

Recap on one page

Not because it was slowSlower-than-hoped progress is a velocity fact, not an estimate fact.
Velocity equalizesConsistent estimates plus measured velocity give a reliable schedule.
Don't mixHindsight estimates for done work plus original estimates for future work distort the forecast.
Do re-estimateWhen the relative size of a whole class of stories has changed.
Partial workPrefer all or nothing. If the rest won't be done soon, split and re-estimate both parts.
