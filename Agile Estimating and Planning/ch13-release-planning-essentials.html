Release Planning Essentials

At its simplest, release planning is arithmetic: iterations × velocity = how much fits. Then the product owner picks the most valuable stories up to that line, and the plan is revisited every iteration.

The core arithmetic

Ship in six months with two-week iterations and a velocity of 20: thirteen iterations, room for 260 points. Try your own numbers.

A release plan is just a list of user stories (plus key assumptions: team, iteration length, start and end dates). It does not say who does what or in what order inside an iteration. Breaking stories into tasks waits until the iteration that contains them.

2

The steps

Start with the conditions of satisfaction. Do the middle four in any order. Select stories and a date, then loop until the conditions are best met. Click a step.

3

Date-driven or feature-driven?

Most projects set targets for scope, schedule and resources, but one usually rules. Date-driven: the date is fixed, features flex. Feature-driven: the features matter most, the date flexes. Velocity 20, ten prioritized themes.

Example: planning the SwimStats release

The spring season starts in four months; the site must be out a month earlier. Budget is fixed: the three people already on staff. Two-week iterations, forecast velocity 8, six iterations: 48 points (plus a spare week). Prioritized by the product owner. Tick stories to include them.

How much detail per iteration?

Assigning every story to a specific iteration takes time and is done with less knowledge than you'll have later. A good compromise: plan the first one to three iterations specifically and treat the rest as one bucket. Index cards on a table or cork board make the what-ifs easy.

Don't let the plan rot on the wall. If velocity is steady and nothing surprising happens, revisit it every four to six weeks; many teams simply update it at the start of every iteration.
