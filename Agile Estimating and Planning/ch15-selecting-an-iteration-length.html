Selecting an Iteration Length

There's no universally right iteration length. Weigh seven factors for your situation, pick a length, and then stick with it so the team finds its rhythm. Two weeks is a good default.

Iteration length advisor

Set each factor for your project. The suggestion updates as you go. (A rough guide that mirrors the chapter's reasoning, not a formula.)

2

Enough chances to steer

Each iteration end is a chance to show potentially shippable software, measure progress and correct course. Aim for at least four or five of them in a release.

3

From idea to working software

Priorities don't change mid-iteration. A new idea, on average, arrives mid-iteration, waits for the next one, then takes a full iteration: about 1.5 iterations from idea to software. Watch it travel.

4

Keeping a steady sense of urgency

Far-away deadlines feel relaxed, near ones frantic. Long iterations swing between a lazy start and a panicked end. Pick a length that spreads the same total effort evenly, without simply adding pressure.

5

Stick with it: a project heartbeat

Size the work to the iteration, not the iteration to the work. A fixed length gives the team a heartbeat and a sustainable pace. A popular variation is 6×2+1: six two-week iterations, then a one-week iteration where the team picks its own priority work (risky refactoring, test automation, research).

Avoid ending iterations at month end. One release slipped from March 31 to April 7, just one week, but into the next fiscal quarter, so its revenue couldn't count until Q2. It might as well have shipped on June 30.
