Buffering Plans for Uncertainty

Some projects need more than a velocity range. When the date and the scope both matter, protect the plan with buffers: a feature buffer (optional work you can drop) and a schedule buffer (one shared pot of time sized from how uncertain each story is).

Feature buffer: the grocery list

You have 20 minutes to shop before the game starts. Some items must come home, others are nice to have. Shop the musts first, and whatever time is left goes to the extras. A release works the same way: deliver the mandatory work, and plan some optional work on top.

Rule of thumb: plan 25–40% optional work on top of the mandatory work. DSDM's MoSCoW says no more than 70% of the effort should be Must Have. The rest (Should, Could, Won't) is the buffer.
3

Schedule buffer: getting to the airport

Each step of a trip to the airport has a 50% estimate (as likely early as late) and a 90% estimate (you'd almost never be later). Compare three ways to plan the trip.

The buffered plan removes the local safety from each step and pools part of it at the end, where it protects the one deadline that matters: the flight. Leaving nothing in individual steps also takes away the room that Parkinson's law and student syndrome would fill.

4

Why one estimate isn't enough

Imagine 100 developers each building the same feature. Most finish around day 2 or 3, and a long tail runs to day 6. Drag the commitment day to see how many would be done by then.

5

Sizing the buffer

Estimate each story twice: 50% first, then 90%. The gap (90% minus 50%) is about two standard deviations, so the buffer that protects the whole project to 90% is the square root of the sum of the squared gaps. Edit the numbers.

Simpler rule: buffer = 50% of the sum of the 50% estimates. It's easy, but it ignores which stories are risky.
Guidelines (Leach): the formula works best with 10 or more stories, and the buffer should be at least 20% of the total project duration.
6

Combine buffers

Buffer each kind of uncertainty in its own units: features with features, time with time. Shoulder belts and airbags protect against different crashes. Using both also lets each one be smaller.

Other buffers exist too, such as a budget or staffing buffer (30 people on a budget for 33). That works on big teams. On a 4-person team, 10% of staff is 0.4 of a person, and the 5th person would just do work anyway.

7

A buffer is not padding

Padding: "I think 3 days, but I'll say 5, just in case." Hidden, arbitrary, per task.
Buffer: local safety is removed from each estimate and a computed margin is added once, in the open. You fully expect to use most of it, like the car lengths ahead of you.
