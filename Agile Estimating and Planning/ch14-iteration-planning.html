2

Plan on cards, not a keyboard

Whoever holds the keyboard controls the plan. One lead typed 8 hours after the team agreed on 12 ("no way it takes that long"), though he wouldn't do the task. Another typed "write data storage code" instead of the "stored procedures" everyone else agreed on. With cards, anyone can grab a pen.

3

Stories become tasks

Tasks hang under their story. Include every task that adds value to this project: analysis, UI design, tests, and meetings too. A one-hour meeting for seven people plus two hours of prep is a nine-hour task. Tasks are not assigned to people now: people sign up for one or two at a time once the iteration starts.

SpikesCan't tell if a legacy change is 4 or 20 hours? Write "find out what's affected, 2 h" plus a placeholder "make the changes, 10 h".
Some design is OKDiscuss enough to name tasks. If someone reaches for a class diagram, take it outside the meeting.
About a day eachAim for tasks one person can finish in about a day. A 16-hour card is a placeholder to replace once understood.
DependenciesUsually fine. Doing stories out of their natural order just moves some tasks (like designing tables) to another story.
4

Two ways to plan an iteration

Adjust priorities after the iteration review (a short prioritization meeting a couple of days before helps). An iteration goal is one unifying sentence, such as "make progress on reports".

5

Can we commit? A simulation

Seven people, two weeks: 560 hours on paper. Nobody does 8 task-hours a day; most teams succeed planning 4 to 6 hours per person per day. First pour in the support and maintenance you can't avoid, then add stories one at a time and ask after each one.

Also check the mix of skills: will the tester drown while the HTML developer idles? Everyone contributes whatever they can, beyond their specialty.

6

Why not just use velocity?

Story points are coarse. A team with velocity 30 five times running will likely do 30 again, but individual 1-, 2- and 3-point stories vary widely in hours. Their distributions overlap, so errors only average out over 20 to 30 stories, more than most iterations hold.

1-point stories (avg 12 h)2-point (avg 24 h)3-point (avg 36 h)
Recommendation: velocity for release planning, commitment-driven for iteration planning. The two approaches often produce the same plan anyway.
