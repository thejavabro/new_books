Techniques for Estimating

Spend a little effort, get most of the accuracy. Estimate as a team, on a non-linear scale, by combining expert opinion, analogy and disaggregation. Planning poker does all three at once.

1

Diminishing returns on estimating

A one-hour car wash gets your car pretty clean. A four-hour detailing with cotton swabs gets it slightly cleaner. Estimating is the same: a small effort buys a big jump in accuracy, and past a point, more effort can even make things worse. The curve is a rule of thumb, not measured data.

Match effort to purpose. Deciding whether to send someone a gift box of cookies doesn't need an audit of their cookie habits. A build-or-buy decision may only need "6 to 12 months", not "7 to 8".

2

Estimates are shared

On an agile team we don't know in advance who will pick up a story, so everyone helps estimate. And people who couldn't build it can still catch mistakes.

3

A scale of buckets

People estimate well within one order of magnitude, so use a short, non-linear set of values: gaps grow as uncertainty grows. Think of each value as a bucket that work, like sand, gets poured into. A story slightly over 5 can go in the 5 bucket. A "7" cannot.

Some teams add 0 for truly trivial work, so tiny items don't inflate velocity. Just remember 13 × 0 is still not 0 of work: there's a limit to free lunches per iteration. And never allow 66 vs. 67. Nobody can see a 1.5% difference.
4

Stories, epics and themes

Not everything needs fine-grained stories yet. A big, distant story is an epic. A set of related stories clipped together is a theme. Near-term work uses 1 to 8. Far-off work can use the bigger buckets, accepting more uncertainty. Click to zoom in.

Three ways to derive an estimate

Expert opinionAsk someone who knows; they answer from gut feel. Fast, and can be surprisingly accurate. Harder on agile teams because a story needs many skills.
Analogy"A bit bigger than that one." Compare with several estimated stories: triangulate.
DisaggregationSplit a big item into smaller pieces that are easier to compare. Don't go too far.

Triangulate a new story

Drop the new story between existing ones. If it's a bit bigger than the 3 and a bit smaller than the 8, it's a 5.

The golf trap: disaggregating too far

Estimate a round of golf hole by hole (each hole 3 to 8 strokes) and the range explodes, plus you might forget a hole. Estimated as a whole, the range is tighter and more useful.

6

Planning poker

Every developer gets a deck: 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 20, 40, 100. The moderator reads a story, the product owner answers questions (but doesn't estimate), everyone picks a card privately, and all cards flip at once. High and low explain. Vote again until estimates converge.

Why planning poker works

When to play: once at the start to estimate the initial backlog, then briefly each iteration for new stories. Keep discussion short. Some teams use a two-minute sand timer.
