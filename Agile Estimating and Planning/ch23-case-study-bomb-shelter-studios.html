A Case Study: Bomb Shelter Studios

The whole book, as a story. A small studio that shipped its last game six months late tries agile planning on its next one, a board game called Havannah. Follow the team from the first story-writing session to the revised plan they present to the CEO, and see each chapter's idea at work.

The game: rings, bridges and forks

Two players take turns placing stones on a hexagonal board. Stones are never captured. You win by building one of three shapes. Pick a shape, or click cells to place your own stones.

For the programmer writing the move engine, this means many ways to win, many cells, and no endgame book like chess has. The good news: so few people play Havannah that version one only needs an engine that beats most humans most of the time.
3

Day 1: writing and estimating stories

Coach Carlos insists the whole team attends, including the artist, who arrives five minutes late. They write stories by walking through a player's experience before, during and after a game, then estimate with planning poker cards: 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 20, 40, 100.

4

Product research: a Kano survey

While the team finishes the old game, Delaney surveys likely buyers: how would you feel if this feature were present, and if it were absent? Each bar shows how respondents classified a feature. Click one.

5

Release planning: Frank chooses

Must-haves are the cost of entry. Linear features add value the more you have. A few exciters make people say "wow" and justify a higher price. Indifferent features don't sell games. Choose what goes in, then see the schedule.

Frank asks, "Can we just call it seven iterations?" Carlos: "We could, but we shouldn't." With work spilling into an eighth iteration, the honest message is 6 to 10 iterations, 12 to 20 weeks. Even better would be to run an iteration first.

Presenting the revised plan to the CEO

Research found an exciter: computer characters with personalities (one who taunts you, for example). The first costs 15 points, each of four more costs 5: 35 points. Online play (30 points) was left out. Walk through Frank's whiteboard conversation with Phil.

"We're no further along than two iterations ago?" No: four weeks of real progress, real velocity data, and a more valuable game. Every new point on the burndown makes the plan more trustworthy, even when it isn't good news.
