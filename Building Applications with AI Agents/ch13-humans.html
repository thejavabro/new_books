Human-Agent Collaboration

Agents work alongside people. Decide who approves what, how autonomy grows as trust grows, and who is accountable when something goes wrong.

1

The human's role changes as the agent matures

Collaboration depends on calibrated autonomy: knowing when the agent acts, when it asks, and when it defers. As a system matures, people move from doing the work with the agent's help to setting the policy it works within. Slide through the four roles.

The book's examples include a bank's contract-review platform and a code-security bot, both starting with people checking every output and moving, as accuracy was proven, toward spot checks and then policy-level oversight.

2

Adoption is a journey, not a switch

Capable agents still fail if people do not embrace them. Involve stakeholders early as co-creators, not just testers. Measure more than accuracy: usefulness, reliability, fit with existing workflows and values. Train people, keep feedback loops open, and expand in phases once the numbers hold up. One example in the book: a coding assistant piloted with 50 engineers before rolling out to more than 400.

3

From personal assistant to enterprise agent

Agents serve different entities: one person, a team, a project, a department, the whole organization. Wider scope means wider data access, more autonomy, higher risk, and heavier governance. Drag the scope.

4

Memory boundaries between scopes

Personal agents remember preferences and tasks. Team and organizational agents remember meetings, decisions, documents: more powerful and more sensitive. Without scoping, an agent can surface one person's private context to a colleague. Ask the team agent a question as another teammate.

Lee asks the team agent: "What's going on with Sam lately?"

Also design context flow (what can move up from personal to team, never down without consent) and make memory visible: let people see, edit and delete what the agent remembers.

5

Progressive delegation and trust repair

Trust is not binary; it evolves. Progressive delegation grants more autonomy as the agent proves itself, with visible track records. When things go wrong, trust needs a repair path. Over-automation without a human fallback backfires: the book recounts a company that replaced hundreds of support staff with a chatbot, saw complaints surge, and rehired people. Run the agent for a while.

6

Accountability: frameworks, audits, logs

Someone must be responsible for every agent action. Rather than inventing a process, adapt an established one, such as the NIST AI Risk Management Framework with its four functions. Back it with ethical audits and logs that let you reconstruct why the agent did what it did.

Ethical audit checks

  • Outputs match intended goals and guidelines
  • Bias and fairness across groups
  • How the agent reached its decision
  • Impact on each stakeholder group
Ongoing, not one-off; independent third-party audits add credibility.

Logs that enable traceability

  • Decision logs: inputs, reasoning steps, outputs
  • Interaction logs: timestamped user inputs and responses
  • Error logs: when and why tasks failed
Protect them like any sensitive data, and make them readable for auditors.
7

Escalation design: act, ask or defer

Escalation policy is the layer that stops an agent acting beyond its authority. Encode it in code and in organizational roles, and feed escalated outcomes back into training and thresholds. Try a refund policy.

8

Privacy and regulatory compliance

Agents in critical workflows face legal scrutiny, and rules change fast. Pick what your agent handles to see which regimes the book highlights.

Build compliance into the pipelineAutomated gates on every build: scan for PII leakage, test prompts against fairness benchmarks, enforce data-handling rules codified with a policy engine such as Open Policy Agent. Keep a living model card and datasheet that record purpose, data, evaluations and limits.
9

Experiment, measure, govern, scale

The book ends with a call to build agents that are smart and wise, efficient and just. Its four practical steps sum up the whole companion.
