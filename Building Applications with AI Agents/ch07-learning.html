Learning in Agentic Systems

Agents can get better with experience in two ways: by collecting examples and insights they feed back into the prompt, or by changing the model's weights.

1

Learning without and with changing weights

An agent can improve in two ways. Nonparametric learning leaves the model alone and changes what goes into the prompt: saved examples, reflections, learned rules. Parametric learning changes the model's weights through fine-tuning. The first is cheap and instant; the second is powerful and needs data, compute and upkeep.

NonparametricExemplars · Reflexion · experiential insightsStore what worked and what was learned; retrieve it into future prompts. No retraining, works with any hosted model, reversible in seconds.
ParametricSFT · vision · DPO · RFT / RLVRTrain the model on curated data so the behavior is built in. Consistent and often cheaper per call later, but needs data, GPUs, versioning and retraining when base models change.
2

Exemplar learning: show, don't tell

The simplest technique: put examples of good behavior in the prompt. Better still, save every successful case to storage and retrieve the most similar ones for each new task, so the agent draws on its own track record. More examples help, but each one costs tokens and latency on every call. Drag to see the trade-off (illustrative curves).

3

Reflexion: write yourself a note after failing

After a failed attempt, the agent writes a short self-critique about its strategic misstep (not a recap of what happened) and stores it. On the next try, those reflections go into the prompt. It takes only a few lines of code. Step through a shopping task like the one the book uses.

4

Experiential learning: rules that earn their place

Reflexion learns within one task. Experiential learning learns across tasks: the agent compares failed and successful trials and edits a shared list of general rules with four operations, ADD, EDIT, AGREE and REMOVE. Agreement raises a rule's weight; rules that cross a threshold are promoted into the prompt, weak or contradicted ones fade out. Feed it trials from an agent that judges whether marketing metrics hit target.

Weight 3 or more = promoted (green) and included in every prompt.

7
The promise of small models

  
Fine-tuned small models can match large ones on narrow, well-defined tasks, at a fraction of the cost and latency. They are easier to inspect, quicker to retrain, cheaper to host, and lighter on energy. The "best" small model changes month to month, so check live leaderboards and your own constraints: latency, hardware, budget.

8
Supervised fine-tuning for function calling

  
SFT is the foundation: curated input and output pairs shape behavior precisely. For agents, the big win is reliable tool calls. Train on examples where the model decides whether to call, fills arguments exactly, handles missing parameters, and sometimes deliberately does not call ("Imagine it's snowing in Boston, what should I wear?" is hypothetical). Reasoning and calls are wrapped in special tokens so the model learns the format.
