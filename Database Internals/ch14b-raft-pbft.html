Raft: consensus designed to be understood

Raft (Ongaro and Ousterhout, 2014) was built to be easier to understand than Paxos. Each node keeps a log of commands; identical logs applied in the same order give identical state machines. Raft makes the leader central: only the leader accepts writes and entries flow only from leader to followers. Time is split into numbered terms, with at most one leader per term. Any message with a higher term makes the receiver update its term and step down. Click a role.

2

A live Raft cluster

Five nodes. Bars show each node's election timer; the leader's heartbeats (empty AppendEntries) reset them. When a timer runs out, the node becomes a candidate, increments its term, votes for itself and asks for votes. A node grants at most one vote per term, and only to a candidate whose log is at least as up to date as its own. A majority makes a leader. Log boxes show entry terms; filled boxes are committed (stored on a majority, with an entry from the current term).

Things to tryTick identical timeouts and crash the leader: several nodes become candidates at once, each votes for itself, and nobody wins (a split vote). Randomized timeouts make that rare. Then partition the leader with one follower and write: the old leader appends entries it can never commit, while the majority elects a new leader. Heal, and watch the old leader step down and have its uncommitted entries overwritten.
3

How a new leader repairs follower logs

Each AppendEntries carries the index and term of the entry just before the new ones. A follower rejects it if its log doesn't contain that entry. Because of this check, two logs that agree on an entry's index and term agree on everything before it (log matching). The leader backs off until it finds the last matching entry, then the follower drops everything after it and takes the leader's entries. The leader never rewrites its own log.

Byzantine consensus: when nodes may lie

Everything so far assumed crashed nodes stay silent and live nodes tell the truth. With Byzantine faults (malice, bugs, corruption), nodes can send conflicting messages. Tolerating f such nodes takes n = 3f + 1: you must make progress with n − f replies (f may be silent), and among those f may be lying, so honest replies must still outnumber liars. Quorums of 2f + 1 overlap in at least f + 1 nodes, so every overlap contains an honest node.

5

Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT)

In PBFT, each view has a primary (replica v mod n); the rest are backups. Messages are signed, so they can't be forged. After a client request: pre-prepare (primary assigns a sequence number and sends the request with its digest), prepare (every backup broadcasts the digest; a replica needs 2f matching prepares), commit (everyone broadcasts commit; a replica needs 2f + 1 matching). The client accepts a result once f + 1 replicas give the same reply. All-to-all phases make it O(n²) messages.
