Machine Learning Foundations

Underneath every LLM is ordinary deep learning: layers, activations, a loss, and an optimizer stepping downhill. Interviewers use these basics to check that you can reason about training, not just call an API.

1

From one neuron to a deep network

A neuron computes a weighted sum plus a bias, then applies a nonlinear activation. Stack layers of them and you get a composition fL(…f2(f1(x))). Without nonlinearity the whole stack collapses into one linear map. With it, the universal approximation theorem says a wide-enough network can approximate any continuous function. Depth makes that far more parameter-efficient. Add ReLU units and watch a network approximate a curve.

2

Activation functions

ReLU is cheap but has zero gradient for negative inputs ("dead" neurons). GELU (GPT-2, BERT) and SiLU/Swish are smooth and let small negative values through; modern LLMs mostly use SiLU inside a gated FFN called SwiGLU (Llama, Mistral, Qwen). Tanh and sigmoid saturate, so their gradients vanish at the extremes; sigmoid survives as a gate.

4

Cross-entropy and perplexity, with numbers

For next-token prediction the loss at a position is −log p(correct token), averaged over positions. It is the same as maximum likelihood. Perplexity = exp(average loss): roughly, how many tokens the model is choosing between. Drag the probability the model gives the right token.

5

SGD, momentum and Adam on a narrow valley

Gradient descent steps against the gradient. On a long narrow valley, plain SGD zig-zags across the steep walls and crawls along the floor. Momentum averages past gradients so oscillations cancel and progress builds. Adam also divides by a running average of squared gradients, giving each parameter its own step size. AdamW applies weight decay directly to the weights instead of mixing it into the adaptive gradient, which is why LLMs use it.

6

Warmup, then cosine decay

Early on, Adam's second-moment estimates are poor and the weights are random, so a large step can blow training up. Linear warmup (often the first 1 to 5% of steps) ramps the rate from zero. Cosine decay then lowers it smoothly to a small floor, so late training settles into a good minimum. Gradient clipping (often a max norm of 1.0) caps occasional spikes.

7

Overfitting, regularization and initialization

Overfitting shows as training loss falling while validation loss rises. Classic remedies: more data, dropout, weight decay, early stopping. For LLMs trained on trillions of tokens for about one epoch, classical overfitting is rare; the bigger concern is memorization of specific examples (privacy, copyright). Fine-tuning on small data is where overfitting comes back.
