Transformer Architecture

One architecture runs nearly all of modern generative AI. Be ready to derive self-attention on a whiteboard, explain every piece of the block, and say why decoder-only won.

1

Why transformers replaced RNNs

An RNN reads one token at a time and squeezes the past into a fixed-size state: training cannot be parallelized across positions, and information from far back fades (vanishing gradients). Self-attention connects every position to every other in one step, so a whole sequence trains in parallel on GPUs and long-range links are a single hop away. The price: computing all pairs costs O(n²·d) in sequence length n.

2

Self-attention, step by step

Each token embedding x is projected three ways: a query (what am I looking for?), a key (what do I offer?) and a value (what do I pass on?). Scores are Q·Kᵀ, scaled by √dk, turned into weights by softmax, and used to mix the values: Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax(QKᵀ/√dk)V. Here it is on three tokens with dk = 2.

Why divide by √dk?With unit-variance entries, a dot product of dk terms has variance dk. Large scores push softmax into a near one-hot regime where gradients vanish. Scaling restores unit variance.
3

Multi-head attention

Instead of one big attention, run h smaller heads of size d/h in parallel, each with its own projections, then concatenate and project back. Same cost, but each head can specialize: one tracks the previous token, one links pronouns to nouns, one follows syntax. Pick a head.

4

Inside a transformer block

A block is attention plus a feed-forward network, each wrapped with a residual connection and normalization. Attention mixes information across tokens; the FFN (expand to about 4d, nonlinearity, project back) transforms each token on its own and holds most of the parameters and much of the stored knowledge. Residuals give gradients a highway through dozens of layers. The original paper normalized after each sublayer (post-norm); modern LLMs normalize before (pre-norm), which trains far more stably at depth.

5

Positional encodings and RoPE

Attention treats its input as a set; without position information "dog bites man" equals "man bites dog". Options: sinusoidal or learned absolute vectors added to embeddings, relative biases, ALiBi (a distance penalty on scores), and RoPE, now the default. RoPE rotates each pair of query and key dimensions by an angle proportional to position. A dot product of two rotated vectors depends only on the difference of their angles, so attention sees relative distance.

6

Counting parameters

Per layer: attention has four d×d projections (Q, K, V, O) = 4d² (less with grouped-query attention); a SwiGLU FFN has three d×dff matrices = 3·d·dff. Add embeddings V·d (and an output head unless weights are tied). Norm parameters are negligible.

7

Decoder-only won; what comes next

Decoder-only won because one objective (next token) serves every task through prompting, every token is a training signal, it scales predictably, and the KV cache makes generation efficient. Encoders remain the right tool for cheap classification, embeddings and retrieval; encoder-decoders for strict input-to-output tasks like translation.
