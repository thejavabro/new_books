Throughput and G1 Collectors

The parallel collector stops everything and works flat out; G1 splits the heap into regions and does much of its work while your threads keep running. Watch both in action.

The throughput (parallel) collector

The parallel collector does two things, and both stop every application thread. A minor GC empties eden into a survivor space or the old generation. A full GC marks the whole old generation, frees the dead objects and compacts the rest. Many threads share the work, so pauses are as short as the hardware allows, but a full GC on a big old generation is still a long pause. Its log lines tell you everything you need. Tune with goals, not sizes

  
The throughput collector can size the heap for you. Give it a pause goal (-XX:MaxGCPauseMillis, unset by default) and a throughput goal (-XX:GCTimeRatio, default 99). It grows or shrinks the heap and generations after each collection to meet them, starting with the pause goal. GCTimeRatio is confusing, so think in terms of the share of time you're willing to give GC:

  

    
    
19GCTimeRatio = goal / (1 − goal)
5%time GC may use = 1 / (1 + ratio)

    
The default of 99 asks for GC in only 1% of the time, which is aggressive: the JVM will usually grow the heap toward its maximum to try to meet it. A ratio of 19 (95% application time) is a common, more realistic setting.

Too-small pause goals backfire: a smaller heap means more frequent GCs and more total time collecting.

How G1 works: regions and four operations

  
G1 cuts the heap into about 2,048 equal regions. Any free region can become eden, survivor or old; generations no longer need to be contiguous. That lets G1 clean the old generation a few regions at a time, picking the ones with the most garbage first ("garbage first"). Step through one full cycle.

When G1 falls back to a full GC

  
A well-tuned G1 heap only ever does young, mixed and concurrent cycles. A full GC (single-threaded in JDK 8, parallel in JDK 11) means something ran out of room. Learn the four signatures.

  






  
5
The race: marking vs. the filling old generation

  
G1 starts concurrent marking when the heap reaches InitiatingHeapOccupancyPercent (default 45%). Marking must finish, and mixed GCs must free space, before promotions fill the old generation. Tune the knobs and run the race.

Parallel = two pausesMinor and full, both stop-the-world, both multithreaded.

    
Goals before sizesMaxGCPauseMillis and GCTimeRatio; fix sizes later if needed.
