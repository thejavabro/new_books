Build, Beta Test, Submit, and Your Own App

Turn the app into signed builds, put them in front of real testers, submit for review, then repeat the whole Book 3 path on a small app of your own.

1

From your phone to anyone's phone

Until now HabitTrail ran inside Expo Go, convenient for development but not how a finished app reaches people. The last stretch: real signed builds, a few trusted testers, the platforms' own review, then release. You need an Expo account plus developer accounts with Apple and Google. Step along.

May have changed since publicationThe book intentionally teaches the workflow rather than printing fees, review times or forms, because Expo's build tooling (EAS Build) and both stores' policies change. It also notes that Apple and Google both charge developer-account fees. Check Expo's current build and submission docs and each store's developer site before you start.

3

A few real testers before the public

Both platforms let you share a build with a small group first, without a full public review: Google Play's internal testing track and Apple's TestFlight. Invite a handful of people you trust, including at least one who has never seen the app, and ask them to really use it for a few days: add a habit, set a reminder, mark it done. One reader's first fresh-eyed tester was immediately puzzled by an icon whose meaning had become "obvious" to him after weeks of building. Then ask specific questions. Sort these:

Question you can act on?

Address the meaningful feedback, then ask Claude to help submit for each platform's review.

4

Book 3 project: a small app you would use

The capstone: decide what a small, offline-first app should do and build it with everything from this book. Choose something backed by data you really track (books you're reading, a workout you actually do), because invented data is hard to judge. Most of the book's suggestions fit HabitTrail's three-screen shape exactly.

A wish list is not a first versionA reading log with pages, ratings, genres, a wishlist and streaks all at once is too much. More than three or four screens? Find the ones that can wait until the first version works on your phone. A reader who travelled weekly built a two-screen packing list in a weekend and was still opening it months later.
5

Build it the HabitTrail way

Test each increment against your brief as you go, then do Chapter 3.11's pass on the finished app: edge cases, airplane mode, a screen-reader walk-through. Your commit history should read like the projects in Chapters 1.12 and 2.12: a short, sensible story.

6

Decide how far to ship it

A finished app, tested on your own phone and matching your brief, is already an achievement. Going further is optional and depends on whether you have, or want, Apple and Google developer accounts. Ask yourself why you're building it.
