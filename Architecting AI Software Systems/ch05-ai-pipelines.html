Requirements and Architecture for AI Pipelines

Most enterprise AI runs as a pipeline: data in, cleaned, transformed, scored, stored. This chapter specifies both the experimental development pipeline and the hardened production one, and how models move safely between them.

1

Development and production pipelines

Most enterprise AI is a pipeline: progressive stages, often chaining several models. Model building is experimental and iterative, so it gets its own development pipeline; serving gets a hardened production pipeline that mirrors it so models behave the same when they go live. Each stage should be independently verifiable, configurable and scalable, versioned, and traceable to its data lineage. Requirements and architecture recurse: components synthesised from requirements become requirements for the next level down, until implementation detail takes over. Promote a model.

The registry holds versions and their parameters.

A configuration-controlled architecture also organises the people: teams form along its boundaries, and the diagrams onboard newcomers. AI requirements are holistic: data engineering, hardware, and how the rest of the system ingests and acts on AI decisions.

2

Data stores: the source of truth

The data store sits at the foundation with minimal processing. Its requirements cover volume and velocity (growth, peaks, variability), formats and processing (structured or not; batch, streaming or hybrid), timeliness (acceptable delay), non-functionals and governance (redundancy, replication, backups, security, compliance) and operations (status, monitoring, alerts). Choose technology from goals, not preferences. Pick what your workload needs.

Scores are a teaching heuristic built from the strengths the book lists for each store type (its Table 5.1 and production-store notes).

3

Quality checks, transforms and summaries

A decision is only as good as its data. The book lists five minimum quality checks, which should be configuration-controlled and tested: completeness, corruption, time-span regularity, format and range. Modern pipelines add drift and anomaly detection and bias tests. Then transforms normalise units, formats and dimensions (feature stores keep them identical in development and production), and summaries (means, medians, variability, distributions, correlations, box plots) verify consistency and feed monitoring. Run the checks on this sensor feed.

4

Model building, tuning and verification

Model building never really ends: data and business needs keep changing. The loop runs sample → quality check → train → evaluate → acceptable? If not, engineer features and tune hyperparameters and go again. If so, validate on unseen data, have someone not involved verify it, and commit it with full metadata. Play the loop (results are simulated).

Acceptance threshold: AUC ≥ 0.90
5

Canary checks in production

In production the model is a carefully managed black box, never edited in place. To know it is still healthy, the pipeline mixes in canary records: known inputs with known expected outputs. An output check compares results, and alerts fire when deviation passes a threshold. Let the world drift and watch the canaries.

Downstream, results and end-user stores offer flexible queries, machine-to-machine APIs, visualisation, input-output traceability, explanations for non-technical users and BI integration.

6

The pipeline operations store: the nervous system

Every stage sends telemetry (logs, metrics, alerts) to a central pipeline operations store that feeds the observability stack: dashboards, trend plots, KPIs, log summaries and real-time alerts, plus human operation inputs, prioritised alerting, incident response and compliance records. Two availability tactics keep it honest: heartbeat (each component pulses regularly) and ping-echo (ask and wait for a reply). Stop a component's heartbeat.

7

CI/CD the Blue and Gold way

DevOps lets you prototype pipelines without disturbing production. In the book's "Blue and Gold" approach, Blue serves users while Gold, with the new model, is built in parallel, connected to production data, and compared. If Gold wins, it becomes production and Blue stays ready for instant rollback.

MLOps extends this with experiment tracking, model registries, feature stores, automated tests and continuous training on new data.

8

Patterns, tactics and non-functional requirements

Tactics are first-order fixes for a specific problem; patterns combine tactics for larger ones. Pipeline patterns: pipe and filter (stages transform and pass data on, scale independently), distributed store, blackboard (a shared repository components pull from) and service orientation (loosely coupled services behind APIs). Tactics: ping-echo, heartbeat, N-party voting, canary testing, and versioned models and datasets for rollback. Try N-party voting: three models vote on each transaction.
