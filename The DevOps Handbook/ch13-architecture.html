Architect for Low-Risk Releases

Tightly coupled architecture makes every change risky. Loosely coupled services with clear APIs let teams change independently, and the strangler fig pattern lets you get there without a rewrite.

Every successful architecture has to evolve

Almost every DevOps exemplar (LinkedIn, Google, eBay, Amazon, Etsy) has had a near-death experience caused by architecture that fitted yesterday's needs. Jez Humble's principle of evolutionary architecture: the architecture of any successful product or organization will necessarily evolve over its life cycle. Randy Shoup, looking back on eBay, notes that each past choice probably served the goals of its time. eBay's architecture went through five generations:

The downward spiral of tight coupling

In a tightly coupled architecture every commit or release risks a global failure. Small changes need coordination with many teams, deployments get batched, and fear of integrating and deploying grows. Charles Betz calls it the Second Law of Architectural Thermodynamics: project owners aren't held accountable for their contributions to overall system entropy. An architecture for productivity, testability and safety

A loosely coupled architecture with well-defined interfaces lets small teams change things independently. Shoup describes Google's stack: Gmail sits on five or six layers of services, each focused on one function, each run by a small team, each consumed through its API. Hover or tap a layer.

4

Monoliths aren't bad; they just don't scale forever

Shoup's rule of thumb: there is no perfect architecture for every product and scale; what works at 1× rarely works at 10× or 100×. A monolith is often the right choice early on, when you need fast prototypes and pivots. Compare the archetypes.

5

Case study: evolutionary architecture at Amazon (2002)

Amazon started in 1996 as Obidos, a monolith holding all business and display logic. It grew too tangled to scale its pieces independently and, as Werner Vogels put it, couldn't evolve any more. From 2001 to 2005 Amazon moved to a fully distributed, decentralized services platform. Vogels' lessons:

  1. Strict service orientation gives isolation, and a level of ownership and control not seen before.
  2. Prohibiting direct database access by clients lets a service improve scaling and reliability without involving its clients.
  3. Development and operations benefit greatly: the services model lets teams innovate quickly with a strong customer focus.
6

The strangler fig application pattern

Martin Fowler coined the term in 2004, inspired by vines that grow around a host tree and eventually replace it. Instead of a risky rewrite, put existing functionality behind an API, unchanged, and build new functionality in the new architecture behind the same API, routing more and more calls there. Use versioned APIs so callers aren't broken. If a legacy system has no clean API, wrap it in a client library that has one. Step through a migration.

Why not just rewrite it?Rewrites tend to reproduce existing functionality, including business processes that are far more complex than they need to be because of years of workarounds. A strangler lets us rethink each piece as we move it, and shows value early.
7

Case study: Blackboard Learn's Building Blocks (2011)

Blackboard (about $650 million revenue in 2011) had a flagship product, Learn, built on a monolith whose build, integration and testing kept getting more complex. David Ashman plotted repository history back to 2005: lines of code kept rising while commits fell, objective evidence that changes were getting harder. From 2012 the team built Building Blocks: separate modules decoupled from the monolith, accessed through fixed APIs. Given the choice, every developer moved to them. Toggle the view.

What the research says

Architecture was the largest contributor to continuous deliveryThe 2017 State of DevOps Report (DORA and Puppet) found architecture the biggest single contributor. The teams scoring highest could complete their work without other teams and change their systems without dependencies. The 2018 and 2019 reports confirmed loosely coupled architecture's importance for deploying and releasing quickly and safely.
