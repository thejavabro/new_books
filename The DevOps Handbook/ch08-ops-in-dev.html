Integrating Operations into the Daily Work of Development

Ops can stop being a ticket queue at the end of the line. Platforms, embedded engineers and shared rituals bring operational knowledge into every team, every day.

1

Case study: Ops liaisons at Big Fish Games (2014)

Big Fish Games had hundreds of mobile and thousands of PC games, and over $266 million in revenue in 2013. Many Dev teams competed for a centralized Ops group. VP of IT Operations Paul Farrall embedded Ops expertise in Dev teams through two liaison roles.

Business relationship managerLearns product groups' business drivers and roadmaps, advocates for product owners inside Ops, and helps teams navigate and prioritize Ops requests.
Dedicated release engineerKnows the product's Dev and QA issues intimately and helps product management get what it needs from Ops to reach its goals.

Working relationships with Ops and release velocity both improved noticeably. A centralized Ops group got the outcomes usually associated with market-oriented teams.

2

Three ways to bring Ops into Dev

The chapter offers three strategies, which can be combined. They line up with Team Topologies: a platform team serving stream-aligned teams, with liaisons acting like an enabling team. Pick one and send a request through it.

3

Build shared services that developers choose to use

Platforms should be automated and on demand, with no tickets. A good platform might offer a shared version control repository with pre-approved security libraries, a deployment pipeline that runs code quality and security scans, and environments with production monitoring already installed. It carries the collective experience of QA, Ops and Infosec.

Crucially, platforms are not mandated. Platform teams must win over internal customers like any product team, which keeps them honest. Netflix's Dianne Marsh describes her tools team this way: they don't build, bake or deploy anything for other teams; it's fine to depend on their tools but not on them.

Platform teams should also watch for toolchains that are spreading organically, and adopt and support the ones that make sense, rather than designing for reuse up front, a common and expensive failure in enterprise architecture.

Illustrative: when use is voluntary, adoption tracks how good the platform is.

4

Embed Ops engineers in service teams

Embedded Ops engineers take priorities from the product team, not from Ops. Disney's Jason Cox describes embedding systems engineers in product teams across business units, inside Development, Test and even Infosec. For big new projects, embedded Ops can influence what to build and how, help choose the platform, and plan operability from day one. Pairing Dev and Ops engineers is also a very efficient way to cross-train.

5

Or assign a liaison: Etsy's designated Ops

When there aren't enough Ops engineers to embed everywhere, assign each team a liaison. At Etsy, "designated Ops" engineers stay in the central group (which keeps all environments consistent) and are responsible for understanding their teams' work. Check off what a designated Ops engineer needs to know.

Liaisons attend standups, fold the team's needs into the Ops roadmap, and escalate resource conflicts so they're prioritized against wider goals. If Ops becomes the constraint, we have to grow it or change the model.

6

Bring Ops into standups, retrospectives and the board

Improvement work found in retrospectives, like fixing defects, refactoring and automating manual work, needs reserved capacity: improving daily work matters more than daily work itself.

7

Case study: Nationwide Building Society (2020)

Nationwide, with sixteen million members, started with Agile in IT and saw gains, but "we deliver well and reliably, but slowly". In 2020, with Jonathan Smart's team from Deloitte, it began pivoting from functional departments to long-lived, multiskilled mission teams aligned to value streams. A mortgage application had been split among many functional teams and reassembled at the end. Then COVID-19 hit and call centers were swamped. Step through what happened.

Patrick Eltridge, the COO: "I think of Agile as our means and DevOps as our target."
