Agile, Continuous Delivery, and the Three Ways

Lean manufacturing taught us to look at the whole value stream, not single departments. Applied to software, it gives us one key measure, deployment lead time, and three guiding principles: Flow, Feedback, and Continual Learning.

1

From factory floor to technology value stream

Lean's core idea is the value stream: the sequence of activities an organization performs to deliver on a customer request. In a factory you can see it, raw materials moving across the floor. In technology it is invisible, which is why it needs deliberate attention. The technology value stream turns a business hypothesis into a technology-enabled service that delivers value to the customer.

2

Lead time is what the customer feels

The book focuses on deployment lead time: from the moment a change is checked into version control until it is running successfully in production. Two clocks matter. Lead time starts when the request is made and stops when it is fulfilled. Process time only counts while someone is actually working on it. The customer feels lead time, so that is what we improve, and the gap between the two is mostly waiting. Adjust the stages below.

3

Lead times of months versus minutes

The common scenario: monthsLarge, tightly coupled monoliths, scarce integration test environments, long test and production deployment lead times, and heavy reliance on manual tests and approvals. Everything only gets merged at the end, so nothing works, and heroics follow at every stage.
The DevOps ideal: minutesDevelopers check small changes into version control continually, automated and exploratory tests run against them, and the code can be deployed to production at any time. Problems are found in minutes, when they are cheap to fix.
4

%C/A: how often work arrives usable as-is

The third key metric is percent complete and accurate. Ask each downstream team what share of the work they receive is usable without correcting, adding missing information or clarifying. Low %C/A anywhere means rework, and the effect compounds along the stream. Drag the stages and watch the end-to-end figure.

The Three Ways

All the DevOps behaviors in the book derive from three principles. Pick one to see its direction on the value stream and the practices that come from it.

The 2014 to 2019 State of DevOps research, led by Nicole Forsgren and published in Accelerate, found that teams practising these principles do better: elite performers were twice as likely to exceed organizational goals such as profitability, productivity and customer satisfaction.
