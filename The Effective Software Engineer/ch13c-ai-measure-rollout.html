Practical AI: Measure, Roll Out, Carry Forward

Treat AI adoption like any engineering change: measure it, run a pilot, keep what works. The final part of the book adds reusable prompt patterns, a 90-day plan, a checklist for choosing tools and the principles to carry forward.

Measure, don't guess: adoption signals

Impressions ("it feels faster") are a poor basis for spending money or changing a team's habits. The chapter's last part is about measuring AI's effect and rolling it out deliberately. Start with adoption: is the tool used, and is its output trusted the right amount? Move the sliders to see how the signals read.

Other adoption signals the chapter mentions: time to first useful suggestion, and whether people actually use the context features (repo indexing, attached files) that make suggestions better.

2

Speed only counts if quality holds

Adoption tells you people use it. Whether it helps shows in delivery and quality metrics: task time, PR cycle time, throughput, defect density, escaped bugs, test coverage (and mutation score, which shows whether tests catch real faults), and security findings. The tip borrowed from DORA research: watch stability next to speed. Here's a sample pilot readout. Toggle which view a too-eager champion might show.

3

Design a pilot that can actually answer the question

The recipe: record a baseline, pick a pilot group and a comparable control group, run for a few weeks, compare the metrics, survey people, iterate. Tag AI-assisted commits so you can tell them apart. See why the control group matters: in this toy pilot, the whole org also got faster that quarter because a slow CI pipeline was fixed.

6
For leaders: choosing a tool, and whether it pays

  
The leader's checklist: fit with your editors and CI, usage telemetry, privacy controls (training opt-out, data retention, on-premises options), security features, governance and admin controls, regulatory fit, and cost against value. Review the decision monthly at first, then quarterly. The value part is arithmetic; try your own numbers.

  

    
    
    
    

    
Add usage-based model costs if you pay per token, and cap or monitor them. Hours "saved" only count if they go into valuable work, so check against the quality metrics above.

  






  
7
What to carry forward from the whole book

  
The book closes with one thread through every chapter: effective engineers create outcomes, not activity. Tools change; principles stay. AI belongs in the toolbox, and you remain accountable for what ships. Tap the principles you already practise.
