Epilogue: What Stays True

Interfaces will move; the habit of defining, checking and deciding stays. The author's parting advice: back up what matters, consider a second AI for second opinions and resilience, and keep checking the result.

Where the book ends and your work continues

The progress didn't come from a clever trick. It came from four stages, each building on the last, and each ending in something finished.

2

What stays true as ChatGPT changes

Menu names, default modes and controls will look different by the time you read this; the author admits some screenshots were already a step behind at printing. What shouldn't change is the approach to every task. That judgment is the actual skill, and it transfers to whatever ChatGPT becomes and to any other AI tool.

3

Spend your next $20 on a second AI, not a bigger plan

When ChatGPT starts hitting its limits, the obvious move is a pricier plan. The author suggests keeping Plus and adding a second AI subscription instead. Compare.

What you gain
    What you risk

      The author likens ChatGPT and Claude to Coke and Pepsi: both capable, and whichever isn't your primary is usually the strongest secondary. A second model gives a genuine second opinion, the extension of the book's checking habit, rather than the same model reasoning with itself twice. It also protects against an outage, one model's blind spot, or one vendor holding all your work.

      Back up what you'd missThe book's sharpest argument: accounts at every major AI provider get suspended, sometimes by an automated system flagging something ordinary, with little explanation and slow appeals. Export finished documents, useful custom instructions and projects you rely on, the way you'd back up a hard drive.
      Time-sensitivePrices (Plus around US$20, higher tiers US$100 to US$200) and the suspension and appeal statistics the book quotes from one provider's transparency report are dated figures; the author himself says the report updates every six months. A third AI (the book mentions Gemini, Mistral, and Chinese providers such as DeepSeek, Qwen or Kimi) is optional, and work data should only go through providers your employer's policy allows.
      4

      If you want to build, not just direct

      Book 3 pushed right up to the edge of this volume: skills, GPTs, schedules, maybe MCP. The next boundary is writing the code behind an automation yourself (repositories, tests, Git, websites and apps), which the author covers in a separate companion title on Codex. He also lists three online bonus books: choosing other AI services, profession-specific prompts for 20 professions, and running OpenAI's open-weight gpt-oss models locally so nothing leaves your device.

      The last wordEvery chapter asked you to check the result before trusting it. That habit is the lasting skill. Keep the volume nearby: the chapter you skimmed may be the one you need in six months.
