Draft Documents and Extract What Matters

A good document starts as a brief and an outline, not a blank prompt. A good summary is written for a named reader and can be traced back to the passage it came from.

1

From a brief to a finished document

ChatGPT can produce a full first draft in minutes, but the draft is only as good as the plan behind it. The book's seven steps put the cheap checks early. The outline is the key checkpoint: a missing section is far quicker to fix there than in the final draft.

2

Step 1: define reader and purpose

A status update for your manager, a client proposal and a staff policy need different length and tone. Tell ChatGPT the reader, what they need to know, what you want them to do afterwards, and the length, tone and deadline. Or ask it to interview you with no more than five short questions and turn your answers into a brief.

3

Step 2: check the outline before any prose

Ask for no more than six sections, each with a heading and one sentence on what it covers, and tell it not to write the report yet. Then ask three questions: does it cover everything the reader needs, does it include anything they don't, and is the order right? Tick any section this manager doesn't need.

4

Steps 4 to 6: one kind of review at a time

"Make it better" gets vague feedback. Ask about one thing per message: accuracy, structure, tone or presentation. Mixing all four makes the feedback harder to act on. Which review is each question?

Name the review

Then check the document against itself: a figure in one section must match the same figure later, and a term must keep its meaning. Click the number that contradicts the rest.

Watch out: voice driftA revision aimed at clarity can quietly move the tone from formal to casual (or back). Say which tone and wording to keep before asking for changes; if too much changes, point at it and ask for a restore. Read the final version aloud before sending. Last step: ask for one final revision that applies only the changes you approved, so no new unreviewed edits sneak in.
5

Where the draft lives, and how it leaves

The book's drafts appear in an editable writing block in the reply: edit text in place, select a passage and ask for changes, revise the whole draft, undo AI edits, and download as PDF or Word. Moving a document into your word processor can break headings, lists and tables, so check formatting after the move and reread the whole thing.

Likely changed since publicationThe book says OpenAI removed Canvas in mid-2026 and folded its tools into writing blocks; that Excel and PowerPoint had official ChatGPT extensions while Word relied on an admin-deployed "ChatGPT add-in for Office"; and that Office extensions keep their own conversation history separate from the main app and may not support tracked changes, macros or animations. All of this is rollout-dependent; check the current help articles.
6

Summarise for a named reader

Chapter 2.4 turns piles of notes into something you can act on. A summary for yourself differs from one for a manager who missed the meeting. Name the reader and their next action, and set the length. Same notes, different readers:

Good practiceLead with what the reader must act on. Tell ChatGPT "use only information stated in the notes; do not infer an owner, date, decision or risk", and ask it to quote or point to the passage behind each item. Then ask: "Flag any statement you can't trace to a specific passage."
7

Compare several documents fairly

Scattered call notes, an emailed proposal and a draft quote can become one table, if every document is asked the same questions. One row per vendor, one column per question, short phrases, only stated facts, no mixing details between vendors, and "Not stated" where a document is silent. Then ask for the questions to settle before choosing.
