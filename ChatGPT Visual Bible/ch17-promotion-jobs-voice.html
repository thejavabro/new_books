Promotion, a New Job, and Rehearsing Out Loud

Turn your achievement record into outcomes a manager cares about, write a résumé you can defend line by line, and rehearse the hard conversation out loud with Voice mode before the real one.

1

Turn an achievement into a case

Your achievement record (chapter 4.3) is the raw material, so keep adding to it months before you ask for anything. A thin record assembled the week before a review convinces nobody. Each entry names a task, a specific before-and-after, and something beyond "faster". Then add the line managers care about: what it meant for the organisation. Climb the ladder.

Show growth, not just speedPromotion cases often rest on expanded responsibility: new kinds of work you now handle, decisions you make without being asked, colleagues who come to you for help. Then propose your next contribution: a specific, credible plan for the bigger role counts as much as your record.
2

Claim exactly what you did

An overstated claim is easy to challenge and can sink the whole case. Which of these would survive a sceptical follow-up question?

Before the conversation

Ask a second AI to argue against youPaste your case into a different AI from the one that helped write it and ask what a sceptical manager would say is missing or overstated. A model that helped draft the case is worse at spotting its own blind spots. For pay, the book points to official wage data (the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, or your country's equivalent).
3

Prepare for a better job or a career change

Research whether the target role is a good bet: ask ChatGPT for current data on demand and outlook, check at least one source directly (the book suggests the US Occupational Outlook Handbook or a local equivalent), note where sources disagree, and look at adjacent roles that might be stronger. Then follow the pathway.

4

An honest résumé: check every line

ChatGPT can make a résumé clearer, but every claim must describe something you actually did and could explain in detail. The book's order: give it your history and the role's requirements, ask for a draft, check every claim and correct anything overstated, then ask for a second pass on clarity and length only, with no new claims. Below, a draft for someone who supported (but didn't lead) a reporting project. Click every line you'd need to correct.

Show, don't only tell: one or two work samples help (a marketer moving into data-informed marketing adds a short write-up of a campaign she changed midway on the evidence). Ask ChatGPT for likely interview questions and to act as an interviewer with follow-ups, and answer out loud. For networking, ask people in or near the role for a brief conversation, not a job, with two or three genuine questions and a specific thank-you afterwards.

5

Rehearse the high-stakes conversation with Voice

Writing the words is one skill; saying them under a little pressure is another. Find a private room, open Voice mode on your phone, describe the scenario, tell ChatGPT who to play, and set a time limit so it stays practice rather than a chat. Vary the difficulty across rehearsals: one friendly run won't prepare you for a hard conversation.

20:00

When time is up, end Voice, photograph any diagram you sketched, and ask for a report: one strong answer, one vague answer, and one specific change to make before the real conversation. Save it where you'll see it again.

Watch outVoice and camera pick up more than you intend. Use a role and a general situation, not your employer's confidential details or a real client's name.
