Learn, Stay Human, Lead a Pilot

Map the skills your next role needs and test that you really learned them. Keep sharpening the human strengths AI cannot carry for you. Then turn a personal win into a small, honest team pilot with a stop condition.

1

Find out what your next role really needs

Roles change as tools and industries change, so research what your target role looks like now: ask ChatGPT to search current job postings and descriptions, find skills that recur across several sources, note any that look new, and check a few sources yourself (chapter 1.8). The book suggests cross-checking with an official occupational database such as the US O*NET, or your country's equivalent.

Then map current against target, specifically. "Comfortable with data" can't be acted on; "can build a pivot table but not a formula-driven dashboard" can. Drag your current level (blue) for a target role of operations analyst.

2

A plan you will actually finish

Watch outAn overloaded plan is easy to write and hard to finish. Fewer skills at a slower pace beat a long list you abandon.

Reading about a skill isn't using it. Finish a small demonstrable project close to the target job; the book's example is someone moving into operations who analyses her own team's data to find which process step causes most delay, and writes it up. 3

Seven strengths that stay yours

Fluency with ChatGPT is valuable but isn't the whole of your value. The book names seven areas that depend on experience, relationships and context. Tap each for its example. In one story, two analysts build the same model with ChatGPT; one notices its seasonal-demand assumption contradicts three years of real sales data. Her judgment, not the model, protects the business.

4

Notice skills fading from disuse

Skills you stop practising weaken quietly, and leaning on ChatGPT for writing, calculation and structured thinking speeds that up. Signs: hesitating over something you used to do easily; no longer catching obvious errors in ChatGPT's work. The cure is deliberate unassisted practice.

Responsibility doesn't transferChatGPT drafts, analyses and suggests, but it doesn't carry the consequences. Before finalising consequential work, ask: could I explain and defend this in a meeting without reading from the screen? If not, review it again.
5

Propose and lead an AI improvement at work

If your measured improvement could help the whole team, propose it responsibly. Start from the problem, not the tool.

Problem statement or tool pitch?

List benefits (time, errors, turnaround, using chapter 4.3 measurements) and real risks (exposed data, unchecked results, dependence on a tool that changes without notice). A benefits-only proposal looks less credible, not more. Check the organisation's AI policy first; if there isn't one, say so and suggest who should help write it. 6

Design a small pilot with a way to stop

Never propose a full rollout first. Propose a small, time-limited pilot: a defined group, a duration, the same baseline and measures as before, feedback during the run, a fixed review date, and a stop condition decided in advance.

When it ends, report what actually happened, including what didn't go to plan, with measured numbers rather than estimates. If it worked, propose a clear next step; if not, say what you learned. The book points to frameworks such as NIST's AI Risk Management Framework for organisations without their own guidance.
