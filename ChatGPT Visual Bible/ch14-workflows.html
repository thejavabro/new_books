Workflows for Life, Content and Operations

One pattern behind every workflow

Book 3's last chapters apply the same few blocks again and again: a project holds ongoing context, a skill or standard prompt does the repeated work, a schedule or trigger starts it, and a person reviews before anything consequential. Even one well-built skill or scheduled task can make a real difference. Chapter 3.5 starts at home.

Watch outFinancial details and health notes belong only in a project you control. Recheck your memory and privacy settings (chapter 1.11) before adding anything you wouldn't want stored longer than necessary.
2

Research and content: evidence before drafting

Chapter 3.6 builds a recurring management review: define what it covers (key metrics, notable changes, open risks), connect sources, build a skill that gathers and drafts in your format, schedule it before the deadline. Strong content depends on evidence gathered first: two independent sources for any important claim, disagreements flagged rather than silently resolved, and a source list kept beside the draft.

Why the first runs need close review

A workflow that drafts automatically can repeat an error automatically. Simulate ten weekly runs where one source contains a wrong figure from run 3 onward.

When the content should end in a recommendation, ask for two or three real options with evidence, benefits and drawbacks, then the recommendation and its reasoning, then check it against the evidence. And keep one part manual forever: a final human read-through before it reaches its audience.

3

Decisions and operations: prepare for the conversation

Chapter 3.7 applies the pattern to things that change: open decisions, risks and promised actions; briefs before a sales or account conversation; and screening briefs before an interview. Each is a trigger, a gathering step, a summary and a human review before the conversation.

A fair screening brief?

Good practiceUse a fixed set of questions or criteria for every screening brief instead of letting the workflow invent new ones each time. Consistency protects fairness as well as time. For prospects, never include personal or confidential information you have no legitimate reason to hold, and flag anything unconfirmed rather than presenting a guess as fact.
4

Book 3 project: build one workflow end to end

Pick a process that repeats often enough to matter, has a defined start and end, and where a mistake is easy to catch and fix. Not your hardest or most sensitive one. Combine only what it needs: a project for context, skills for stable steps, a plugin or MCP connection only for genuine outside data. Every workflow needs at least one human checkpoint, and must know when to stop and ask.

5

Prove it against a baseline

The sequence: record the baseline before the workflow existed; define success in checkable terms; test a normal case, an unusual case and a case designed to fail; confirm exactly when it stops for a person; record every test; compare with the baseline; keep a manual fallback. Enter your numbers.

Keep this recordChapter 4.3 turns exactly this kind of evidence (baseline, result, honest notes) into an achievement record and, later, a case for promotion.
