Frameworks and Mental Models

Every agent framework is an opinionated bundle of decisions. Learn the five abstractions they all share and the axes they differ on, and a framework becomes an implementation vehicle rather than your architecture.

1

A framework is decisions someone else already made

A framework is a mental model with an API: a bundle of decisions about coordination, state and control flow. Inheriting them isn't automatic. A plain loop that calls the model, validates the output, runs tools and repeats can be the cleanest code for a bounded system, even one with several logical roles. A framework starts earning its weight when coordination gets tricky (who queues, who retries, who resolves disagreement) and when the system keeps changing.

What it decides for you, and what stays yours

The rows where a framework is most opinionated (orchestration, memory) are where leaving it costs the most.

2

Four mental models of collaboration

Frameworks cluster by what they think collaboration is: a dialogue, a workflow, an organization, or just a thin loop. Pick a category to see the same three-step research task drawn in its shape.

3

The five abstractions every framework shares

Beneath different surfaces, every framework expresses the same five things: agents, messages, tools, memory, orchestration. Observability cuts across all of them. These are the five questions to ask of a framework that doesn't exist yet. Click a layer.

Where to look first

The runtime rule: the model is sovereign within its turn, the framework is sovereign over how turns are sequenced, and the application owns everything in between. Diagnose each symptom.

4

The six-step tool cycle

Tool integration looks the same everywhere. What differs is who executes. Step through it.

Unstructured messagesChat-style text with a role. Flexible and readable; details can get lost or misread over long conversations. (AG2)
Typed events and handoffsStructured objects that transfer control and context between agents. In between. (OpenAI Agents SDK)
Structured stateEach node reads and returns updates to a typed state. Precise and deterministic; needs schema design up front. (LangGraph)
5

Three orchestration models, seven principles

Conversation-based, graph-based and handoff-based orchestration each express the Chapter 2 principles differently. None is universally better: conversation suits exploratory work, graphs suit predictable replayable flows, handoffs suit routing between specialists. Pick a principle.

A graph makes the path inspectable. It doesn't make model outputs or external effects deterministic.

6

AG2, the book's teaching vehicle

AG2 grew out of AutoGen, the Microsoft Research project that showed multi-agent conversation could be a programming paradigm. The book uses its classic conversational surface because its transcript is also its execution surface: you can print the history and see what every agent said. That surface is now frozen in maintenance mode, which keeps examples stable; new development happens elsewhere.
