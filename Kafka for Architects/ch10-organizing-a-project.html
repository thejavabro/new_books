Organizing a Kafka Project

Kafka projects fail on organization more often than on technology. Turn workflows into events, manage topics as code, and test at every level.

1

Start with the business, not the broker

Successful event-driven systems start at the strategic level, before schemas or topics. Teams map business workflows, often with event storming: sticky notes on a wall for domain events (things that happened, past tense), the commands that cause them, the actors who issue them and the systems involved. Ideas from domain-driven design (bounded contexts, aggregates) help find boundaries. Tap the notes of this loan-processing storm.

domain eventcommandactorsystem

The result goes into an event catalogue: for every event its purpose, producer, consumers, key data and owner, written by and for business and technical people alike. Early catalogues can be spreadsheets; later, general metadata or catalogue tools work better than hand-kept lists.

3
Nonfunctional requirements

  
Then the numbers that size and tune everything: message format and average and peak message size, latency targets (average and maximum), throughput now and in a year (peaks at month-end or campaigns, bursts from imports), security needs (PII, payment data, encryption in transit or at rest) and data-loss tolerance: critical, important, or best effort. Also: what happens to messages that keep failing, and who reprocesses them?

4
Maintaining the cluster structure

  
Topics, ACLs, schemas and connectors are shared infrastructure, and they drift without governance. First switch off auto.create.topics.enable (it defaults to true): otherwise a typo in a producer's topic name silently creates a new topic with default settings. Then choose how changes are made.

  

    

    

    

  


# topics.yaml in Git: the desired state (illustrative)
topics:
  - name: CUSTOMER_PROFILES
    partitions: 12
    replication: 3
    config: { cleanup.policy: compact, min.insync.replicas: 2 }
    owner: team-profile
acls:
  - principal: User:c360-svc
    allow: [Read]
    topic: CUSTOMER_PROFILES

  
Field notes
Northwind's team picks GitOps and wants their AsyncAPI specifications to be the single source of truth for the cluster. Since the tools expect their own formats, they plan a small generator that turns AsyncAPI documents into the configuration the GitOps tool applies.






  
5
Environments

  
At minimum: Dev for building and prototyping, Test (or QA/staging) for integration and performance testing with production-like settings, and Prod. Each needs topics, schemas, ACLs and connectors kept in step, which is exactly what cluster-as-code gives you: the same definitions applied with environment-specific values.
