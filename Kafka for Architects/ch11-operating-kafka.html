Growing and shrinking the cluster

Upgrades bring bug fixes, features and performance, and Kafka's components (brokers, Connect, Schema Registry, client libraries) depend on each other, so plan versions together. Adding capacity is easy to start: give a new broker a unique ID and start it. But it receives no existing partitions. Only new topics land on it until you move partitions with the reassignment tool (or a balancer such as Cruise Control).

Removing a broker safely: move its partition replicas and leadership elsewhere first (so no partition becomes under-replicated), shut it down cleanly, then unregister it from the cluster metadata. Reassignment copies data over the network, so throttle it to protect production traffic.

2

Monitoring: what to watch

Metrics fall into four technical kinds (errors, saturation, resource utilization, performance) plus business metrics such as orders per minute. They come from three layers: the host (CPU, disk, network, file handles), the JVM (heap, GC pauses) and Kafka itself (via JMX): under-replicated partitions, offline partitions, active controller count, request latency, ISR shrinks, and on the client side consumer lag, produce errors and retries. Inject an incident and diagnose from the dashboard.

Who owns client monitoring?Developers tend to assume operations will watch it; operators treat clients as generic JVM apps and miss Kafka-specific signals such as lag, rebalances and retries. Make ownership explicit: platform teams watch brokers, application teams watch their clients and business metrics, and both share dashboards.

Tuning clinic: throughput versus latency

Defaults favour low latency. Throughput comes from more parallelism (partitions, consumers) and bigger batches (linger, batch size, compression). Latency comes from sending immediately and skipping extra work. Batching raises throughput but can delay individual messages. Turn the knobs.

Balancing data safety and uptime

When too few in-sync replicas remain, Kafka takes the partition offline rather than risk losing data. Correct for most systems; not for all. Some use cases (metrics, telemetry) prefer to keep running even at the cost of some loss.

5

Disaster recovery: RTO and RPO

Kafka has no built-in backup; replication is its fault-tolerance strategy. Data mobility between clusters relies on replication tools (MirrorMaker 2, Confluent Replicator or Cluster Linking, and similar). Two metrics frame the design: RTO, how long you are down, and RPO, how much data you may lose. Set broker.rack so replicas of a partition land in different racks or data centers.
