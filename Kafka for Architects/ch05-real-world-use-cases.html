4

Large messages and the claim-check pattern

The broker rejects messages above message.max.bytes, a little over 1 MiB by default. Large messages hurt everywhere: producer batch memory, consumer memory, replication (followers fetch like clients) and network. Options: raise the limits consistently on broker, topic, producer and consumer; split messages into numbered chunks sent to one partition; or, preferably, store the payload elsewhere and send a reference.

5

RabbitMQ, Pulsar and cloud services

The deepest difference is where logic lives. Kafka follows smart endpoints, dumb pipes: brokers store and forward bytes, and clients do the thinking. RabbitMQ follows smart pipes: producers publish to an exchange that routes messages to queues by rules and content.
