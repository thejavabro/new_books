Case file: the first day of class

What happens in the story
  • Nine months after the mugging, Larry and Laura join Professor Martin Armstrong's famous 15-student seminar, Thriving on Stupidity, held at St. Andrews and in corporate boardrooms.
  • Armstrong singles out a yawning Stewart, then praises Clara: the most important part of deciding is how you frame the decision.
  • Laura seems tense. She worries classmates will treat her differently because the professor is her uncle.
  • Paul Gerber smashes a chair to pieces in the Commons after an argument with Clara.
  • At the Armstrong Foundation awards dinner, the professor is angry: the board, led by Laura's brother Donald, voted to keep money in Chicago instead of funding projects abroad. Phil, a young detective, is there scoping security.

Why thrive on stupidity?

Armstrong's logic: if you truly learn from each mistake, you stop repeating it, and eventually you run out of mistakes to make. Compare two decision makers over 30 decisions. Both start equally error-prone. Only one reviews what went wrong.

Each mistake you study removes a little of the chance you repeat it. The curve is a toy model, but the shape is the point: learning compounds.

3

A frame is a viewfinder

Framing is drawing a boundary around a problem: which goals, options and facts count. Widen or narrow the viewfinder below for a familiar problem, "Sales are falling. What should we do?" Notice what disappears when the frame is tight.

The framing dilemma. From inside, a frame always looks solid. But to decide at all you must simplify, and every simplification distorts. So frame with imagination and humility.
4

Be a contrarian

A boss who asks "this looks good, right?" will hear "yes". Questions that run against your own belief are the only way to learn what people really think. Pick a belief and flip the question.
