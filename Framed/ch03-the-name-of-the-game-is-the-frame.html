The Name of the Game is the Frame

Where you stand depends on where you sit. Each profession, department and person sees a problem through a frame. The class meets a billion-dollar corporate mystery and learns to fight narrow frames with competing ones.

1

Case file: a billion-dollar mystery

What happens in the story
  • The class meets at Global Options (GO), the consulting firm the professor founded and his nephew Donald now runs. GO sells one thing: new ways of looking at old problems, by setting up competing frames.
  • Students are split into three mixed "frame teams" in a theater-in-the-round.
  • The client is PACE. Its statin drug Cicor cut bad cholesterol about 30% and triglycerides about 40% in trials. Three years after launch it was pulled after unexpected patient deaths: about a billion dollars of sales lost this year, shares down 30%, and several billion more in lawsuits to come.
  • Larry tells Chris the folk tale of the blind men and the elephant.

3

Same crisis, different lenses

Inside PACE, each department framed Cicor's failure through its own job. Pick a department and see which causes light up. Then pick the frame team.

4

Frame control

Explore widely, but don't lose the plot. Step through a team chasing a goal of +10% market share.

5

Where you stand depends on where you sit

Donald's point: training gives you more than knowledge, it gives you a mental structure. Here is how four disciplines might first see the same question: "Why did patients on Cicor die?"

That's why frame teams mix economists, psychologists, statisticians and business students: each one's blind spot is someone else's home turf.
