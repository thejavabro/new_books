Case file: you're in the slammer

What happens in the story
  • Clara runs the Prisoner's Dilemma in class. 80% confess. Phil: cops play this game with suspects all the time.
  • After class, Paul Gerber offers Larry flat, emotionless condolences.
  • The Tribune reports Donald was nearly run down by a hit-and-run driver. Phil works out the base rate: about 1 in 6,000 Chicagoans a year. Another coincidence?
  • Phil finds Paul was a teenage "boy wonder" of a pump-and-dump scheme on a penny stock: he walked away with $14,000 after a settlement with the SEC.
  • Phil hints to Angela that cooperating now beats facing "the big cops" later, and dangles help with a green card. She refuses, for now.

What changes with repeated play

The book's dilemma is one-shot. Real relationships repeat, and that is where trust can pay. Play 10 rounds against a partner who copies your last move ("tit for tat"). Fewer years is better.

Recap on one page

Interdependent choicesYour best move depends on what others do: the domain of game theory.

Two ways outTrust and cooperation, or a binding, enforceable agreement with monitoring.
