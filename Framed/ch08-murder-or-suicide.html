1

Case file: suicide or murder?

What happens in the story
  • Driving back from the phone company, Larry and Phil try to reconstruct the past weekend and decide where to look next. Recalling events accurately is harder than it sounds.
  • They run a formal decision exercise on the core question: did Laura take her own life, or was she murdered? Later Phil admits it "cleared our thinking".
  • The chapter introduces the subjective traps that bend our judgment when emotions run high, starting with Larry's own guilt-soaked hindsight.

3

Hindsight bias: the past gets rewritten

Before Laura died, her signals looked ambiguous. After, Larry remembers them as obvious warnings. Toggle the two views of the same week.

The fix: write down what you knew and expected before the outcome. Larry, telling the story years later, admits he kept a diary for exactly this reason.
4

How we escape a painful decision

Decisions under stress are a hot cognitive process. Tap each escape route to see it in the story and in everyday life.

Satisficing: good enough vs. best

You are viewing 20 apartments in random order, scored 0 to 100. Set your "good enough" threshold. A satisficer takes the first one above it. Run it many times to see the trade-off between effort and quality.

Satisficing is not always bad. When search is costly it's sensible. The trap is a bar set too low out of fatigue, as Larry's company found when it accepted any project beating the interest rate by 3 or 4 points.
