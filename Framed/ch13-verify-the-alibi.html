Case file: the report

What happens in the story
  • The medical examiner, Dr. Murdoch, estimates death between 10:00 PM Sunday and 2:00 AM Monday, and grumbles that his request for a time-of-death software program is stuck in the budget.
  • The autopsy confirms murder: Laura was suffocated, and the poison was injected after death. It stayed localized in her arm because her heart had stopped.
  • Alibis: Paul has none. Donald was at a Rush Street party from 9:00 to 2:30, 20 minutes away. Professor Armstrong caught a 9:30 PM flight to Washington, which Phil, ever the cop, checked too.
  • Larry digs up Donald's past: a failed partnership with James Knapp, a fight over sunk costs, and two months later Knapp was hurt in a hit-and-run.
2

Estimate the time of death

The examiner's main clue is body temperature. A body starts at 37 °C and loses roughly 1 °C per hour for the first 12 hours, slowing as it nears room temperature. Set the readings and see the window. (The book's rule of thumb is linear; we flatten the curve as it nears room temperature.)

Lividity (blood settling to the lowest point, about 75% developed here; complete in 8 to 12 hours) agreed with the window. Rigor was set aside as too variable. Keep this page in mind: the clock turns out to have been tampered with in chapter 16.

Man + Machine

Research says statistical models beat unaided experts because they don't have off days. But models miss things that don't fit their inputs. Blend them. Run 200 cases, some with an unusual factor only a human would spot.

Toy simulation: the model is consistent but blind to rare special factors; the expert notices them but is noisier day to day. Phil's doctor did the same: a computed risk score of 1.2 on a 0–9 scale, then questions about lifestyle, then a clinical judgment.
