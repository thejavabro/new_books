Gravitate to Your Own Risk Taste

Each of us has a comfort level of risk. Push us away from it, with seat belts, safety caps or new medicines, and we tend to drift back by taking risk somewhere else.

Case file: a phone log

What happens in the story
  • In the library, Raj points out that the FDA quickly approved a doubled dose of Cicor (from 0.5 to 1 milligram): more benefit, multiplied risk. Fifty-two people have died while taking it.
  • John Scott explains the compensation hypothesis, and Clara connects it to patients who feel protected by Cicor and eat worse.
  • Paul Gerber, hollow-eyed, ignores the group and types furiously.

Recap on one page

Risk tasteHow much risk feels comfortable depends on personality, age, income and circumstance.
Compensation hypothesisImposed safety can be offset by riskier behavior elsewhere.
Often unconsciousNobody decides to drive faster because of a seat belt. It just happens.
Know your exposureAsk: is this risk acceptable in the worst case? Am I using real odds?
Risk vs. crimeLegitimate risk-taking is not the same as fraud with a calculated chance of getting caught.
More isn't always betterHigher doses bring more benefit and multiply the risk of side effects.
