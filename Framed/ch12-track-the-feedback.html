Track the Feedback

Every decision is an opportunity twice: once when you choose, and again when you learn from what happened. But feedback has holes, and the act of watching can change what you see.

Case file: good news in pieces

What happens in the story
  • Unable to sleep, Larry reads the professor's notes on feedback and applies them to the suspects and to PACE (about 21% of new drugs later have their doses reduced).
  • Phil calls with news. Larry asks him to split up the good news and combine the bad. The good: Angela was seen in the dorms at about 7:00 Sunday evening and seems to be hiding something; Paul has an expunged high-school assault on his record; and Donald, as sole surviving heir, eventually stands to inherit everything.
  • The bad: the Peeping Tom email came from a public computer at the Harold Washington Library, under a fake account.
  • Larry starts searching the web for Donald: 9,813 hits, refined to 841. A long night.
2

Deciding and learning are one loop

Feedback isn't something you collect after the decision is over. Watch the loop run; the inner pulse is feedback gathered while deciding (pilots, tests, questions).

For a new product: pilot it with likely customers before launch (feedback while deciding), survey buyers after launch (feedback after), and give them a reason to answer.

5

Split the gains, combine the losses

Gains and losses feel asymmetric: each extra dollar of gain feels a little smaller, and losses sting more than equal gains please. Deliver $120 of good news, or $120 of bad news, in pieces.

7

Recap on one page

Integrated processGather feedback while deciding and after. They are not separate phases.
Repeated decisionsBuild a routine for regular feedback, such as pilots and periodic surveys.
Overlooked feedbackTrack the paths not taken: rejected applicants, shelved designs, other drugs.
Treatment effectsBonuses, attention or suspicion change behavior and blur what you measure.
Split gainsGood news lands better in pieces; each extra unit of gain feels smaller.
Combine lossesBad news hurts less delivered at once than drip by drip.
